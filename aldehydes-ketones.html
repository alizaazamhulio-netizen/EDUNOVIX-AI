🔬 Molecular Architecture

ALDEHYDE (R—CHO): Carbonyl carbon bonded to at least ONE Hydrogen (H).

SECTION 01 FUNDAMENTAL CONCEPT

The Carbonyl Group (—C=O)

Understanding bond polarity, hybridization, and electrophilic nature.

Electrophilic Carbon (Cδ+)
Nucleophilic Oxygen (Oδ-)
Terminal Hydrogen (H)
Alkyl / Aryl Group (R)

⚡ High Bond Polarity (Cδ+=Oδ-)

Oxygen has an electronegativity of 3.44, whereas Carbon is 2.55. This substantial difference pulls the π-electron cloud strongly toward oxygen, rendering the carbonyl carbon markedly electron-deficient (electrophilic).

📐 Geometry & Hybridization

The carbonyl carbon is sp² hybridized with planar trigonal geometry and bond angles of approximately 120°. It forms 3 σ-bonds and 1 π-bond with oxygen.

⭐ MDCAT Golden Rule: Nucleophiles ALWAYS attack the carbonyl Carbon (Cδ+), while electrophiles (like H⁺) attack the carbonyl Oxygen (Oδ-).

SECTION 03 IUPAC & COMMON NAMES

Nomenclature Lab

Systematic IUPAC rules and common names tested frequently in MDCAT.

IUPAC Suffix Principles

1
Aldehydes: Suffix "-al"

The —CHO carbon is automatically Carbon-1. No position number is needed for the aldehyde group in mono-aldehydes (e.g., Ethanal, Butanal).

2
Ketones: Suffix "-one"

The continuous carbon chain is numbered from the end closer to the carbonyl carbon. Position must be indicated for chains of 5+ carbons (e.g., Pentan-2-one vs Pentan-3-one).

3
Common Names to Memorize

HCHO = Formaldehyde (Methanal); CH₃CHO = Acetaldehyde (Ethanal); CH₃COCH₃ = Acetone (Propanone); CH₃COCH₂CH₃ = MEK (Methyl Ethyl Ketone).

SECTION 05 OXIDATION REACTION

Aldehyde Oxidation Lab

Rapid oxidation of aldehydes into carboxylic acids.

🔥 Reaction Transformation

R—CHO + [O] ⟶ R—COOH

Example: Ethanal (CH₃CHO) reacts with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄ to form Ethanoic Acid (CH₃COOH).

Color Change Indicator: Orange Dichromate (Cr₂O₇²⁻) is reduced to Green Chromium ions (Cr³⁺).

🧪 Why Ketones Resist Mild Oxidation

Aldehydes have a C—H bond directly attached to the carbonyl carbon that cleaves easily. In contrast, ketones (R—CO—R') have two strong C—C bonds attached to the carbonyl carbon, which cannot be cleaved without vigorous conditions and carbon chain fragmentation (Popoff's Rule).

MDCAT Takeaway: Aldehydes are strong reducing agents; ketones are NOT.
SECTION 06 DIAGNOSTIC TEST

Tollens' Test Lab (Silver Mirror Test)

Distinguishing Aldehydes from Ketones using Ammoniacal Silver Nitrate.

Tollens' Reaction Chamber

Awaiting Reaction Select an organic compound to add to the freshly prepared Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.

🪞 Chemical Principle & Equation

Tollens' reagent consists of ammoniacal silver nitrate solution containing the complex cation [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.

RCHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ ⟶ RCOO⁻ + 2Ag↓ (Mirror) + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O
  • Positive Result: Shining silver deposit on glass tube inner surface.
  • Aliphatic & Aromatic Aldehydes: BOTH give a positive Tollens' test (Benzaldehyde gives silver mirror!).
  • Ketones: Give a negative test (no silver mirror).
SECTION 07 DIAGNOSTIC TEST

Fehling's Test Lab

Detection of Aliphatic Aldehydes with Alkaline Copper(II) Tartrate.

Fehling's Reaction Chamber

Awaiting Reaction Equal volumes of Fehling's A (aqueous CuSO₄) and Fehling's B (alkaline sodium potassium tartrate).

🔴 Chemical Principle & Equation

Fehling's solution contains Cu²⁺ ions stabilized as a deep blue tartrate complex in alkaline medium.

RCHO + 2Cu²⁺ + 5OH⁻ ⟶ RCOO⁻ + Cu₂O↓ (Brick-Red) + 3H₂O
⭐ Critical MDCAT Distinction: Aliphatic aldehydes (like Ethanal) readily reduce Fehling's solution, but Aromatic aldehydes (like Benzaldehyde) generally do NOT reduce Fehling's solution under standard test conditions!

SECTION 09 REDUCTION LAB

Reduction of Carbonyl Compounds

Hydride addition converting Aldehydes and Ketones into Alcohols.

🌿 Aldehyde Reduction ⟶ Primary (1°) Alcohol

R—CHO + 2[H] ⟶ R—CH₂—OH

Example: Ethanal (CH₃CHO) + NaBH₄ ⟶ Ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH).

🔷 Ketone Reduction ⟶ Secondary (2°) Alcohol

R—CO—R' + 2[H] ⟶ R—CH(OH)—R'

Example: Propanone (CH₃COCH₃) + NaBH₄ ⟶ Propan-2-ol [CH₃CH(OH)CH₃].

SECTION 10 REACTION MECHANISM

Nucleophilic Addition Mechanism (AN)

Step-by-step electronic movement and tetrahedral intermediate formation.

Mechanism Steps:
⚡ Reactivity Series toward Nucleophiles: HCHO (Methanal) > CH₃CHO (Ethanal) > CH₃COCH₃ (Propanone).
Why? Formaldehyde has less steric crowding and no electron-donating alkyl groups diminishing Cδ+.
SECTION 11 SPECIFIC ADDITION

Addition of HCN & Cyanohydrins

Nucleophilic addition yielding α-hydroxy nitriles and synthetic carboxylic acids.

🧪 Cyanohydrin Synthesis

>C=O + HCN ⟶ >C(OH)(CN)

Base Catalyst Role: HCN is a weak acid. Adding a trace of base (OH⁻) removes H⁺ to generate the active nucleophile :CN⁻.

💡 Hydrolysis to α-Hydroxy Carboxylic Acids

When a cyanohydrin is heated with aqueous acid (H⁺/H₂O), the nitrile group (—CN) hydrolyzes into a carboxylic acid (—COOH):

CH₃CH(OH)CN + 2H₂O + H⁺ ⟶ CH₃CH(OH)COOH (Lactic Acid) + NH₄⁺
SECTION 12 PHYSICAL TRENDS

Physical Properties Matrix

Boiling points, intermolecular forces, and solubility trends.

Boiling Point Order
Alcohols > Aldehydes & Ketones > Ethers > Alkanes (for similar molar mass). Carbonyls lack O—H donors for self H-bonding.
Water Solubility
Small carbonyls (Methanal, Ethanal, Acetone) are completely soluble due to H-bonding between water and carbonyl oxygen.
Chain Length Effect
As the hydrophobic alkyl (R) chain lengthens (>5 Carbons), water solubility drops sharply.
Dipole-Dipole Forces
Polar C=O groups lead to strong permanent dipole-dipole attractions, raising B.P. above nonpolar hydrocarbons.
SECTION 13 STRUCTURAL REASONING

Why Aldehydes Oxidize More Easily Than Ketones

Bond dissociation energy and structural comparison.

Aldehyde: R—C(=O)—H

Contains a C—H bond attached directly to the carbonyl carbon. This hydrogen is readily abstracted during oxidation, converting the aldehyde into a carboxylic acid without breaking carbon-carbon bonds.

Ketone: R—C(=O)—R'

Has NO hydrogen attached to the carbonyl carbon. Oxidation requires breaking strong C—C single bonds with energetic disruption of the carbon skeleton.

SECTION 15 REVISION NOTES

MDCAT High-Yield Revision Notes

Condensed syllabus notes formatted for rapid retention.

1. Introduction & Functional Group

Aldehydes (R—CHO) and ketones (R—CO—R') are carbonyl compounds containing the highly polar >C=O group. Formaldehyde (HCHO) is the simplest aldehyde (two H atoms attached), and Acetone (CH₃COCH₃) is the simplest ketone.

2. Physical Properties Summary

Carbonyl compounds have dipole-dipole attractions. Their boiling points are higher than non-polar alkanes of comparable molar mass, but lower than alcohols because they cannot act as hydrogen bond donors to themselves. Lower aldehydes and ketones are water-soluble due to hydrogen bonding with water molecules.

3. Diagnostic Tests at a Glance

Tollens' Test: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ reduced to Silver Mirror by aldehydes.
Fehling's Test: Blue Cu²⁺ complex reduced to brick-red Cu₂O by aliphatic aldehydes.
2,4-DNP (Brady's Test): Yellow/orange hydrazone precipitate with both aldehydes and ketones.

SECTION 16 LAST 5 MINUTES

MDCAT Memory Wall (Last 5 Minutes Quick Recap)

The 10 essential golden facts you MUST review before entering the exam room.

1. Carbonyl Group
>C=O bond with sp² hybridization, 120° bond angle, polar Cδ+=Oδ-.
2. Aldehyde = RCHO
Carbonyl carbon attached to at least ONE Hydrogen atom.
3. Ketone = RCOR'
Carbonyl carbon attached to TWO carbon groups (no direct H).
4. 1° Alcohol ⟶ Aldehyde
Controlled oxidation of primary alcohol yields aldehyde.
5. 2° Alcohol ⟶ Ketone
Oxidation of secondary alcohol yields ketone.
6. Aldehyde ⟶ Acid
Aldehydes oxidize easily to carboxylic acids (+[O]).
7. Aldehyde Reduction
Reduction with NaBH₄/LiAlH₄ produces Primary (1°) Alcohol.
8. Ketone Reduction
Reduction with NaBH₄/LiAlH₄ produces Secondary (2°) Alcohol.
9. Tollens' ⟶ Silver Mirror
Aldehydes reduce Tollens' reagent to metallic silver (Ag⁰).
10. 2,4-DNP ⟶ Carbonyls
Gives yellow/orange precipitate for BOTH Aldehydes and Ketones.
