Quick Revision Notes

Condensed rules and memory aids designed for fast pre-test recall.

1. Proximity Rule with 'Either...or / Neither...nor'
When subjects are connected by 'either...or' or 'neither...nor', the verb agrees with the subject closest to it.
Neither the teacher nor the students were aware of the delay.
2. 'Along with', 'As well as', 'Together with'
Parenthetical prepositional phrases do not change the number of the subject. The verb agrees with the first subject.
The captain, along with his crew members, was rescued.
1. Dangling Modifiers
A participial phrase must clearly refer to the subject immediately following it.
Incorrect: Walking in the park, a snake bit Ali.
Correct: While Ali was walking in the park, a snake bit him.
2. Redundancy & Double Negatives
Never use 'hardly', 'scarcely', or 'barely' with negative words like 'not' or 'without'.
Incorrect: She could not hardly hear the speaker.
Correct: She could hardly hear the speaker.
1. High-Yield Roots
Bene- (Good: Benevolent, Beneficial) | Mal- (Bad: Malevolent, Malign) | Loqu/Loc- (Speak: Loquacious, Eloquent) | Luc/Lum- (Light: Lucid, Luminous).
2. Context Clue Elimination
Identify contrast transition signals (however, although, whereas, despite) to guess antonyms in sentence completion.
1. Read the Questions First
Scan question stems to identify keywords before diving into the passage; this focuses your reading on target data.
2. Beware of Extreme Language
Options containing absolute terms like 'always', 'never', 'all', or 'impossible' are typically incorrect in inference questions.
Affect vs. Effect
Affect is usually a verb (to influence: "The cold affects my health"). Effect is usually a noun (a result: "The effect of the drug was immediate").
Elicit vs. Illicit
Elicit (verb: to draw forth response). Illicit (adjective: illegal or forbidden).
Time Allocation & Process of Elimination
Spend no more than 35-45 seconds per English MCQ. Immediately cross out 2 options that violate basic agreement or tense rules.
Score Maximizers

MDCAT English Strategy

Proven techniques used by top medical college qualifiers.

📖

Read Full Sentence First

Always read the entire sentence thoroughly to grasp the intended tense and context before choosing any option.

🔍

Check Subject-Verb Agreement

Locate the true grammatical subject and ignore intervening prepositional phrases to prevent plural/singular confusion.

⏳

Look for Tense Consistency

Ensure verb tenses remain coherent across compound sentences, conditional clauses, and reported speech conversions.

💡

Learn Vocabulary Through Context

Use surrounding tone and semantic clues rather than relying on isolated dictionary memorization for difficult synonyms.

🎯

Eliminate Incorrect Options First

Apply the Process of Elimination (POE) to discard grammatically flawed choices to improve guessing accuracy.

⚡

Practice Grammar Daily

Consistent daily 10-MCQ practice trains your brain to spot errors in seconds under real MDCAT time pressure.
