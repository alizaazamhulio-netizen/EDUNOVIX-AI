Unit 01

1. What is Direct Speech?

When we reproduce someone's exact, verbatim words without alterations, we use Direct Speech. The exact words uttered by the speaker are framed strictly inside quotation marks (inverted commas).

Direct Speech Example
Ali said, "I am preparing for MDCAT."
• Exact speaker utterance inside quotes
Structure Breakdown
Reporting Verb: said
Reported Speech: "I am preparing for MDCAT."
Punctuation: Comma before quote + quotation marks.
Anatomy of Direct Speech for MDCAT:
  • Reporting Speech (Introductory Clause): Contains the subject and the reporting verb (e.g., Dr. Bilal said to the patient).
  • Reported Speech (Quoted Clause): The actual message spoken, placed within double quotation marks ("Take this medicine daily.").
Unit 02

2. What is Indirect (Reported) Speech?

When we convey the essence or content of someone's statement from our own perspective without quoting their verbatim words, we use Indirect Speech. The quotation marks disappear, clauses are linked with appropriate subordinating conjunctions, and pronouns/tenses are shifted systematically.

Direct Speech
Ali said, "I" "am preparing" for MDCAT.
Indirect Speech
Ali said that he was preparing for MDCAT.
Four Crucial Adjustments Made:
1
Quotation Marks Removed: Inverted commas and the introductory comma are deleted.
2
Conjunction Added: The assertive connector that connects the two clauses.
3
Pronoun Shift: 1st person I becomes 3rd person he (referring to Ali).
4
Tense Backshift: Present Continuous am preparing shifts into Past Continuous was preparing.
Unit 03

3. Main Changes in Direct → Indirect

Whenever you encounter an MDCAT question on speech conversion, run this 7-point audit checklist to eliminate incorrect distractors rapidly:

⭐ 1. Pronouns

Subject/object perspective alignment using the S-O-N formula.

⭐ 2. Tense Backshift

Moving one step back into the past when reporting verb is in past tense.

⭐ 3. Time Expressions

Shifting proximal time markers (now ➔ then, today ➔ that day).

⭐ 4. Place Expressions

Proximal place pointers become distal (here ➔ there).

⭐ 5. Demonstratives

Shifting demonstrative adjectives and pronouns (this ➔ that, these ➔ those).

⭐ 6. Reporting Verb

Choosing accurate functional verb (asked, ordered, advised, warned).

⭐ 7. Sentence Type Order

Converting questions and commands into assertive statement word order.

Unit 04

4. Pronoun Changes & The S-O-N Formula

Pronouns in reported speech must be systematically re-anchored to the speaker and listener of the introductory clause. Remember the famous S-O-N formula:

The Golden S-O-N Pronoun Rule

  • 1 (First Person: I, we, me, us, my, our): Changes according to the SUBJECT (S) of the reporting verb.
  • 2 (Second Person: you, your): Changes according to the OBJECT (O) of the reporting verb.
  • 3 (Third Person: he, she, it, they, his, her, their): Undergoes NO CHANGE (N).
Direct Pronoun Possible Indirect Pronoun Medical Example Context
I he / she Ahmed said, "I am exhausted." ➔ Ahmed said that he was exhausted.
we they The lab techs said, "We titrated the acid." ➔ They said that they had titrated the acid.
my his / her Ayesha said, "My stethoscope is broken." ➔ Ayesha said that her stethoscope was broken.
our their Doctors said, "Our duty starts now." ➔ Doctors said that their duty started then.
you I / he / she / they The tutor said to me, "You solved it." ➔ The tutor told me that I had solved it.
me / us him / her / them The patient said, "Help us." ➔ The patient requested to help them.
Unit 05

5. Tense Changes Hierarchy (Backshift)

When the reporting verb is in the Past Tense (said, told, asked), the tense inside reported speech systematically moves one step back into the past:

Present Simple ➔ Past Simple

She said, "I study biology." ➔ She said that she studied biology.

V1 ➔ V2
Present Continuous ➔ Past Continuous

He said, "I am studying." ➔ He said that he was studying.

is/am/are ➔ was/were
Present Perfect ➔ Past Perfect

Sara said, "I have completed my work." ➔ Sara said that she had completed her work.

has/have ➔ had + V3
Past Simple ➔ Past Perfect

He said, "I visited Lahore." ➔ He said that he had visited Lahore.

V2 ➔ had + V3

Unit 07

7. When Tense Does NOT Change (Critical Exceptions)

MDCAT examiners frequently design trap questions based on these 3 mandatory non-backshift scenarios:

1. Universal Scientific Truths

Direct: The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
Indirect: The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
(❌ revolved is wrong because it remains permanently true).

2. General Biological & Physical Facts

Direct: The scientist said, "Water boils at 100°C."
Indirect: The scientist said that water boils at 100°C.
(Retains present simple tense).

3. Present / Future Reporting Verb

Direct: Dr. Asad says, "I am busy in surgery."
Indirect: Dr. Asad says that he is busy in surgery.
(Reporting verb "says" is in present ➔ NO tense change).

Unit 09

9. Assertive Statements

For standard declarative statements, the conjunction that is used to introduce the subordinate clause.

Formula: Subject + said / told + (Object) + that + Subject (shifted) + Verb (shifted) + predicate.

Direct: She said, "I am happy with my MDCAT biology score."
Indirect: She said that she was happy with her MDCAT biology score.
Unit 10

10. Say vs. Tell (High-Yield Exam Trap)

This is one of the most common distractors placed in MDCAT choices:

SAY (Intransitive Pattern)

Pattern: say + (that) OR say + to + someone

✅ He said that he was tired.
✅ He said to me that he was tired.

TELL (Transitive Pattern - Mandatory Object)

Pattern: tell + someone + (that)

❌ He told that he was tired. (WRONG - No listener)
❌ He told to me that he was tired. (WRONG - 'to' added)
✅ He told me that he was tired. (CORRECT)

Unit 11

11. Interrogative Sentences (Questions)

In indirect speech, questions lose their question marks and convert strictly to statement word order (Subject + Verb):

Type A: Yes / No Questions (Use if / whether)

Direct: He said to me, "Are you ready?"
Indirect: He asked if I was ready.
(⚠️ Trap: Never say "asked that if I was ready").

Type B: WH Questions (Keep the Question Word)

Direct: She said, "Where do you live?"
Indirect: She asked where I lived.
(⚠️ Trap: ❌ "asked where did I live" ➔ ✅ "asked where I lived").

Unit 12

12. Commands & Orders (Imperatives)

For direct authoritative commands, verbs such as ordered, commanded, or told are used with the to + V1 (infinitive) construction:

Direct: The teacher said to the student, "Open the book."
Indirect: The teacher told the student to open the book.
(❌ The teacher said that open the book is WRONG).

Negative Commands: Use 'not to + V1'

Direct: The doctor said to me, "Do not eat junk food."
Indirect: The doctor advised me not to eat junk food.
Unit 13

13. Requests, Advice & Suggestions

Requests (Please / Kindly)

Use: requested / begged / pleaded + Object + to + V1

Direct: He said to me, "Please help me."
Indirect: He requested me to help him.
(Notice: 'Please' is dropped).

Advice & Counsel

Use: advised / suggested / recommended + Object + to + V1

Direct: The doctor said, "Take rest."
Indirect: The doctor advised the patient to take rest.

Unit 14

14. Exclamatory Sentences

Exclamatory sentences express emotions (joy, sorrow, surprise, admiration). They are converted to assertive clauses introduced by exclaimed with [emotion] that:

Direct: She said, "What a beautiful place!"
Indirect: She exclaimed with admiration that it was a very beautiful place.

⚠ MDCAT Trap Zone

Top 10 examiner traps designed to confuse students in entry tests.

Trap 1 — Question Word Order Inversion
❌ He asked where did I live.
✅ He asked where I lived.
Examiner Trick: Indirect questions must use statement word order (Subject + Verb), never question order.
Trap 2 — "Tell" Without Object or with "To"
❌ He told that he was busy. / He told to me...
✅ He told me that he was busy.
Examiner Trick: "Tell" requires a direct personal object without "to".
Trap 3 — Backshifting Universal Truths
❌ The teacher said that the Earth revolved around the Sun.
✅ The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun.
Examiner Trick: Universal scientific facts retain Present Simple tense.
Trap 4 — Commands with "That" instead of "To + V1"
❌ The teacher said that sit down.
✅ The teacher told the students to sit down.
Examiner Trick: Commands connect via full infinitive (to + V1), not "that".
Trap 5 — Negative Commands Double Negative with "Forbade"
❌ He forbade me not to enter the lab.
✅ He forbade me to enter the lab. (OR: warned me not to enter)
Examiner Trick: "Forbade" is already negative; adding "not" creates an incorrect double negative.
Trap 6 — Double Conjunctions ("That If" / "That Where")
❌ She asked that if I was ready.
✅ She asked if I was ready.
Examiner Trick: Never combine "that" with "if", "whether", or WH-words.
Trap 7 — Present/Future Reporting Verb Backshift
❌ Ali says that he was studying. (Direct: Ali says, "I am studying.")
✅ Ali says that he is studying.
Examiner Trick: If reporting verb is in present ("says") or future ("will say"), do NOT shift tense.
Trap 8 — Split Infinitive in Negative Commands
❌ Doctor advised me to not smoke.
✅ Doctor advised me not to smoke.
Examiner Trick: Use "not to + V1", never "to not + V1".
Trap 9 — Retaining "Please" with "Requested"
❌ He requested me to please help him.
✅ He requested me to help him.
Examiner Trick: Words like "please" and "kindly" must be deleted in indirect speech.
Trap 10 — "Must" Nuance: Universal Moral Duty vs Immediate Urgent Task
❌ Teacher said we had to respect parents.
✅ Teacher said we must respect parents. (Immediate urgent task: "had to").
Examiner Trick: Moral duty / universal obligation retains "must"; specific temporal necessity becomes "had to".

Exam Gold Tips for MDCAT

Tip 1: Word Order Check

Remember: Indirect questions ALWAYS use statement word order (Subject + Verb), never auxiliary inversion.

Tip 2: Universal Truths

Remember: Scientific laws, biological constants, and permanent natural phenomena generally retain their present tense.

Tip 3: Tell Syntax

Remember: "Tell" normally requires a direct personal object without the preposition "to" (told me, told us).

Tip 4: Imperative Mood

Remember: Commands and requests commonly link via "to + V1" (positive) or "not to + V1" (negative).
