Introduction to Sequences & Series

In mathematics, understanding the distinction between ordered lists of numbers and the accumulation of numbers is fundamental to solving university admission test questions.

📜 What is a Sequence?

A sequence is an ordered list of numbers or elements generated by a specific mathematical rule. Each individual member is called a term.

Example: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, ...

Terms are separated by commas.

➕ What is a Series?

A series is the indicated summation of the terms of a sequence.

Example: 2 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 10 + ...

Terms are connected by addition or subtraction signs.

🔑 Critical Distinction for Admission Tests
Sequence = List of terms  |  Series = Sum of terms
Never confuse the nth term formula aₙ with the sum formula Sₙ!
Topic 02 / 23

Terms, Position & Standard Notation

To work systematically with sequences, mathematicians use standardized subscript notation:

  • a₁ (or a) represents the First Term.
  • a₂ represents the Second Term.
  • a₃ represents the Third Term.
  • aₙ represents the nth Term (also called the general term).
  • n represents the Position or Index (n ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, ...}, strictly a positive integer).

Finite vs. Infinite Sequences

• Finite Sequence: Has a definite end term. Example: 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 (contains exactly 5 terms).
• Infinite Sequence: Continues indefinitely, denoted by ellipsis. Example: 3, 6, 9, 12, ...

Worked Example: Identifying Sequence Components Basic

Consider the sequence: 5, 12, 19, 26, 33, ...

Step 1First Term: a₁ = 5
Step 2Second Term: a₂ = 12
Step 3Third Term: a₃ = 19
Step 4Position of 33: n = 5 (since 33 is the 5th number in the sequence).

Systematic Pattern Recognition

FAST and scholarship aptitude exams test your ability to spot numerical rules in seconds. Use this structured decision tree rather than guessing randomly:

1. Constant Addition (+d)
3, 7, 11, 15, 19 (+4 each step)
2. Constant Subtraction (-d)
20, 16, 12, 8, 4 (-4 each step)
3. Constant Multiplication (×r)
2, 6, 18, 54, 162 (×3 each step)
4. Constant Division (÷k)
81, 27, 9, 3, 1 (÷3 each step)
5. Square Numbers (n²)
1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49
6. Cube Numbers (n³)
1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216
💡
FAST Aptitude Secret: Always write down the difference between consecutive terms first. If the first differences are not constant, take the difference of those differences (second difference). A constant 2nd difference indicates a quadratic sequence!

Arithmetic Progression (AP)

An Arithmetic Progression (AP) or Arithmetic Sequence is a sequence of numbers in which the difference between any two successive members is a constant quantity.

Mathematical Definition
aₙ₊₁ - aₙ = d   (constant for all n ≥ 1)
Where d is the common difference.

Positive vs. Negative Common Differences

• Increasing AP: When d > 0. Example: 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, ... (here d = +3).
• Decreasing AP: When d < 0. Example: 10, 7, 4, 1, -2, ... (here d = -3).

To verify whether any given sequence is an arithmetic progression, verify that:

d = a₂ - a₁ = a₃ - a₂ = a₄ - a₃

The General (nth) Term Formula of an AP

The general formula allows finding any distant term without writing out intermediate terms:

General Term Formula
aₙ = a₁ + (n - 1)d
• aₙ = value of the nth term
• a₁ = first term
• n = term position (number of terms)
• d = common difference
Solved Entry-Test Example Medium

Question: Find the 20th term of the arithmetic sequence: 4, 7, 10, 13, ...

Step 1Identify known variables: a₁ = 4, d = 7 - 4 = 3, n = 20.
Step 2Substitute into formula: a₂₀ = 4 + (20 - 1) × 3
Step 3Simplify arithmetic: a₂₀ = 4 + 19(3) = 4 + 57 = 61.
Answera₂₀ = 61

Finding Missing Terms in an AP

Entry test questions frequently feature sequences with blanks:

Example 1: Single Missing Term Basic

Find the missing term in: 3, 7, __, 15, 19

Step 1Calculate common difference: d = 7 - 3 = 4.
Step 2Add difference to 2nd term: a₃ = 7 + 4 = 11.
CheckVerify with 4th term: 11 + 4 = 15 ✓
Example 2: Multiple Consecutive Blanks Medium

Find the blanks in: 5, __, __, 20

Step 1Here a₁ = 5 and the 4th term a₄ = 20.
Step 2Use a₄ = a₁ + 3d  ⇒  20 = 5 + 3d  ⇒  3d = 15  ⇒  d = 5.
Step 3Missing terms are 5 + 5 = 10 and 10 + 5 = 15. Sequence: 5, 10, 15, 20.
Topic 08 / 23

Finding the Position (n) of a Term

When a problem gives a numerical value and asks "Which term is equal to X?", solve for n using algebra:

Position Formula Shortcut
n = [(aₙ - a₁) / d] + 1
Always verify that n evaluates to a positive whole integer. If n is a fraction, the number is NOT in the sequence!
Solved Entry-Test Problem FAST Level

Question: In the sequence 5, 9, 13, 17, ... which term is equal to 81?

Step 1Identify values: a₁ = 5, d = 9 - 5 = 4, aₙ = 81.
Step 2Apply shortcut: n = [(81 - 5) / 4] + 1 = [76 / 4] + 1 = 19 + 1 = 20.
Result81 is the 20th term (a₂₀).
Topic 09 / 23

Arithmetic Mean (AM) & Inserting Means

The Arithmetic Mean (AM) between two numbers a and b is the number A such that a, A, b forms an arithmetic progression.

Arithmetic Mean Formulas
AM = (a + b) / 2
• For inserting k arithmetic means between a and b:
d = (b - a) / (k + 1)
Inserting 3 Arithmetic Means Medium

Insert 3 arithmetic means between 8 and 24.

Step 1a = 8, b = 24, k = 3 means.
Step 2Calculate d = (24 - 8) / (3 + 1) = 16 / 4 = 4.
Step 3Means are: A₁ = 8 + 4 = 12, A₂ = 12 + 4 = 16, A₃ = 16 + 4 = 20.
ResultThe sequence is: 8, 12, 16, 20, 24.
Topic 10 / 23

Arithmetic Series & Sum of First n Terms (Sₙ)

An Arithmetic Series is the sum of terms in an AP. Two standard formulas exist depending on what information is given:

Formula 1 (When d is known)
Sₙ = (n/2)[2a₁ + (n - 1)d]
Use when you know first term, difference, and number of terms.
Formula 2 (When Last Term l is known)
Sₙ = (n/2)(a₁ + l)
Faster when the first and final terms are given directly!
Example: Sum of 30 Terms Medium

Find the sum of the first 30 terms of the series: 2 + 5 + 8 + 11 + ...

Step 1a = 2, d = 3, n = 30.
Step 2S₃₀ = (30/2)[2(2) + (30 - 1)3] = 15[4 + 29(3)] = 15[4 + 87] = 15[91].
Step 315 × 91 = 1365.
AnswerS₃₀ = 1365

Geometric Progression (GP)

A Geometric Progression (GP) is a sequence where each term after the first is obtained by multiplying the previous term by a non-zero constant called the common ratio (r).

Geometric Rule
aₙ₊₁ / aₙ = r   (constant for all n ≥ 1)
General form: a, ar, ar², ar³, ..., arⁿ⁻¹

Types of Common Ratios

  • Integer Ratio (r > 1): 2, 6, 18, 54, ... (r = 3, rapid growth).
  • Fractional Ratio (0 < r < 1): 64, 32, 16, 8, ... (r = 1/2, decay).
  • Alternating Negative Ratio (r < 0): 3, -6, 12, -24, 48, ... (r = -2, oscillating signs).
Topic 12 / 23

Calculating & Verifying Common Ratio (r)

To find the common ratio:

r = a₂ / a₁ = a₃ / a₂
Checking GP Ratio Basic

Find r for: 81, -27, 9, -3, ...

Step 1r = a₂ / a₁ = -27 / 81 = -1/3.
Step 2Check next ratio: r = 9 / -27 = -1/3 ✓
ResultCommon Ratio: r = -1/3
Topic 13 / 23

nth Term of Geometric Sequence

GP General Term Formula
aₙ = a₁ · rⁿ⁻¹
Note that the exponent is strictly n - 1, not n!
Worked Example: Finding 8th Term Medium

Find the 8th term of the geometric sequence: 3, 6, 12, 24, ...

Step 1a₁ = 3, r = 6 / 3 = 2, n = 8.
Step 2Substitute: a₈ = 3 × 2⁸⁻¹ = 3 × 2⁷.
Step 3Since 2⁷ = 128, a₈ = 3 × 128 = 384.
Answera₈ = 384

Sum of Finite Geometric Series (Sₙ)

The sum of the first n terms of a geometric progression is calculated by:

Finite Geometric Series Sum
Sₙ = a(rⁿ - 1) / (r - 1)   [for r > 1]
Sₙ = a(1 - rⁿ) / (1 - r)   [for r < 1]
Both forms are algebraically identical, but choosing the right one avoids negative denominators.
Example: Sum of 6 Terms Medium

Find the sum of the first 6 terms of: 2 + 6 + 18 + 54 + ...

Step 1a = 2, r = 3, n = 6.
Step 2S₆ = 2(3⁶ - 1) / (3 - 1) = 2(729 - 1) / 2 = 728.
AnswerS₆ = 728

Sum of Infinite Geometric Series (S∞)

An infinite geometric series has a finite sum if and only if each successive term becomes progressively smaller in magnitude.

Infinite Sum Formula & Convergence Condition
S∞ = a / (1 - r)   [STRICT CONDITION: |r| < 1]
• If |r| ≥ 1, the infinite series diverges and has NO finite sum!
Example 1: Classic Halving Series Basic

Find the sum of: 1 + 1/2 + 1/4 + 1/8 + ...

Step 1a = 1, r = 1/2. Check condition: |1/2| < 1 ✓
Step 2S∞ = 1 / (1 - 1/2) = 1 / 0.5 = 2.
ResultSum = 2
Example 2: Recurring Decimal Conversion FAST High Frequency

Express 0.454545... as a common fraction using infinite series.

Step 1Write as series: 45/100 + 45/10000 + 45/1000000 + ...
Step 2First term a = 45/100, common ratio r = 1/100.
Step 3S∞ = (45/100) / [1 - 1/100] = (45/100) / (99/100) = 45/99 = 5/11.
ResultFraction = 5/11

Special Number Patterns Frequently Tested

Entry test aptitude questions frequently use sequences that are neither pure AP nor pure GP. Memorize these fundamental sequences:

Triangular Numbers:
1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28...

Formula: Tₙ = n(n+1)/2

Fibonacci Sequence:
1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21, 34...

Rule: aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + aₙ₋₂

Factorial Sequence:
1, 2, 6, 24, 120, 720, 5040...

Rule: n! = n × (n - 1)!

n² - 1 Sequence:
0, 3, 8, 15, 24, 35, 48...

Notice they are all 1 less than perfect squares!

In advanced aptitude tests, two distinct sequences are often interlaced into a single sequence:

Interleaved Two-Sequence Pattern FAST Aptitude

Find the next two terms: 2, 30, 4, 25, 8, 20, 16, 15, __, __

Odd PosLook at positions 1, 3, 5, 7, 9: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32 (Doubling GP, next is 32).
Even PosLook at positions 2, 4, 6, 8, 10: 30, 25, 20, 15, 10 (Subtracting 5 AP, next is 10).
ResultNext two terms are 32, 10.

Real-World Word Problems & Mathematical Models

University admission tests test your ability to convert English descriptions into arithmetic or geometric equations:

Problem 1: Bouncing Ball Total Distance Physics / Math Model

A ball is dropped from a height of 100 meters. Each time it strikes the ground, it rebounds to 1/2 of its previous height. Find the total vertical distance traveled before it comes to rest.

UnderstandInitial drop is 100m. Every rebound travels UP and DOWN (multiplied by 2).
FormulaTotal = Initial + 2 × [Sum of Rebounds]
Rebound GPFirst rebound = 50m. S∞ = 50 / (1 - 1/2) = 100m.
TotalTotal Distance = 100 + 2(100) = 300 meters.
Problem 2: Theater Seating Arrangement Medium

A concert hall has 25 rows of seats. Row 1 has 20 seats, Row 2 has 24 seats, Row 3 has 28 seats, and so on. What is the total seating capacity?

ModelAP with a = 20, d = 4, n = 25.
CalculationS₂₅ = (25/2)[2(20) + (24)(4)] = 12.5[40 + 96] = 12.5 × 136 = 1700.
AnswerTotal capacity = 1,700 seats.

🚀 8 FAST & Scholarship Speed Shortcuts

Shortcut 1: Sum of Two AP Ratios to Term Ratio

If the ratio of sums of n terms of two APs is given as f(n) / g(n), replace n with 2k - 1 to immediately obtain the ratio of their kth terms!

Shortcut 2: Common Difference from Sₙ = An² + Bn

Whenever the sum of an AP is a quadratic expression Sₙ = An² + Bn, the common difference is ALWAYS d = 2A and first term is a₁ = A + B.

Shortcut 3: Sum of First n Natural Numbers

1 + 2 + 3 + ... + n = n(n + 1) / 2.

Shortcut 4: Sum of First n Odd Numbers

1 + 3 + 5 + ... + (2n - 1) = n² (Always a perfect square!).

Shortcut 5: Sum of First n Even Numbers

2 + 4 + 6 + ... + 2n = n(n + 1) = n² + n.

Shortcut 6: Product of Terms in a GP

The product of n terms in a GP is equal to (Middle Term)ⁿ (when n is odd).

Shortcut 7: Telescoping Differences

For series like 1/(1·2) + 1/(2·3) + ... + 1/(n(n+1)) = n / (n + 1).

Shortcut 8: Working Backwards & Option Elimination

When quadratic equations result in tough factoring under timed pressure, substitute middle options (B and C) directly into the given relation.

15 Common Admission Test Mistakes

❌ WRONG:Using aₙ = arⁿ for GP nth term.
✅ CORRECT:The power must be n - 1: aₙ = arⁿ⁻¹.
💡 REASON:The 1st term has r⁰ = 1, so the nth term only multiplies by r (n - 1) times.
❌ WRONG:Applying S∞ = a / (1 - r) when r = 2 or r = -1.5.
✅ CORRECT:The formula is ONLY valid when |r| < 1. Otherwise it diverges!
💡 REASON:If |r| ≥ 1, the terms grow without bound and do not converge to a finite number.
❌ WRONG:Calculating d = a₁ - a₂ instead of a₂ - a₁.
✅ CORRECT:Always subtract the earlier term from the later term: d = a₂ - a₁.
💡 REASON:Getting the sign backwards flips increasing sequences to decreasing.
❌ WRONG:Confusing the value of a term aₙ with its position index n.
✅ CORRECT:n is which number in line it is (1st, 2nd, 10th); aₙ is what the number actually is.
💡 REASON:In 5, 10, 15, 20, the 4th term has n = 4 and a₄ = 20.
