📌 Axial (Pseudovectors)

Directed along the axis of rotation determined by the Right Hand Rule:

  • Angular displacement (Δθ) for infinitesimal angles
  • Angular velocity (ω)
  • Angular acceleration (α)
  • Torque (τ)
  • Angular momentum (L)

📌 Polar (True) Vectors

Directed along the line of translational motion or in the plane of rotation:

  • Position vector (r)
  • Linear velocity (v, tangential to circle)
  • Centripetal acceleration (a_c, directed radially inward)
  • Linear momentum (p)
  • Linear Force (F)

MDCAT High-Yield Examiner Traps

Common misconceptions, trick questions, and rapid mental shortcuts tested on entrance exams.

Trap #1

Uniform Circular Motion Acceleration

Mistake: Thinking that because speed is constant (v = const), acceleration is zero.
Reality: Direction of velocity changes continuously, creating a non-zero centripetal acceleration a_c = v²/r = rω² directed toward the center.
✓ In uniform circular motion: Speed and Kinetic Energy are constant, but Velocity, Momentum, and Acceleration are constantly changing vectors!
Trap #2

Work Done by Centripetal Force

Mistake: Calculating work over one revolution as W = F · 2πr.
Reality: Centripetal force is always perpendicular to instantaneous displacement (θ = 90°), so W = F · d · cos(90°) = 0 at every single instant.
✓ Centripetal force can NEVER change the kinetic energy or speed of a body; it can only change its direction.
Trap #3

Tension Difference in Vertical Loop

Question: How much greater is the tension at the bottom of a vertical circle than at the top?
Reality: T_bottom - T_top = 6 mg, regardless of the loop radius or entry speed (as long as a full loop is made).
✓ Shortcut Formula: ΔT = 6mg. If mass is 2 kg, ΔT = 6 × 2 × 9.8 = 117.6 N immediately!
Trap #4

Top of a Pure Rolling Wheel

Mistake: Assuming all points on a rolling wheel move at the vehicle speed v.
Reality: Contact point with ground is instantaneously at rest (v = 0). Center moves at v_cm. The topmost point moves at twice the vehicle speed (v = 2 v_cm).
✓ Instantaneous axis of rotation passes through the contact point with the ground.
Trap #5

Skater Angular Kinetic Energy

Mistake: Claiming kinetic energy is conserved when a skater pulls arms in.
Reality: Angular momentum L = Iω is conserved. Since K = L² / (2I) and I decreases, kinetic energy increases.
✓ The skater performs internal muscular work to pull masses in against the outward centrifugal inertia.
Trap #6

Angular Displacement as a Vector

Mistake: Treating large angular rotations as true vectors.
Reality: Finite angular rotations do NOT obey the commutative law of vector addition (θ₁ + θ₂ ≠ θ₂ + θ₁).
✓ Only infinitesimal angular displacements (dθ) obey commutative addition and are true vectors!
