Quick Revision Notes

Condensed grammar formulas, error traps, and root-word heuristics for rapid recall.

1. Proximity Rule with 'Either...or / Neither...nor'
When compound subjects are connected by 'either...or' or 'neither...nor', the verb agrees in number and person with the closest subject.
Example: Neither the teacher nor the students were aware of the schedule change.
2. Intervening Parenthetical Phrases
Phrases such as 'along with', 'as well as', 'together with', and 'in addition to' do not alter the grammatical number of the subject. The verb strictly matches the original head subject.
Example: The surgeon, along with his assistants, was commended for the procedure.
1. Dangling Modifiers
An introductory participial or prepositional modifier must be followed immediately by the logical noun executing the action.
Incorrect: Walking into the operating theater, the scalpel fell.
Correct: While the surgeon was walking into the operating theater, he dropped the scalpel.
2. Redundancies & Double Negatives
Adverbs of inherent negativity ('hardly', 'scarcely', 'barely') must never be paired with 'not', 'no', or 'without'.
Incorrect: The patient could not hardly breathe.
Correct: The patient could hardly breathe.
1. High-Frequency Medical & Academic Roots
Bene- (Good/Well: Benevolent, Beneficent, Benign)
Mal- (Bad/Evil: Malevolent, Malignant, Malady)
Loqu- / Loc- (To speak: Loquacious, Eloquent, Soliloquy)
Path- (Feeling / Disease: Pathology, Apathy, Empathy)
2. Context Clue Contrast Heuristic
Watch for contrastive transition conjunctions (nevertheless, whereas, notwithstanding, yet) to infer antonymic meanings in sentence completion questions.
1. Question-First Skimming
Read question stems and key anchor nouns prior to digesting the passage; this focuses mental retention on target facts without reread delays.
2. Avoid Extreme Universal Generalizations
MCQ options featuring absolute quantifiers (always, never, all, impossible, totally) are statistically wrong in inference evaluations.
Affect vs. Effect
Affect is typically a verb meaning to produce an influence ("High stress affects immune response"). Effect is typically a noun denoting a consequence ("The medication showed positive effects").
Elicit vs. Illicit
Elicit (verb: to draw forth response or truth). Illicit (adjective: illegal, unlawful, or forbidden by ethics).

MDCAT English Strategy

Proven techniques used by top medical college qualifiers to achieve maximum marks.

📖

Read Full Sentence First

Always read the entire sentence thoroughly to grasp the intended tense and context before choosing any option.

🔍

Check Subject-Verb Agreement

Locate the true grammatical head subject and disregard intervening prepositional phrases to prevent number confusion.

⏳

Ensure Tense Consistency

Ensure verb tenses remain coherent across compound clauses, conditional structures, and reported speech conversions.

💡

Learn Vocabulary in Context

Use surrounding tone and semantic clues rather than relying on isolated dictionary memorization for difficult synonyms.

🎯

Eliminate Incorrect Options First
