01 • FOUNDATION

What Are Carboxylic Acids?

Organic compounds characterized by the presence of at least one carboxyl group (–COOH) attached to an alkyl, aryl group, or hydrogen atom.

🔧 General Formula & Structure

R–COOH General Formula
CnH2n+1COOH Aliphatic Monocarboxylic
CnH2nO2 Molecular Formula (n ≥ 1)

The term carboxyl is a contraction of CARBonyl (C=O) and hydrOXYL (–OH). However, due to mutual electronic interactions between the two groups, carboxylic acids do not show typical ketone/aldehyde addition reactions, nor standard alcohol properties.

  • If R = H: Gives Formic acid (Methanoic acid, HCOOH) — the only carboxylic acid with a formal aldehydic hydrogen.
  • If R = Alkyl group: Aliphatic carboxylic acids (Fatty acids like acetic, propionic, palmitic, stearic).
  • If R = Aryl group: Aromatic carboxylic acids (e.g., Benzoic acid, C6H5COOH).

🛈 Classification by Carboxyl Count

Monocarboxylic

Contains one –COOH group.

e.g., Ethanoic Acid (CH3COOH)
Dicarboxylic

Contains two –COOH groups.

e.g., Oxalic Acid (HOOC–COOH)
Tricarboxylic

Contains three –COOH groups.

e.g., Citric Acid (Krebs cycle intermediate)
Aromatic

–COOH directly on a benzene ring.

e.g., Benzoic Acid (C6H5COOH)
MDCAT High Yield Tip

Fatty Acids are long-chain aliphatic monocarboxylic acids derived from natural fats and oils (e.g., Palmitic acid C15H31COOH, Stearic acid C17H35COOH). They usually contain an even number of carbon atoms.

02 • GEOMETRY & BONDING

Structure & Hybridization of the Carboxyl Group

Understanding the orbital architecture, planar geometry, and dipole distribution.

sp²
Carboxyl Carbon Hybridization

The carboxyl carbon forms 3 σ bonds with sp² orbitals and 1 unhybridized 2p orbital forming a π bond with oxygen.

~120°
Planar Bond Angles

Bond angles around the carboxyl carbon (∠O=C–O and ∠R–C=O) are approximately 120° forming a planar arrangement.

2 Pairs
Lone Pairs on Each Oxygen

Both carbonyl oxygen and hydroxyl oxygen carry 2 lone pairs, facilitating resonance delocalization and extensive hydrogen bonding.

04 • PHYSICAL PROPERTIES

Physical Properties & Intermolecular Forces

Carboxylic acids form exceptionally strong hydrogen bonds, resulting in higher boiling points than alcohols, aldehydes, and ketones of comparable molar mass.

🔗 Cyclic Dimer Formation

2 Strong H-Bonds

In vapor phase and non-polar solvents (e.g., benzene), carboxylic acids exist as stable cyclic dimers held by two complementary intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

💡 Exam Fact

Because of dimer formation, the apparent molecular weight of acetic acid determined in benzene is 120 g/mol (double its normal molar mass of 60 g/mol)!

🌡️ Boiling Points & Water Solubility

Compound Formula Molar Mass Boiling Point
Ethanoic Acid CH₃COOH 60 g/mol 118 °C
1-Propanol CH₃CH₂CH₂OH 60 g/mol 97 °C
Propanal CH₃CH₂CHO 58 g/mol 49 °C
Butane CH₃(CH₂)₂CH₃ 58 g/mol -0.5 °C
Solubility in Water:

Lower members (C1–C4: Formic, Acetic, Propionic, Butyric) are completely miscible with water due to H-bonding. As the hydrophobic alkyl chain (R–) lengthens, solubility drops dramatically.

State & Odor:

C1–C3 are sharp, pungent liquids. C4–C9 are unpleasant, rancid/goat-like liquids (e.g. Butyric acid in rancid butter). C10+ are odorless wax-like solids.

05 • ACIDIC NATURE & IONIZATION

Acidic Nature & Equilibrium in Solution

Carboxylic acids ionize partially in water to furnish hydronium (H3O+) and carboxylate (RCOO−) ions.

⚖️ Weak Acid Ionization Equilibrium

RCOOH + H2O ⇌ RCOO− + H3O+
Partial Ionization (Equilibrium lies largely to the left)

Carboxylic acids are typical weak acids because they undergo only partial ionization in aqueous solution. For acetic acid, the acid dissociation constant is:

Ka = [CH3COO−][H3O+] / [CH3COOH] ≈ 1.8 × 10−5 mol/dm3 pKa = −log10(Ka) ≈ 4.76
  • Higher Ka (or lower pKa) = Stronger Acid.
  • Weak acid ≠ Non-acidic: Carboxylic acids turn blue litmus red and readily liberate CO2 from carbonates and bicarbonates!

06 • CORE VISUAL CONCEPT

Resonance Stabilization of the Carboxylate Ion

Why is a carboxylic acid vastly more acidic than an alcohol or phenol? The answer lies in the symmetry and resonance of the RCOO− conjugate base.

✨ The Resonance Hybrid of Carboxylate Anion

When RCOOH loses a proton, the negative charge is NOT stuck on a single oxygen. It is completely delocalized across both electronegative oxygen atoms.

Canonical Form I
R–C(=O)–O−
Negative charge on bottom O
⟷ Equivalent Canonical Forms
Canonical Form II
R–C(O−)=O
Negative charge on top O
≡ Resonance Hybrid
Actual Resonance Hybrid
R—C
O−½ O−½
Both C–O bonds are identical (127 pm)

❌ Alkoxide Ion (R–O−) from Alcohol

Negative charge is localized on a single oxygen atom. The electron-donating alkyl group increases charge density, destabilizing the anion. Thus, alcohols are extremely weak acids (pKa ≈ 16).

✅ Carboxylate Ion (R–COO−) from Acid

Negative charge is spread symmetrically between two highly electronegative oxygen atoms in identical resonance contributors. This extraordinary stabilization drives equilibrium toward H+ release!

Esterification Reaction (Fischer Esterification)

🧪 Isotopic Tracer Evidence (18O)

When alcohol labeled with heavy oxygen (C2H518OH) is used, the heavy oxygen remains inside the ester (CH3CO–18OC2H5). This proves that the –OH group comes from the carboxylic acid, and only –H comes from the alcohol!

⚙️ Dual Role of conc. H2SO4

1. Acid Catalyst: Protonates the carbonyl oxygen, making the carbonyl carbon more electrophilic.
2. Dehydrating Agent: Absorbs water (H2O) formed during the reaction, shifting the equilibrium to the right (Le Chatelier's principle) to maximize ester yield.

10 • KEY CARBOXYLIC ACIDS

Important Carboxylic Acid Compounds

High-yield industrial, physiological, and laboratory compounds tested in MDCAT entrance exams.

🐜

Methanoic Acid (Formic Acid)

HCOOH
  • Found in venom of red ants (Formica), bees, and stinging nettles.
  • Unique property: Possesses both an acid group and a formal aldehyde group (H–C=O).
  • Reduces ammoniacal AgNO3 (Tollens' reagent) and Fehling's solution.
  • Dehydrated by conc. H2SO4 to toxic Carbon Monoxide (CO + H2O).
🥣

Ethanoic Acid (Acetic Acid)

CH3COOH
  • Vinegar is a 5–8% aqueous solution of acetic acid.
  • Glacial Acetic Acid: 100% pure anhydrous acetic acid. Freezes at 16.6 °C into ice-like crystalline solids.
  • Boiling point is 118 °C.
  • Industrial manufacturing by aerobic bacterial oxidation of ethyl alcohol.
⭐

Benzoic Acid

C6H5COOH
  • Simplest aromatic carboxylic acid; white crystalline solid (m.p. 122 °C).
  • Undergoes sublimation upon heating.
  • Its sodium salt, Sodium Benzoate (C6H5COONa), is widely used as a food and fruit juice preservative.
  • Synthesized by vigorous oxidation of toluene with alkaline KMnO4.
🔥 MDCAT EXAM ZONE

🔥 MDCAT High-Yield Exam Zone & Common Traps

The golden facts you must memorize and the trick questions examiners set to catch unprepared students.

✔️ MUST REMEMBER FOR MDCAT

✓ –COOH = carboxyl group (Carbonyl + Hydroxyl).
✓ Carboxyl carbon = Carbon 1 in all IUPAC chains.
✓ Carboxylic acids are WEAK acids (partially ionized, Ka ≈ 10−5).
✓ Carboxylate ion is resonance-stabilized (two identical C–O bonds, 127 pm).
✓ NaHCO3 gives CO2 gas with brisk effervescence (Diagnostic test).
✓ NaOH gives salt + water (Neutralization).
✓ Alcohol + acid gives ester + water (Reversible, H from alcohol, OH from acid).
✓ LiAlH4 gives primary alcohol (NaBH4 cannot reduce carboxylic acids).
✓ Formic acid reduces Tollens' reagent because it possesses a formal aldehydic H.
✓ Acetic acid exists as a cyclic dimer in non-polar solvents with Mr = 120.

⚠️ Common MDCAT Examiner Traps & Misconceptions

TRAP 1 Gas with NaHCO3
❌ WRONG: Reaction of RCOOH with NaHCO3 produces H2 gas.
✅ CORRECT: It produces Carbon Dioxide (CO2) gas with brisk effervescence that turns limewater milky!
TRAP 2 Acid Strength Classification
❌ WRONG: Carboxylic acids are strong mineral acids like HCl.
✅ CORRECT: They are weak organic acids (< 1% ionized), though much stronger than phenols and alcohols.
TRAP 3 Reduction Final Product
❌ WRONG: Strong reduction with LiAlH4 stops at an aldehyde.
✅ CORRECT: LiAlH4 reduces carboxylic acids directly to Primary Alcohol (R–CH2OH).
TRAP 4 IUPAC Carbon Numbering
❌ WRONG: The carbon next to –COOH is carbon 1, so –COOH carbon is carbon 2.
✅ CORRECT: The –COOH carbon is ALWAYS Carbon 1. The adjacent carbon is C-2 (α-carbon).
TRAP 5 Esterification Cleavage
❌ WRONG: The –OH group in esterification water comes from alcohol.
✅ CORRECT: The –OH group comes from the Carboxylic Acid, and only the –H comes from the alcohol.
TRAP 6 Reducing Agent Specificity
❌ WRONG: Sodium borohydride (NaBH4) reduces carboxylic acids.
✅ CORRECT: NaBH4 CANNOT reduce carboxylic acids or esters. Only strong LiAlH4 or B2H6 can.

The essential high-density memory anchors for quick last-minute review before walking into your exam room.

01

Functional Group

–COOH = Carbonyl (C=O) + Hydroxyl (–OH). sp² hybridized, planar ~120° bond angles.

02

IUPAC Carbon #1

The carboxyl carbon is always Carbon-1. Suffix replaces "-e" with "-oic acid".

03

Cyclic Dimer

Forms two hydrogen bonds creating a stable cyclic dimer; boils higher than alcohols of equal molar mass.

04

Resonance Stabilization

Carboxylate ion (RCOO−) has equivalent C–O bonds (127 pm) with negative charge delocalized over 2 oxygens.

05

Inductive Effects on Acidity

−I groups (F, Cl, NO2, CF3) increase acidity.
+I groups (Alkyls) decrease acidity (HCOOH > CH3COOH).

06

NaHCO3 Diagnostic Test

Gives brisk effervescence of CO2. Distinguishes carboxylic acids from phenols and alcohols!

07

Esterification Rule

Acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water (conc. H2SO4). –OH comes from acid, –H from alcohol.

08

LiAlH4 Reduction

Reduces carboxylic acids directly to Primary Alcohols (RCH2OH). NaBH4 fails.

09

Derivatives Reactivity

RCOCl > (RCO)2O > RCOOR' > RCONH2 based on leaving group quality (Cl− > RCOO− > RO− > NH2−).

10

Formic vs Acetic

Formic acid (HCOOH) reduces Tollens' reagent. Acetic acid (CH3COOH) freezes at 16.6 °C as glacial acetic acid.
