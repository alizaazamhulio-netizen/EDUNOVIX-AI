01. Fundamental Concept

What is a Wave?

Core Definition

A wave is a disturbance that transfers energy and momentum from one place to another without the permanent transfer of matter.

⭐ MDCAT Key Phrase: “Energy is transferred from point A to point B, while medium particles only execute simple harmonic motion about their mean equilibrium position.”

When a wave travels through water, the water molecules bob up and down or in small circles, but they do not travel across the sea with the wave crests.

Mechanical vs Electromagnetic Waves

Mechanical Waves:

Require a material medium for propagation. Cannot travel through vacuum.
Examples: Sound waves, waves on stretched strings, water waves, seismic waves.

Electromagnetic (EM) Waves:

Do NOT require a material medium. Propagate through vacuum at speed $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.
Examples: Visible light, radio waves, microwaves, X-rays, gamma rays.

02. Mode of Propagation

Transverse & Longitudinal Waves

Interactive Particle Motion Simulator

Transverse Wave: Particles oscillate perpendicular (90°) to wave propagation. Features: Crest, Trough, Amplitude, Wavelength. Examples: Light, EM Waves, Wave on String.

Transverse Wave Features

  • Crest: The point of maximum positive displacement from equilibrium.
  • Trough: The point of maximum negative displacement from equilibrium.
  • Vibration direction: Perpendicular ($\perp$) to energy propagation.
  • Polarization: CAN be polarized.

Longitudinal Wave Features

  • Compression: Region of high pressure and maximum particle density.
  • Rarefaction: Region of low pressure and minimum particle density.
  • Vibration direction: Parallel ($\parallel$) to energy propagation.
  • Polarization: CANNOT be polarized.
03. Key Parameters

Important Wave Quantities

Frequency (f)

Number of complete vibrations/cycles passing a fixed point per second.

f = 1 / T
SI Unit: Hertz (Hz) = $\text{s}^{-1}$
MDCAT Rule: Frequency depends strictly on the source.

Time Period (T)

Time taken by a particle of the medium to complete one full oscillation.

T = 1 / f
SI Unit: Seconds (s)
Relationship: $f \times T = 1$

Wavelength (λ)

Distance between two consecutive points in the exact same phase of vibration.

λ = v / f
SI Unit: Meter (m)
Crest-to-Crest or Compression-to-Compression distance.

Amplitude (A)

Maximum displacement of a particle from its central mean equilibrium position.

Intensity ∝ A²
SI Unit: Meter (m)
Sound: Greater amplitude $\to$ louder sound.

Wave Speed (v)

Distance covered by the wave disturbance per unit time through a medium.

v = f λ
SI Unit: $\text{m/s}$
Depends purely on the medium's elasticity and density.

⭐ MDCAT Formula Alert

If the medium does not change, wave speed $v$ is CONSTANT. Therefore:

f ∝ 1 / λ (when v = const)

If frequency doubles ($2f$), wavelength is cut in half ($\lambda/2$).

05. Wave Geometry

Phase & Phase Difference

In-Phase vs Out-of-Phase

Phase specifies the state of motion of a vibrating particle (both its position and direction of motion).

In-Phase Points:
Separated by path difference $\Delta x = \lambda, 2\lambda, 3\lambda \dots n\lambda$
Phase difference $\Delta \phi = 0, 2\pi, 4\pi \dots 2n\pi\text{ rad}$
Opposite Phase (Out-of-Phase):
Separated by path difference $\Delta x = \frac{\lambda}{2}, \frac{3\lambda}{2}, \frac{5\lambda}{2} \dots (2n+1)\frac{\lambda}{2}$
Phase difference $\Delta \phi = \pi, 3\pi, 5\pi \dots (2n+1)\pi\text{ rad}$
⚠️ MDCAT Formula Alert

Phase & Path Difference Formula

Δφ = (2π / λ) × Δx

MDCAT Quick Rule:
• For path diff $\Delta x = \lambda \implies \Delta\phi = 2\pi\text{ rad}$ ($360^\circ$)
• For path diff $\Delta x = \lambda/2 \implies \Delta\phi = \pi\text{ rad}$ ($180^\circ$)
• For path diff $\Delta x = \lambda/4 \implies \Delta\phi = \pi/2\text{ rad}$ ($90^\circ$)

06. Wave Addition

Superposition Principle

When two or more waves overlap in the same medium, the resultant displacement at any point is the algebraic sum of their individual displacements:

y = y₁ + y₂ + y₃ + ... + yₙ
0° (Constructive)
07. Interference

Constructive & Destructive Interference

Constructive Interference

Occurs when waves meet in phase (Crest meets Crest, Trough meets Trough). Amplitudes reinforce each other to produce maximum intensity.

Δx = n λ (n = 0, 1, 2, 3...)

Resultant Amplitude $A_{\text{max}} = A_1 + A_2$
If $A_1 = A_2 = A$, then $A_{\text{resultant}} = 2A$, Intensity $I \propto 4A^2$.

Destructive Interference

Occurs when waves meet out of phase by 180° (Crest meets Trough). Amplitudes cancel each other to produce minimum intensity.

Δx = (2n + 1) λ / 2

Resultant Amplitude $A_{\text{min}} = |A_1 - A_2|$
If $A_1 = A_2$, then $A_{\text{resultant}} = 0$ (complete darkness / silence).

08. Stationary Waves

Standing (Stationary) Waves

Produced when two identical waves (same frequency, wavelength, speed, amplitude) travel in opposite directions along the same medium.

Harmonic n = 3 (3 loops, 4 nodes, 3 antinodes)
Node (N):

Point of permanently ZERO displacement, zero velocity, and maximum strain/pressure.

Antinode (A):

Point of MAXIMUM displacement and maximum speed ($2A$).

⭐ Vital MDCAT Formulas:
• Distance between two consecutive nodes $= \frac{\lambda}{2}$
• Distance between two consecutive antinodes $= \frac{\lambda}{2}$
• Distance between an adjacent node and antinode $= \frac{\lambda}{4}$
09. Periodic Interference

Beats & Beat Frequency

Beat Phenomena

When two sound waves of slightly different frequencies ($f_1 \approx f_2$, diff $\le 10\text{ Hz}$) travel in the same direction, periodic waxings and wanings of sound intensity occur.

f_b = |f₁ − f₂|

• 1 Beat = 1 Maxima + 1 Minima
• Human ear can distinguish beats when $f_b \le 10\text{ Hz}$ due to persistence of hearing ($0.1\text{ s}$).

10. Relative Motion

Doppler Effect

The apparent change in observed frequency of a wave caused by relative motion between the source of wave and the observer.

Source Moving Toward Observer:

Wavefronts get compressed $\to$ Wavelength decreases ($\lambda' < \lambda$) $\to$ Observed frequency INCREASES ($f' > f$). Pitch sounds higher.

Source Moving Away from Observer:

Wavefronts spread out $\to$ Wavelength increases ($\lambda' > \lambda$) $\to$ Observed frequency DECREASES ($f' < f$). Pitch sounds lower.

⭐ MDCAT Trap: Doppler effect changes the observed (apparent) frequency perceived by the listener. It does NOT mean the source changes its actual emitted frequency!
11. Acoustic Physics

Sound Waves

Properties of Sound

  • Mechanical & Longitudinal wave.
  • Requires a material medium (air, water, steel).
  • Cannot travel through vacuum.
  • Speed in air $\approx 340\text{ m/s}$ at RTP ($20^\circ\text{C}$).

Pitch vs Loudness

Frequency determines Pitch:
Higher frequency $\implies$ shrill / high-pitched voice.
Amplitude determines Loudness:
Larger amplitude $\implies$ louder sound ($I \propto A^2$).

Speed in Media

v_solid > v_liquid > v_gas

Steel: $\approx 5100\text{ m/s}$
Water: $\approx 1500\text{ m/s}$
Air: $\approx 340\text{ m/s}$
Due to much higher Elastic Modulus ($E$) in solids.

12. Energy Transmission

Intensity & Inverse Square Law

Intensity Definition & Formula

Intensity is the acoustic/radiant power transmitted per unit area perpendicular to wave direction:

I = P / A
SI Unit: $\text{Watt/m}^2$ ($\text{W}\cdot\text{m}^{-2}$)

For a spherical point source spreading in 3D space:

I ∝ 1 / r²

13. Boundary Conditions

Reflection of Waves

Law of Reflection

When a wave strikes a boundary, it bounces back into the same medium:

Angle of Incidence (i) = Angle of Reflection (r)

Wave speed $v$, frequency $f$, and wavelength $\lambda$ remain UNCHANGED because the wave stays in the same medium.

Phase Change at Boundaries

Fixed / Denser End:
Undergoes 180° (π rad) phase inversion. Crest reflects back as a Trough.
Free / Rarer End:
Undergoes 0° (0 rad) phase change. Crest reflects back as a Crest.
14. Medium Transition

Refraction of Waves

Refraction is the change in direction and wavelength of a wave when it crosses the boundary from one medium into another due to a change in wave speed.

⭐ CRITICAL MDCAT TRAP: During refraction, FREQUENCY REMAINS CONSTANT!
Since $v = f\lambda$ and $f = \text{constant}$, then $\mathbf{\lambda \propto v}$.
• In denser (slower) medium: Speed decreases $\implies$ Wavelength shortens ($\lambda \downarrow$).
• In rarer (faster) medium: Speed increases $\implies$ Wavelength lengthens ($\lambda \uparrow$).
15. Wave Bending

Diffraction of Waves

Diffraction is the bending and spreading of waves around the edges of obstacles or through an aperture/slit.

Slit ≈ λ (Maximum Spreading / Strong Diffraction)
⭐ Condition for Maximum Diffraction: The aperture size ($d$) must be comparable to the wavelength ($\lambda$):
Opening Size (d) ≈ λ
Longer wavelengths (e.g. Sound or Radio waves) diffract much more prominently in daily life than short visible light waves.
16. Wave Orientation

Polarization

Polarization is the process of confining the vibrations of a wave to a single plane perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

0° (Parallel - Max Transmission)
⭐ MDCAT Proof: Polarization is strictly a property of TRANSVERSE WAVES (such as light). It proves light is transverse.
⭐ MDCAT Trap: Longitudinal waves (sound) CANNOT be polarized because particle vibrations are already parallel to wave propagation.

18. High-Yield Pitfalls

⚠️ MDCAT Traps & Pitfalls

Click or hover on any trap card below to reveal the high-yield examiner trick:

Trap 01: Refraction Does wave frequency change during refraction?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
NO!

Frequency is determined solely by the source oscillator and remains CONSTANT. Only speed $v$ and wavelength $\lambda$ change!

Trap 02: Sound in Vacuum Can sound propagate through outer space vacuum?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
NO!

Sound is a mechanical wave that requires elastic medium particles to transmit compressions. In a vacuum, sound is zero.

Trap 03: Polarization Nature Can sound waves be polarized?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
NEVER!

Sound is longitudinal (particles oscillate parallel to wave direction). Only transverse waves can be polarized.

Trap 04: Node vs Antinode What is the displacement and pressure at a node?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
ZERO Displacement!

At a Node: Displacement = 0, but Pressure Variation / Strain is MAXIMUM. (At Antinode: Displacement is Max, Pressure Variation is Zero).

Trap 05: Node Spacings Distance between consecutive node & antinode?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
λ / 4

Node-to-Node is $\lambda/2$. But Node-to-adjacent-Antinode is $\lambda/4$!

Trap 06: Doppler Frequency Does the siren really change its emitted pitch?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
Source Frequency is Fixed!

The siren always emits the same $f$. The Doppler shift only alters the observed (apparent) frequency due to relative motion.

Trap 07: Loudness vs Pitch Does increasing sound amplitude increase its pitch?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
NO!

Amplitude controls Loudness & Energy ($I \propto A^2$). Frequency controls Pitch & Shrillness.

Trap 08: Speed vs Frequency If $f$ increases in air, does wave speed $v$ increase?
Hover/Click to reveal answer ➔
Speed Stays Constant!

Wave speed in air is fixed by medium properties ($\sqrt{\gamma P/\rho}$). An increase in $f$ causes an exact proportional decrease in wavelength $\lambda$.
