Section 01

What is Genetics?

Genetics is the biological science studying heredity, biological variation, gene transmission, and the molecular mechanisms controlling physical traits.

Heredity

★★★

Transmission of physiological, anatomical, and biochemical characteristics from parents to offspring.

Example: Inheritance of blood groups (ABO) or eye color across family generations.
MDCAT Tip: Governed by Mendelian segregation and independent assortment during meiosis.

Variation

★★★

Differences in phenotypic and genotypic characteristics among individuals of the same species.

Example: Continuous variation (human height) vs discontinuous variation (blood groups).
MDCAT Tip: Generated by mutation, crossing over (Prophase I), independent assortment, and fertilization.

Gene

★★★

Specific segment of DNA containing sequence information required to synthesize a functional polypeptide or RNA.

Example: The HBB gene on chromosome 11 coding for hemoglobin β-chain.
MDCAT Tip: The functional and physical unit of heredity.

Allele

★★★

Alternative, variant form of a gene located at the exact same locus on homologous chromosomes.

Example: Allele T (tall pea plant) vs allele t (dwarf pea plant).
MDCAT Tip: An individual inherits two alleles per autosomal gene (one maternal, one paternal).

Locus

★★☆

The precise physical and anatomical address of a specific gene on a chromosome.

Example: CFTR gene locus is 7q31.2 (Chromosome 7, long arm q, region 3, band 1).
MDCAT Tip: Homologous chromosomes always carry identical gene loci in identical order.

Genome

★★★

The complete genetic inventory and total DNA sequence present within a single haploid set of chromosomes in an organism.

Example: Human genome consists of ~3.2 billion base pairs containing ~20,000–25,000 protein-coding genes.
MDCAT Tip: Includes both coding exons and non-coding introns, regulatory regions, and repetitive DNA.

Human Somatic Karyotype (46 Chromosomes / 23 Pairs)

Normal diploid somatic cells contain 2n = 46 chromosomes, comprising 22 pairs of Autosomes (Chromosomes 1–22) and 1 pair of Sex Chromosomes (XX in females, XY in males).

Homologous Chromosomes Defined

Pairs possessing identical length, centromere position, and staining band patterns, carrying genes for identical traits at identical loci. One homolog is inherited maternally, one paternally.

MDCAT Distinction: Autosomes vs Sex Chromosomes

Autosomes control somatic non-gender traits equally in both sexes. Sex chromosomes (Allosomes) determine biological sex and transmit X-linked or Y-linked traits.

Section 03

DNA Structure & Complementary Base Pairing

DNA is a double-stranded, antiparallel polymer of deoxyribonucleotides twisting into a right-handed double helix.

Each deoxyribonucleotide consists of three components:

  • Deoxyribose Pentose Sugar (lacking an oxygen at carbon 2′).
  • Inorganic Phosphate Group (forming 3′,5′-phosphodiester linkages along the outer backbone).
  • Nitrogenous Base: Purines (Adenine, Guanine) and Pyrimidines (Thymine, Cytosine).

Section 10

Mendelian Genetics & Fundamental Laws

Gregor Mendel (Father of Genetics) established the foundational laws of heredity using controlled crosses of garden pea plants (Pisum sativum).

Law of Segregation

★★★

The two alleles for a heritable trait segregate during gamete formation, so each gamete carries strictly ONE allele per gene locus.

Meiotic Basis: Separation of homologous chromosomes during Anaphase I of Meiosis.

Law of Independent Assortment

★★★

Alleles of two or more different non-linked genes assort independently of one another during gamete formation.

Meiotic Basis: Random independent orientation of homologous pairs along equatorial plate in Metaphase I.

Genotype vs Phenotype

★★★

Genotype: The complete genetic allele composition (e.g. AA, Aa, aa). Phenotype: The observable physical trait (e.g. Purple vs White flower).

MDCAT Trap: Organisms with identical phenotypes (AA and Aa) can possess completely different genotypes!

Section 12

The Test Cross (A_ × aa)

A test cross determines the unknown genotype of an organism exhibiting a dominant phenotype by breeding it with a known homozygous recessive tester (aa).

Result for Heterozygous Parent (Aa × aa):

• Offspring: 50% Aa (Dominant phenotype) : 50% aa (Recessive phenotype) — 1:1 Ratio.
• Conclusion: The appearance of even ONE recessive individual proves the unknown dominant parent carried a hidden recessive allele (Aa).

Sections 14–16

Incomplete Dominance, Codominance & Sex-Linkage

Non-Mendelian inheritance patterns including blended heterozygotes, dual-expressed alleles, and X-linked transmission.

Incomplete Dominance

Neither allele is completely dominant; heterozygous individuals exhibit an intermediate blended phenotype.

Example (4 O’Clock Plant / Snapdragon):
Red (RR) × White (rr) → 100% Pink (Rr).
F2 Cross: 1 Red (RR) : 2 Pink (Rr) : 1 White (rr) = 1:2:1 Ratio.
In Incomplete Dominance, phenotypic ratio exactly mirrors genotypic ratio (1:2:1).

Codominance & ABO Blood Groups

Both alleles are simultaneously and fully expressed in the heterozygous phenotype without blending.

Example (AB Blood Group):
Alleles IA and IB are codominant. Allele i is recessive.
• Genotype IAIB expresses BOTH A & B antigens on erythrocyte membranes.
Multiple Alleles: 3 alleles (IA, IB, i) produce 6 genotypes and 4 blood phenotypes (A, B, AB, O).

Sex-Linked (X-Linked Recessive) Inheritance

Human males are hemizygous (XY) and express whatever allele is present on their single X chromosome. Females (XX) need two copies of the recessive allele to be affected.

Hemophilia A & B

Deficiency of clotting Factor VIII (Hemophilia A) or Factor IX (Hemophilia B). Leads to prolonged internal bleeding.

Red-Green Color Blindness

Defective opsin photopigment genes on the X chromosome. Affects ~8% of males vs ~0.5% of females.

Criss-Cross Inheritance

Carrier mother (XHXh) transmits mutant X to sons (50% risk). Affected fathers transmit mutant X to 100% of daughters (carriers) and 0% of sons.

Sections 17–20

Meiosis, Crossing Over & Genetic Linkage

Meiosis converts 1 diploid germ cell (2n) into 4 genetically distinct haploid gametes (n), providing the fundamental cellular mechanism for Mendelian inheritance.

Prophase I (Synapsis & Crossing Over)

🧬 [Homologs Paired: Maternal (Pink) & Paternal (Blue) Chiasma Exchange]

Homologous chromosomes pair up closely in synapsis to form bivalents (tetrads). Non-sister chromatids exchange genetic segments at chiasmata (crossing over), creating novel allele combinations.

Autosomal Dominant Inheritance

  • Trait appears in every generation (no skipping of generations).
  • Every affected individual has at least one affected parent.
  • Males and females are affected with equal frequency.
  • Examples: Huntington's disease, Achondroplasia, Marfan syndrome.
Sections 22–24

Gene Mutations & Chromosomal Nondisjunction

Mutations are permanent alterations in genetic nucleotide sequence or chromosome architecture, acting as the ultimate source of all novel biological variation.

Nondisjunction & Aneuploidy (Down Syndrome / Trisomy 21)

Nondisjunction is the failure of homologous chromosomes (in Meiosis I) or sister chromatids (in Meiosis II) to separate properly, yielding gametes with n+1 or n-1 chromosomes.

Trisomy 21 (Down Syndrome): 47 chromosomes (47, XX,+21 or 47, XY,+21). Manifests with intellectual disability, characteristic epicanthic facial folds, simian crease, and congenital heart defects. Risk correlates with advanced maternal age.

🔥 MDCAT GENETICS — 25+ High-Yield Must Know

Essential high-yield facts, numerical constants, and memory anchors frequently tested in MDCAT biology examinations.

Gene

Functional unit/segment of hereditary information encoding polypeptide or functional RNA.

Allele

Alternative variant forms of a gene occupying the same locus on homologous chromosomes.

Locus

Fixed physical chromosomal position of a gene.

Human Somatic Count

46 chromosomes (23 homologous pairs): 44 autosomes + 2 sex chromosomes.

Haploid Gamete Count

23 chromosomes (n = 23) in mature spermatozoa and secondary oocytes.

DNA Replication

Semiconservative process occurring in the nuclear S-phase of interphase.

Helicase

Unwinds double helix by cleaving hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs.

DNA Polymerase III

Main replicative enzyme; synthesizes DNA strictly 5′ → 3′ from a 3′-OH primer.

DNA Ligase

Seals single-strand phosphodiester nicks between Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.

Leading Strand

Synthesized continuously toward the advancing replication fork in 5′ → 3′ direction.

Lagging Strand

Synthesized discontinuously away from the fork in short Okazaki fragments.

Transcription

DNA → mRNA catalyzed by RNA Polymerase inside the eukaryotic nucleus.

Translation

mRNA → Polypeptide protein chain occurring on cytosolic and rough ER ribosomes.

Start Codon (AUG)

Universal initiation codon coding for Methionine in eukaryotes (Formyl-Met in prokaryotes).

Stop Codons

UAA (Ochre), UAG (Amber), UGA (Opal) — do not code for amino acids.

Law of Segregation

Alleles separate into distinct gametes during meiotic Anaphase I.

Independent Assortment

Non-homologous chromosomes orient independently along Metaphase I plate.

Monohybrid F2 Ratio

Genotypic: 1:2:1 (1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa). Phenotypic under complete dominance: 3:1.

Dihybrid F2 Ratio

Classic phenotypic ratio: 9:3:3:1 for two unlinked independently assorting genes.

Test Cross

Unknown dominant phenotype (A_) crossed with homozygous recessive (aa).

Incomplete Dominance

Heterozygote displays intermediate blend (e.g. Pink flower); F2 phenotype is 1:2:1.

Codominance

Both alleles simultaneously expressed (e.g. AB Blood Group IAIB).

Crossing Over

Prophase I non-sister chromatid exchange at chiasmata creating recombinant gametes.

Genetic Linkage

Syntenic genes close on the same chromosome inherited together; do NOT assort independently.

Frameshift Mutation

Insertion/deletion of bases not divisible by 3, altering entire downstream reading frame.

Trisomy 21

Down syndrome caused by meiotic nondisjunction of chromosome 21 (47, XX/XY,+21).
