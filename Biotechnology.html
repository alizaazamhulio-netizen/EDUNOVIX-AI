SECTION 01

1. What is Biotechnology?

Biotechnology is the broad discipline of applying biological organisms, cells, or biological molecules to develop useful products, industrial processes, and therapeutics.

🏛️

Classical vs. Modern Biotechnology

Classical Biotechnology: Utilizes natural biological capabilities of intact microorganisms (e.g., fermentation for bread, brewing, and yogurt production).

Modern Biotechnology: Centers on deliberate manipulation of genetic material using recombinant DNA technology, gene cloning, and molecular editing tools.

🎯

Key MDCAT Terminological Distinction

• Biotechnology: Broad umbrella field using biological systems for societal utility.
• Genetic Engineering: The deliberate molecular alteration/manipulation of genetic material.
• Recombinant DNA Technology: The specific laboratory technique of splicing DNA from distinct sources to create hybrid chimeric DNA.

Major Fields of Application

💊

Medicine

Recombinant insulin, human growth hormone, monoclonal antibodies, gene therapy, and mRNA vaccines.

🌱

Agriculture

Bt cotton pest resistance, Golden Rice (pro-Vitamin A biofortification), and herbicide tolerance.

🌍

Environment

Bioremediation using oil-degrading microorganisms, sewage treatment, and biofuel production.

🔎

Forensics

DNA profiling, VNTR/STR identification, paternity testing, and criminal case evidence analysis.

SECTION 03 ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD

3. Restriction Enzymes (Molecular Scissors)

Bacterial endonucleases that recognize specific palindromic DNA sequences and cleave phosphodiester bonds at or near those sequences.

Palindromic Recognition Sequences

A sequence is palindromic when it reads the exact same in the 5' ➔ 3' direction on both complementary strands.

5' ── G ↓ A A T T C ── 3'
3' ── C T T A A ↑ G ── 5' (EcoRI cleavage site)

Sticky vs. Blunt Ends

• Sticky (Cohesive) Ends: Staggered cuts leaving single-stranded overhangs that readily base-pair with complementary sequences.
• Blunt Ends: Straight cuts across both strands at the identical nucleotide position (e.g., SmaI), leaving no overhangs.

5. Plasmids & Cloning Vectors

A vector is a DNA carrier molecule. Plasmids are small, circular, extrachromosomal, double-stranded DNA molecules found naturally in bacteria that replicate autonomously.

Origin of Replication (ori)

A specific DNA sequence where replication initiates inside the host bacterium. Without 'ori', the plasmid cannot duplicate when the host cell divides.

Origin of replication controls copy number per bacterial cell (high copy vs low copy plasmids).

SECTIONS 06 & 07 ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD

6. Recombinant DNA & Transformation

Forming chimeric DNA molecules by combining genetic elements from two organisms, followed by uptake into bacterial hosts.

6. Recombinant DNA (rDNA)

Recombinant DNA is DNA created by artificially linking DNA from different species or sources. In molecular cloning:

Human Insulin Gene + Linearized Plasmid ➔ Recombinant Chimeric Plasmid

7. Bacterial Transformation

Transformation is the process through which competent bacterial cells take up naked foreign DNA from their surroundings.

• Chemical Competence: CaCl2 treatment creates transient membrane pores.
• Heat Shock: 42°C pulse allows plasmid entry.
• Electroporation: High-voltage electric pulses enhance permeability.

8. PCR — Polymerase Chain Reaction

Invented by Kary Mullis in 1983. PCR enables rapid in vitro enzymatic amplification of billions of copies of a specific target DNA region from a minute sample.

2. Annealing

Temperature lowers to allow forward and reverse oligonucleotide primers to bind to complementary 3' ends.

3. Extension

Thermostable Taq DNA Polymerase adds dNTPs to extend the primers in the 5' ➔ 3' direction.

SECTION 09 ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD

9. Gel Electrophoresis

A laboratory method for separating macromolecules (DNA, RNA, proteins) based on charge, physical size, and molecular shape across an agarose or polyacrylamide matrix.

1. DNA Charge

DNA has a constant negative charge due to its phosphate groups (PO₄³⁻).

2. Electric Migration

Under electric current, DNA migrates from Cathode (-) toward Anode (+).

3. Size Sieve Principle

Agarose pores act as a sieve: Smaller fragments travel faster & farther.

SECTIONS 11 & 12 ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD

11. DNA Sequencing & 12. Types of Cloning

Distinguishing molecular gene replication from organism-level reproductive duplication.

11. DNA Sequencing

Determines the exact chronological order of nucleotides (Adenine, Thymine, Guanine, Cytosine) in a DNA strand.

A T G C A A G T C

• Sanger Sequencing: Dideoxynucleotide (ddNTP) chain termination method.
• Next-Gen Sequencing (NGS): High-throughput massively parallel sequencing.

12. Molecular vs. Reproductive Cloning

Molecular (Gene) Cloning:

Copying a specific DNA fragment or gene in vitro / in bacteria to obtain multiple copies of that sequence.

Reproductive Cloning:

Producing an entire multi-cellular organism genetically identical to the nucleus donor (e.g. Dolly the sheep via Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer - SCNT).

MDCAT Must Remember: Molecular cloning ≠ Reproductive cloning!

SECTION 14 ⭐⭐⭐ CLASSIC MDCAT EXAMPLE

14. Recombinant Human Insulin Production

In 1978/1982, Eli Lilly and Genentech produced the first recombinant biopharmaceutical (Humulin) using genetically modified E. coli bacteria.

Production Pathway

1. Synthesize cDNA corresponding to human insulin Chain A (21 amino acids) and Chain B (30 amino acids).
2. Insert cDNA into plasmid vector adjacent to lacZ promoter.
3. Transform into E. coli; bacteria synthesize beta-galactosidase fusion proteins.
4. Cleave fusion proteins using cyanogen bromide (CNBr).
5. Combine purified A & B chains in vitro to form two inter-chain disulfide bridges and one intra-chain disulfide bond.

Why Recombinant Insulin is Superior

• Eliminates Allergic Reactions: Identical in sequence to native human insulin (unlike bovine or porcine insulin which differ by 3 and 1 amino acids respectively).
• Unlimited Scalable Supply: Fermenters produce kilograms of pure hormone without sacrificing millions of slaughterhouse livestock.
• Zero Risk of Animal Pathogen Transmission.

SECTION 15 ⭐⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD

15. Gene Therapy

The insertion, alteration, or replacement of functional genes within an individual's cells and tissues to treat hereditary genetic disorders.

1. Gene Augmentation

Introducing a functional normal copy of a mutated or missing gene to restore normal protein synthesis.

2. Somatic vs Germline

• Somatic: Targets non-reproductive body cells (therapeutic, non-heritable).
• Germline: Modifies egg/sperm/embryo (heritable to future generations, strictly restricted).

3. Classic Case: ADA-SCID

First human gene therapy (1990) treated Severe Combined Immunodeficiency by inserting normal Adenosine Deaminase gene into T-lymphocytes using a retroviral vector.

SECTION 20 ⭐⭐ HIGH YIELD MNEMONIC

20. Southern, Northern & Western Blotting

Molecular blotting techniques transfer macromolecules from a gel to a nitrocellulose/nylon membrane for hybridization probing.

❄️
Gold Standard Mnemonic
S N O W  ➔  D R O P
S=DNA, N=RNA, W=Protein
S
SOUTHERN BLOT
Target: DNA

Detects specific DNA fragments using labeled complementary single-stranded DNA/RNA probes. Named after Edwin Southern (1975).

N
NORTHERN BLOT
Target: RNA

Measures gene expression levels by detecting specific mRNA transcripts in biological samples using radioactive or fluorescent cDNA probes.

W
WESTERN BLOT
Target: PROTEIN

Identifies specific proteins/antigens separated on SDS-PAGE using specific primary and secondary enzyme-linked antibodies (e.g. HIV confirmatory test).

COMPARISONS

📊 High-Yield Comparison Tables

Direct side-by-side differentiation of closely related MDCAT biological concepts.

1. Biotechnology vs. Genetic Engineering

Feature Biotechnology Genetic Engineering
Definition Broad field utilizing living organisms/systems for human benefit. Direct, deliberate manipulation of an organism's genetic material.
Scope Encompasses traditional fermentation, bioremediation, and agriculture. Sub-discipline focused on molecular cloning, rDNA, and CRISPR editing.
Technique Can use intact wild microorganisms without DNA modification. Requires enzymatic cutting, splicing, and recombinant vector insertion.

2. Restriction Endonuclease vs. DNA Ligase

Parameter Restriction Enzyme DNA Ligase
Action Cleaves / hydrolyzes phosphodiester bonds at specific recognition sites. Synthesizes / forms phosphodiester bonds between 3'-OH and 5'-P ends.
Metaphor Molecular Scissors ✂️ Molecular Glue / Linker 🔗
Energy Source Does not require external ATP for simple cleavage. Requires ATP (or NAD+) to drive covalent phosphodiester bond formation.

3. Molecular Cloning vs. Reproductive Cloning

Aspect Molecular (Gene) Cloning Reproductive (Organism) Cloning
Target Specific DNA fragment, gene, or cDNA sequence. Entire multi-cellular living organism.
Primary Mechanism Insertion into bacterial plasmid vector followed by host division. Somatic Cell Nuclear Transfer (SCNT) into enucleated ovum.
Example Cloning human insulin gene in E. coli. Dolly the sheep (1996) cloned from adult mammary gland nucleus.
