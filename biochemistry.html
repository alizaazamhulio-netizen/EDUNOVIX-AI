TOPIC 01

What is Biochemistry & Elements of Life

Biochemistry investigates the chemical substances, physical properties, and molecular reactions governing all living systems.

⚛️ The CHNOPS Matrix (98% of Biomass)

Out of 92 naturally occurring elements, 16 bio-elements are essential for living organisms. Six foundational elements constitute ~98% of total human biomass:

O Oxygen 65%
C Carbon 18%
H Hydrogen 10%
N Nitrogen 3%
Ca Calcium 2%
P Phosphorus 1%
Trace Bio-Elements (Remaining ~2%): K (0.35%), S (0.25%), Cl (0.15%), Na (0.15%), Mg (0.05%), Fe (0.004%), plus Cu, Mn, Zn, I.

💎 Carbon: The Organic Backbone

Carbon is tetravalent (atomic number 6, valency 4). It forms four stable covalent bonds in a 3D tetrahedral geometry (109.5° bond angle), enabling limitless structural diversity: linear chains, branched skeletons, and closed aromatic rings.

Tetrahedral Symmetry of Carbon with 4 Functional Groups

PLANTS

STARCH

  • Role: Primary carbohydrate energy storage in plants (grains, tubers, seeds).
  • Structure:
    • Amylose (10-20%): Unbranched helical α-1,4 bonds (soluble in hot water).
    • Amylopectin (80-90%): Branched α-1,4 with α-1,6 branch points every 24-30 residues.
  • Iodine Test: Gives intense Blue/Black Color.
  • Digestion: Hydrolyzed by salivary & pancreatic α-Amylase.
🧬
ANIMALS

GLYCOGEN

  • Role: Primary carbohydrate storage in animals ("Animal Starch"), stored in Liver & Skeletal Muscles.
  • Structure: Polymer of α-D-glucose, extensively branched (α-1,6 branch every 8-12 residues). Highly compact for rapid mobilization.
  • Iodine Test: Gives distinct Red / Brown / Violet Color.
  • Hormonal Control: Insulin stimulates glycogenesis; Glucagon & Epinephrine stimulate glycogenolysis.
🌿
PLANT CELL WALL

CELLULOSE

  • Role: Most abundant biological polymer on Earth! Provides structural rigidity to plant cell walls and wood fibers.
  • Structure: Linear, straight, unbranched chains of β-D-glucose linked via β-1,4-glycosidic bonds, bound by extensive cross-linking hydrogen bonds into microfibrils.
  • Iodine Test: Gives No Color change with iodine.
  • MDCAT Trap: Humans cannot digest cellulose because human digestive juices lack Cellulase. Acts as essential dietary fiber (roughage).

SOLID AT ROOM TEMP (FATS)

🧈 Saturated Fatty Acids

No double bonds ($C-C$ single bonds only). Carbon chain is completely saturated with hydrogen atoms. Straight hydrocarbon tails pack tightly together with strong van der Waals forces, resulting in high melting points (solid fats e.g. Palmitic acid $C_{16}$, Stearic acid $C_{18}$, Butter, Ghee).

Straight Zig-Zag Chain (Dense Packing)
LIQUID AT ROOM TEMP (OILS)

🫒 Unsaturated Fatty Acids

Contain one or more carbon-carbon double bonds ($C=C$) in cis-conformation. Double bonds introduce rigid kinks / bends preventing tight molecular packing. Possess lower melting points (liquid oils e.g. Oleic acid $18:1$, Linoleic acid $18:2$, Olive oil, Fish oil).

Rigid Double-Bond Kink (Loose Packing & Fluidity)
TOPIC 06 • STRUCTURAL BIOLOGY

Proteins: Amino Acids & 4 Levels of Structural Folding

Polypeptide macromolecules composed of 20 standard L-α-amino acids.

Structure of an Alpha-Amino Acid

Every amino acid possesses a central tetrahedral Alpha-Carbon ($C_\alpha$) bonded to four distinct chemical groups:

  • Amino Group ($-NH_2$): Basic, accepts protons to form $-NH_3^+$ at physiological pH 7.4.
  • Carboxyl Group ($-COOH$): Acidic, donates protons to form $-COO^-$.
  • Hydrogen Atom ($-H$): Standard fourth valency.
  • Variable R-Group (Side Chain): Dictates chemical specificity (non-polar, polar, acidic, basic). Glycine has $R=-H$ (optically inactive); Alanine has $R=-CH_3$.
Zwitterion: Dipolar ion bearing simultaneous positive and negative charges at isoelectric pH.
LEVEL 1
📿

Primary Structure

Peptide Bonds (-CO-NH-)

The linear sequence of amino acids determined genetically by mRNA codons. Maintained exclusively by covalent peptide bonds.

Example: Insulin (51 amino acids, Frederick Sanger, 1953)
LEVEL 2
🌀

Secondary Structure

Backbone Hydrogen Bonds

Local regular folding: α-Helix (spiral with 3.6 amino acids/turn, e.g. Keratin) and β-Pleated Sheet (parallel or anti-parallel, e.g. Silk fibroin).

Linus Pauling & Robert Corey (1951)
LEVEL 3
🧶

Tertiary Structure

R-Group Interactions

Overall compact 3D globular conformation formed by bending and folding of secondary structures. Stabilized by Hydrogen bonds, Ionic bonds, Hydrophobic interactions, and covalent Disulfide bridges (-S-S-).

Example: Myoglobin (single chain oxygen store in muscle)
LEVEL 4
🧩

Quaternary Structure

Multi-Subunit Assembly

Spatial arrangement of two or more independent polypeptide chains (protomers/subunits) clustered together non-covalently into a functional oligomeric protein.

Example: Hemoglobin (4 subunits: 2α + 2β chains, $574\text{ AAs}$, binds $4\text{ O}_2$)

🔒 Lock & Key Model

Proposes that the enzyme's active site has a rigid, pre-existing structural template perfectly complementary to the substrate, exactly like a key fitting into a lock. No conformational change occurs during substrate entry.

Limitation: Fails to explain non-competitive inhibition or transition state stabilization.
DANIEL KOSHLAND (1958)

🧤 Induced Fit Model

Proposes that the active site is flexible, not rigid. When the substrate approaches, it induces a subtle conformational adjustment in the active site (like a hand slipping into a flexible glove) to align catalytic groups for maximal transition-state strain.

MDCAT Standard: Universally accepted model of modern enzymology.

TOPIC 09 • GENETIC CODE

Nucleic Acids: DNA vs RNA Molecular Comparison

Polymers of nucleotides storing genetic instructions and directing protein synthesis.

DOUBLE STRANDED (B-DNA HELIX)

🧬 Deoxyribonucleic Acid (DNA)

  • Sugar: 2-Deoxyribose (Lacks oxygen atom at C-2' position, $C_5H_{10}O_4$).
  • Nitrogenous Bases:
    • Purines: Adenine (A), Guanine (G)
    • Pyrimidines: Thymine (T), Cytosine (C)
  • Chargaff's Rule: $A = T$ (2 Hydrogen bonds) and $G \equiv C$ (3 Hydrogen bonds). Purines = Pyrimidines ($A+G = T+C$).
  • Geometry: Right-handed double helix, antiparallel strands ($5' \rightarrow 3'$ and $3' \rightarrow 5'$), pitch $3.4\text{ nm}$ ($10\text{ base pairs/turn}$), diameter $2.0\text{ nm}$.
  • Location: Nucleus, Mitochondria, and Chloroplasts.
SINGLE STRANDED TRANSCRIPTS

🧬 Ribonucleic Acid (RNA)

  • Sugar: D-Ribose (Has standard $-OH$ at C-2', $C_5H_{10}O_5$).
  • Nitrogenous Bases:
    • Purines: Adenine (A), Guanine (G)
    • Pyrimidines: Uracil (U) [replaces Thymine], Cytosine (C)
  • Chargaff's Rule: Does NOT apply to single-stranded RNA ($A \neq U, G \neq C$).
  • Three Functional Classes:
    • mRNA (3-5%): Carries genetic message from DNA to ribosome.
    • tRNA (10-20%): Clover-leaf shape, carries specific amino acids.
    • rRNA (80%): Most abundant! Structural component of ribosomes.

🔬 Deoxyribose vs Ribose Sugar Difference

2-Deoxy-D-Ribose (DNA)

Contains $-H$ at C-2' (Increases chemical stability for long-term genome storage)

D-Ribose (RNA)

Contains $-OH$ at C-2' (Makes RNA more reactive and prone to alkaline hydrolysis)
TOPIC 10 • MEDIUM OF LIFE

Water: The Dipolar Solvent of Life

Accounts for 65–70% of mammalian body weight (up to 85% in brain cells, 20% in bone cells).

💧 Dipolar Charge Distribution & H-Bonds

Water is an asymmetric bent molecule ($104.5^\circ$ bond angle). Oxygen has higher electronegativity than hydrogen, creating partial negative charge ($\delta^-$) on Oxygen and partial positive charges ($\delta^+$) on Hydrogens.

Bent Dipole Geometry ($104.5^\circ$) Forming up to 4 Hydrogen Bonds per Molecule

🌡️ Physiological Functions in MDCAT

  • Universal Solvent: Forms hydration shells around polar molecules and electrolytes. Non-polar substances are driven together by hydrophobic interactions.
  • High Specific Heat Capacity ($1\text{ cal/g}^\circ\text{C}$): Absorbs huge thermal energy with minimal temperature change, serving as a biological temperature buffer.
  • High Heat of Vaporization ($574\text{ kcal/kg}$ at $37^\circ\text{C}$): Provides potent evaporative cooling (e.g. sweating in humans, transpiration in plants).
  • Cohesion & Adhesion: Hydrogen bonding generates high surface tension and pulls sap through xylem capillaries during transpiration stream.

HIGH-YIELD FACTS

  • Non-reducing Sugar: Sucrose (table sugar) is non-reducing because its both anomeric carbons ($C_1$ of glucose and $C_2$ of fructose) are engaged in the glycosidic bond.
  • Cellulose Bond: $\beta\text{-1,4-glycosidic linkage}$; requires cellulase enzyme which is absent in human digestive tract.
  • Enzyme Kinetics: Enzymes lower activation energy ($E_a$) without altering equilibrium constant ($K_{eq}$) or standard free energy change ($\Delta G$).
  • Hemoglobin: $574\text{ amino acids}$, $4\text{ subunits}$ ($2\alpha + 2\beta$), molecular weight $\sim 64,500\text{ Da}$, carries $4\text{ molecules of } O_2$.
⚠️

COMMON TRAPS

  • Trap 1: Starch is storage in plants; Glycogen is storage in animals. Do NOT mix these on test day!
  • Trap 2: Denaturation breaks secondary, tertiary, and quaternary bonds, but leaves primary peptide bonds intact.
  • Trap 3: Saturated fatty acids have single bonds only (solid fats); unsaturated fatty acids have one or more double bonds (liquid oils).
  • Trap 4: RNA contains Uracil (U) instead of Thymine (T), and has Ribose with $-OH$ at $C_2'$, not Deoxyribose.
📌

MUST REMEMBER

  • Carbohydrate Ratio: $H:O = 2:1$ (same as water).
  • ATP Hydrolysis: $ATP + H_2O \rightarrow ADP + P_i + 7.3\text{ kcal/mol}$ ($30.5\text{ kJ/mol}$).
  • Triglyceride Synthesis: $1\text{ Glycerol} + 3\text{ Fatty Acids} \rightarrow 1\text{ Triglyceride} + 3\text{ } H_2O$.
  • Chargaff's Base Ratio: $A+G = T+C$ (Purines = Pyrimidines) in double-stranded DNA.
⚡

RAPID REVISION

  • Carbohydrates: Glycosidic bonds
  • Proteins: Peptide bonds
  • Lipids: Ester bonds
  • Nucleic Acids: 3',5'-Phosphodiester bonds
  • Secondary Protein: Backbone Hydrogen bonds
  • Tertiary Protein: R-group interactions + Disulfide bridges
