Step 1: Understand & Highlight Key Details

Select a highlighter tool below, then click the words in the word problem to categorize them.

Marcus has a budget of $50. He buys 3 sketchbooks for $8 each. The art store was located 2 miles away. How much money does he have remaining?

Analysis Feedback

Click words in the story to classify Given facts, Find target, and Distractors.
Key Questions to Ask:
  • What numbers are given? ($50 budget, 3 books @ $8)
  • What is the unknown? (Remaining balance)
  • What is irrelevant? (Store distance of 2 miles)

Step 2: Analyze & Organize Information

Separate what is known from what is unknown to structure the problem clearly.

📋 Given Information
  • Initial Budget = $50.00
  • Quantity of Sketchbooks = 3
  • Price per Sketchbook = $8.00 each
  • Distance = 2 miles (Distractor)
🎯 Target to Find
Remaining money (in dollars $) after purchasing all 3 sketchbooks

Step 4: Solve with Step-by-Step Rigor

1
Sub-Calculation 1: Total Cost of Sketchbooks

Multiply quantity by unit price:

3 × $8 = $24
2
Sub-Calculation 2: Remaining Balance

Subtract total cost from initial budget:

$50 − $24 = $26
3
Final Formulated Answer

State the numerical result clearly with unit notation:

Marcus has $26 remaining.

Step 5: Check & Verify the Solution

Ensure your solution passes all sanity checks before concluding:

✓
Reasonability Check: $26 remaining is less than the $50 starting budget, which makes sense after spending $24.
✓
Unit Verification: The answer is expressed in dollars ($), matching the original monetary question.
✓
Reverse Check: Spent ($24) + Remaining ($26) = Starting Budget ($50). Equation holds perfectly.

Worked Examples Archive

Examine 8 realistic problems with comprehensive step-by-step reveal breakdowns.

Shopping
A student buys 3 notebooks for $4 each and a pen for $2. How much does the student spend altogether?
Equation: (3 × $4) + $2
Step 1: 3 × 4 = $12 (notebooks)
Step 2: $12 + $2 = $14 (total spent)
Final Answer: $14.00
Change
Sara has $50. She buys a book for $18 and a calculator for $15. How much money is left?
Equation: $50 − ($18 + $15)
Step 1: Total spent = 18 + 15 = $33
Step 2: 50 − 33 = $17 left
Final Answer: $17.00
Equal Sharing
48 students are divided equally into 6 groups. How many students are in each group?
Equation: 48 ÷ 6
Calculation: 48 ÷ 6 = 8
Final Answer: 8 students per group
Distance
A car travels at 60 km/h for 3 hours. How far does it travel?
Formula: Distance = Speed × Time
Calculation: 60 × 3 = 180 km
Final Answer: 180 km
Percentage
A bag costs $80 and has a 25% discount. What is the final price?
Discount: $80 × 0.25 = $20
Final Price: $80 − $20 = $60
Final Answer: $60.00
Ratio
The ratio of boys to girls is 3:5. If there are 15 boys, how many girls are there?
1 Part: 15 boys ÷ 3 = 5 per part
Girls: 5 parts × 5 = 25 girls
Final Answer: 25 girls
Age
Ali is 12 years old. His sister is 4 years older. How old will his sister be after 5 years?
Sister Now: 12 + 4 = 16 years old
After 5 Years: 16 + 5 = 21 years old
Final Answer: 21 years old
Multi-Step
A bakery makes 120 muffins at a cost of $0.50 each. They sell 100 muffins at $1.50 each and give away the rest for free. What is their profit?
Cost: 120 × $0.50 = $60.00
Revenue: 100 × $1.50 = $150.00
Profit: $150 − $60 = $90.00
Final Answer: $90.00 profit

Modeling Tips

Word problems express mathematical relationships in words. Building an equation requires matching the story flow:

  • Left Hand Side: Represents the operations performed on the given quantities.
  • Equal Sign (=): Represents "results in", "is", or "leaves".
  • Right Hand Side: The computed value answering the prompt.

Formula Toolkit & Notes

Keep essential mathematical relationships and your personal problem-solving takeaways readily available.

Distance, Speed & Time
Distance = Speed × Time
Speed = Distance ÷ Time
Time = Distance ÷ Speed
Percentage & Discounts
Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100
Discount = Price × Rate
Final = Price − Discount
Commerce & Profit
Profit = Selling Price − Cost
Total Revenue = Qty × Unit Price
Change = Paid − Total Cost
Ratios & Work Rates
1 Part = Total ÷ (a + b)
Combined Rate = 1/A + 1/B
Together Time = 1 ÷ Rate
