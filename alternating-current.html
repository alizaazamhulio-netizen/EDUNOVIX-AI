MODULE 01 • FUNDAMENTALS & WAVEFORMS

1. What is Alternating Current & Sinusoidal AC Voltage?

Alternating Current (AC) is an electric current whose magnitude and direction change periodically with time. In DC, charges flow in one unidirectional path; in AC, charges oscillate back and forth continuously.

Waveform Controls & Equations

i = I₀ sin(ωt)
V = V₀ sin(ωt)
ω = 2πf = 2π/T

MDCAT Instantaneous Value Trap ⭐
At $t = T/4$ ($90^\circ$), $i = I_0 \sin(\pi/2) = +I_0$ (Peak).
At $t = T/12$ ($30^\circ$), $i = I_0 \sin(30^\circ) = 0.5 I_0$ (Half of Peak value).
At $t = T/8$ ($45^\circ$), $i = I_0 \sin(45^\circ) = \frac{I_0}{\sqrt{2}} = I_{rms}$!

MODULE 04 • EFFECTIVE VALUE (RMS)

4. RMS (Root Mean Square) Value & Heating Effect ⭐⭐⭐

RMS value is the effective value of alternating current. It represents the value of steady DC current that would produce the same heating effect in a given resistor over the same time interval!

Peak vs RMS Level Comparison

I_rms = I₀ / √2 ≈ 0.707 I₀
Peak Voltage (V₀)
311.1 V
RMS Voltage (V_rms)
220.0 V

Equivalent DC Heating Experiment

JOULE'S LAW

Since power dissipated is $P = i^2(t) R$, heat produced in AC depends on the mean of $i^2$. Because the average value of $\sin^2(\omega t)$ over one complete cycle is $\frac{1}{2}$, we get:

P_avg = I²_rms × R = (I₀ / √2)² × R = ½ I₀² R
Thus, a 220V AC source produces the exact same heat in an electric heater as a 220V steady DC battery!
311.1 V
MDCAT High-Yield Meter Fact
Standard AC voltmeters and ammeters (moving-iron or hot-wire types) are calibrated to measure RMS values, not peak or average values!
MODULE 05 • COMPARISON

5. AC vs DC Direct Comparison Lab

Watch charge carriers (electrons) in action! In DC, electrons drift steadily in one direction. In AC, electrons oscillate back and forth in place without any net unidirectional migration.

DIRECT CURRENT (DC)

f = 0 Hz | Constant Unidirectional
• Electrons drift steadily from negative terminal to positive terminal.
• Voltage and current magnitude are constant over time.
• Cannot be stepped up or down using ordinary transformers.

ALTERNATING CURRENT (AC)

f = 50 Hz | Sinusoidal Reversing
• Electrons oscillate symmetrically about mean positions.
• Voltage and current continuously vary between $+V_0$ and $-V_0$.
• Easily stepped up or down for high-voltage, low-loss transmission.
Feature Alternating Current (AC) Direct Current (DC)
Direction Reverses direction periodically Flows in one constant direction
Magnitude Continuously varies with time Essentially constant
Frequency (f) Non-zero (50 Hz / 60 Hz) Zero (0 Hz)
Transformer Action Easily transformed via Mutual Induction Cannot be stepped up/down with ordinary transformer
Capacitor Opposition Offers finite reactance $X_C = 1/(2\pi f C)$ Offers infinite resistance ($X_C \to \infty$, blocks DC)

MODULE 08 • SERIES RESONANCE ⭐⭐⭐

8. Series Electrical Resonance Simulator

Resonance occurs when $X_L = X_C$. At this unique resonant frequency ($f_0$), the net reactance cancels out to zero, impedance drops to its absolute minimum ($Z = R$), and current reaches its maximum peak!

MDCAT Armature Flux vs EMF Orientation ⭐
• When coil is perpendicular to magnetic field: Flux is MAXIMUM ($\Phi = BA$), but $\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = 0 \implies \text{EMF} = 0$.
• When coil is parallel to magnetic field: Flux is ZERO ($\Phi = 0$), but flux changes fastest $\frac{d\Phi}{dt} = \text{max} \implies \text{EMF} = E_0$ (MAXIMUM)!

Why Ordinary Transformers Fail on Steady DC?
For mutual induction, changing magnetic flux is required ($\frac{d\Phi}{dt} \neq 0$). In steady DC, flux is constant $\implies \frac{d\Phi}{dt} = 0 \implies$ Secondary voltage is ZERO. Furthermore, the low DC resistance of primary coil will cause excessive current and burn out the winding!
