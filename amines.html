What Are Amines?

Organic derivatives of Ammonia ($NH_3$) with Nitrogen-centered chemistry

Core Definition & Principles

Amines are organic derivatives of ammonia ($NH_3$) in which one, two, or all three hydrogen atoms have been replaced by alkyl (aliphatic) or aryl (aromatic) hydrocarbon groups.

Ammonia: $NH_3$
→
Primary (1°): $R-NH_2$
→
Secondary (2°): $R_2-NH$
→
Tertiary (3°): $R_3-N$
⭐
The Nitrogen Lone Pair Rule: In all amines, the nitrogen atom contains three covalent sigma bonds and one unshared lone pair of electrons ($:N$). This unshared pair governs nearly all chemical properties: Lewis basicity, nucleophilic attacks, proton acceptance, and coordination.

Amine Classification Lab ⭐⭐⭐

Differentiate 1°, 2°, 3° & Aromatic Amines with the MDCAT Golden Rule

🧠
CRITICAL MDCAT TRICK & RULE:

In Alcohols and Alkyl Halides, you classify by looking at the carbon attached to $-OH$ or $-X$ (e.g. is the carbon 1°, 2°, or 3°?).
FOR AMINES, THIS IS DIFFERENT! You do NOT count total carbons in the molecule. You count the number of carbon groups DIRECTLY ATTACHED to the NITROGEN atom itself!

$(CH_3)_3C-NH_2$ (tert-Butylamine) → PRIMARY (1°) AMINE (Because only 1 carbon is attached directly to N!)
$C_6H_5-CH_2-NH_2$ (Benzylamine) → PRIMARY (1°) ALIPHATIC AMINE (Nitrogen is bonded to $CH_2$ carbon, NOT directly to aromatic ring!)

Molecular Structure & Geometry

Hybridization, Trigonal Pyramidal Shape & Bond Angle Deviations

Orbital Hybridization in Amines

  • Hybridization of Nitrogen: In amines, the central nitrogen atom has 4 electron pairs (3 bond pairs + 1 lone pair). Hence, Nitrogen is $sp^3$ hybridized.
  • Electronic vs Molecular Geometry: The electronic geometry is tetrahedral, but because one position is occupied by a non-bonding lone pair, the observed molecular geometry is trigonal pyramidal.
  • Bond Angle ($C-N-H$ or $C-N-C$): In ideal tetrahedral methane ($CH_4$), the angle is $109.5^\circ$. In ammonia ($NH_3$), lone pair–bond pair repulsion contracts it to $107^\circ$. In trimethylamine $(CH_3)_3N$, steric repulsion of bulky methyl groups opens the angle to approximately $108^\circ$.
  • Pyramidal Inversion (Umbrella Flipping): At room temperature, simple tertiary amines undergo rapid nitrogen inversion like an umbrella turning inside out in the wind ($\Delta G^\ddagger \sim 25\text{ kJ/mol}$), making enantiomers of chiral trivalent nitrogen amines non-resolvable at room temperature.

The Nitrogen Lone Pair & Lewis Basicity ⭐⭐⭐

Electron donation mechanics, coordinate bonding, and nucleophilic centers

Why Is the Lone Pair So Reactive?

According to Lewis Acid-Base Theory:

Lewis Base Definition

A Lewis Base is any chemical species that possesses a non-bonding pair of electrons which it can donate to form a coordinate covalent (dative) bond with an electron-deficient species (Lewis acid).

Because nitrogen has an unshared electron pair in an $sp^3$ orbital and relatively moderate electronegativity (3.0 vs Oxygen 3.5), it readily donates this electron pair to:

  • Protons ($H^+$): Acting as a Brønsted-Lowry & Lewis Base $\rightarrow RNH_2 + H^+ \rightarrow RNH_3^+$
  • Electrophiles (Alkyl halides, Carbonyls): Acting as a Nucleophile $\rightarrow RNH_2 + R'-X \rightarrow R-NH(R') + HX$
  • Transition Metal Cations: Acting as a ligand (e.g. $[Cu(NH_3)_4]^{2+}$ or amine coordination complexes).

Basicity of Aliphatic Amines ⭐⭐⭐

Inductive effect (+I), Solvation energy, Steric hindrance, and Medium dependence

Aqueous Dissociation & $pK_b$

When an amine dissolves in water, it abstracts a proton from $H_2O$, generating hydroxide ions ($OH^-$):

$$R-NH_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons R-NH_3^+ + OH^-$$
Base dissociation constant: $K_b = \frac{[RNH_3^+][OH^-]}{[RNH_2]}$ • Higher $K_b$ = Lower $pK_b$ = Stronger Base

The Three Competing Factors

1
Inductive Effect (+I): Alkyl groups release electron density toward nitrogen, increasing electron density on $:N$ and stabilizing the positive charge on $RNH_3^+$.
Order favored: $3^\circ > 2^\circ > 1^\circ > NH_3$
2
Solvation (Hydration): The substituted ammonium cation is stabilized in water through hydrogen bonding ($RNH_3^+$ has 3 H's, $R_2NH_2^+$ has 2 H's, $R_3NH^+$ has only 1 H).
Order favored: $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$
3
Steric Hindrance: Bulky alkyl groups crowd the nitrogen atom, hindering incoming proton attack and hydration shells.
Order favored: $1^\circ > 2^\circ > 3^\circ$

Aniline & Resonance Delocalization ⭐⭐⭐

Why aromatic amines are dramatically weaker bases than aliphatic amines

Resonance Effect in Benzenamine ($C_6H_5NH_2$)

Aniline ($pK_b \approx 9.4$) is millions of times weaker as a base than methylamine ($pK_b \approx 3.36$) and ammonia ($pK_b \approx 4.75$).

Nitrogen $:N$ Lone Pair → Delocalized into Benzene $\pi$-system → Less Available for Proton ($H^+$) → Dramatically Lower Basicity

Anilinium Ion Instability: If aniline accepts a proton, it forms the anilinium cation ($C_6H_5NH_3^+$), which has only 2 resonance contributors (Kekulé structures of the ring), while neutral aniline has 5 resonance contributors. Therefore, protonation is thermodynamically disfavored!

Aliphatic ($CH_3NH_2$) $pK_b = 3.36$ +I effect, lone pair localized on N
>
Ammonia ($NH_3$) $pK_b = 4.75$ No alkyl, lone pair localized
>
Aniline ($C_6H_5NH_2$) $pK_b = 9.40$ Resonance delocalized into ring

Physical Properties of Amines

Intermolecular Hydrogen Bonding, Boiling Points, and Water Solubility

💧

Hydrogen Bonding

Primary (1°) and Secondary (2°) amines possess polar $N-H$ bonds ($N^{\delta-}-H^{\delta+}$) and form intermolecular hydrogen bonds.

H-Bond Capacity: 1° (2 donors) > 2° (1 donor) > 3° (0 donors)
Tertiary amines have no $N-H$ bond, so they cannot form H-bonds with themselves!
🔥

Boiling Points

For isomeric amines of the same molecular formula ($C_4H_{11}N$):

1° Butylamine:
77.8°C
2° Diethylamine:
56.3°C
3° Dimethylethylamine:
37.5°C
Amines have lower B.P. than comparable Alcohols because $N-H$ is less polar than $O-H$.
🧪

Water Solubility

Lower aliphatic amines ($C_1-C_3$) are completely miscible with water because nitrogen accepts and donates H-bonds with $H_2O$.

Solubility Trend: Decreases as Alkyl chain grows ($C_6+$ insoluble)
Amine salts ($RNH_3^+Cl^-$) are highly water-soluble ionic solids!

Reactions with Acids & Nucleophilicity

Salt Formation, Acylation, Alkylation & Amide Synthesis

1. Reaction with Mineral Acids (Ammonium Salts)

Being bases, amines react exothermically with mineral acids ($HCl, H_2SO_4, HNO_3$) to form crystalline, water-soluble alkylammonium salts:

$$CH_3NH_2 + HCl \rightarrow \underset{\text{Methylammonium chloride (Ionic Salt)}}{CH_3NH_3^+ Cl^-}$$

Acid-Base Extraction Application: Insoluble organic amines can be extracted into an aqueous acid layer as $RNH_3^+Cl^-$, and regenerated by adding strong base ($NaOH$): $$RNH_3^+Cl^- + NaOH \rightarrow RNH_2\downarrow + NaCl + H_2O$$

2. Acylation (Reaction with Acyl Chlorides)

1° and 2° amines attack acid chlorides or anhydrides via nucleophilic acyl substitution to form N-substituted amides:

$$CH_3COCl + C_6H_5NH_2 \xrightarrow{\text{Base}} \underset{\text{Acetanilide (Amide)}}{C_6H_5NHCOCH_3} + HCl$$
MDCAT Protection Strategy: Aniline's $-NH_2$ group is strongly activating and undergoes polyhalogenation. Acetylation to acetanilide tempers activating power, allowing monobromination at the para position!

Nitrous Acid ($HNO_2$) Differential Lab ⭐⭐⭐

How 1° Aliphatic, 1° Aromatic, 2°, and 3° Amines behave with in-situ $HNO_2$

💡
IN SITU REAGENT GENERATION: Nitrous acid is unstable and prepared in situ by reacting Sodium Nitrite with Hydrochloric Acid: NaNO₂ + HCl → HNO₂ + NaCl
STARTING REAGENT

Amine Class + $NaNO_2 / HCl$

Click any amine class below to observe the distinct chemical behavior and gas evolution:

1° Aliphatic Amine

$R-NH_2$ (e.g. $CH_3CH_2NH_2$)

Rapid Decomposition

Forms an unstable diazonium intermediate that decomposes immediately at room temperature:

$R-NH_2 + HNO_2 \rightarrow [R-N_2^+] \rightarrow R-OH + \mathbf{N_2\uparrow} + H_2O$
Observation: Brisk effervescence of odorless, colorless $N_2$ gas + Alcohol formed.
1° Aromatic Amine

$Ar-NH_2$ (Aniline, $C_6H_5NH_2$)

Stable Diazotization (0–5°C)

Forms a resonance-stabilized Arenediazonium Salt:

$C_6H_5NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{0-5^\circ C} \mathbf{C_6H_5N_2^+ Cl^-} + NaCl + 2H_2O$
Observation: Clear cold solution of Benzenediazonium chloride.
2° Amines (Aliph / Arom)

$R_2NH$ (e.g. $(CH_3)_2NH$)

Yellow Oily Layer

Undergoes N-nitrosation to form insoluble, yellow, carcinogenic N-Nitrosoamines:

$R_2NH + HONO \rightarrow \mathbf{R_2N-N=O} \text{ (yellow oil)} + H_2O$
Observation: Yellow oily liquid separates (no $N_2$ gas evolved!).
3° Amines (Aliphatic)

$R_3N$ (e.g. $(CH_3)_3N$)

Soluble Nitrite Salt

No H on nitrogen to lose; simply acts as base to form water-soluble trialkylammonium nitrite:

$R_3N + HNO_2 \rightleftharpoons \mathbf{[R_3NH]^+ NO_2^-} \text{ (soluble)}$
Observation: Dissolves into a clear solution; upon warming, decomposes into nitrosoamine + alcohol.

Diazotization Lab (0–5°C) ⭐⭐⭐

The Crown Jewel of Nitrogen Organic Chemistry for MDCAT

What is Diazotization?

Diazotization is the process of converting a primary aromatic amine ($Ar-NH_2$) into an arenediazonium salt ($Ar-N_2^+ X^-$) by reaction with cold nitrous acid ($NaNO_2 + HCl$) at 0°C to 5°C.

$$C_6H_5NH_2 + NaNO_2 + 2HCl \xrightarrow{\mathbf{0-5^\circ C \text{ (Ice Bath)}}} C_6H_5N_2^+ Cl^- + NaCl + 2H_2O$$
Aniline (Base) + Sodium Nitrite + HCl → Benzenediazonium Chloride

Why Is Strict Ice-Cold Temperature (0–5°C) Mandatory?

  • Aryl Diazonium Salts are Thermally Labile: Above $5^\circ C$ or $10^\circ C$, the diazonium group $-N_2^+$ rapidly hydrolyzes with water, releasing nitrogen gas and producing Phenol and gummy brown resinous tars: $$C_6H_5N_2^+Cl^- + H_2O \xrightarrow{> 10^\circ C} C_6H_5OH + N_2\uparrow + HCl$$
  • Preserving the Intermediate: Diazonium salts are rarely isolated dry because solid diazonium salts are explosive shock-sensitive powders! They are always kept in cold aqueous solution and used immediately.

Azo Coupling Lab (Dye Synthesis) ⭐⭐

Electrophilic Aromatic Substitution yielding vibrant $-N=N-$ Azo Chromophores

What is Azo Coupling?

Arenediazonium cations act as relatively weak electrophiles and attack electron-rich activated aromatic rings (such as Phenols and Aromatic Amines) at the para-position (or ortho if para is blocked) to form intensely colored Azo Compounds ($Ar-N=N-Ar'$).

The Azo Chromophore Group
$-N=N-$

The extended conjugation between both aromatic rings through the $-N=N-$ bridge lowers the HOMO-LUMO $\pi \rightarrow \pi^*$ gap, allowing absorption of visible light and reflecting brilliant orange, yellow, and red colors!

Crucial pH Conditions for Coupling

Coupling Partner Medium Required Optimal pH Why This pH?
Phenol ($C_6H_5OH$) Mildly Alkaline $pH \approx 9 - 10$ Generates strongly activated phenoxide ion ($C_6H_5O^-$).
Aniline ($C_6H_5NH_2$) Mildly Acidic $pH \approx 4 - 5$ Prevents protonation to deactivating anilinium while activating coupling.
