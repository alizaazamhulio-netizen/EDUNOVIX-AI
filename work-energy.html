01

What Does "Work" Really Mean?

🧑‍🎓
📦
→

A student pushes a box across the floor. The box moves in the direction of the push.

YES! Physics work IS being done because:

  • A force is applied to the box
  • The box has a displacement in the direction of force
  • Work = Force × Displacement × cos θ

The Work Formula

W = F · d · cos θ
W Work Joules (J)
F Applied Force Newtons (N)
d Displacement Metres (m)
θ Angle between F and d Degrees (°)

θ is the angle between the force direction and the displacement direction

💡
Key Insight: Only the component of force parallel to displacement does work. That's why we multiply by cos θ — it extracts only the useful component.

03

Three Types of Work

✅

Positive Work

Force and displacement point in the same general direction (0° ≤ θ < 90°)

W = Fd cos θ → W > 0

Example: Pushing a box forward; it accelerates and gains kinetic energy.

⭕

Zero Work

Force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°)

cos 90° = 0 → W = 0

Example: Carrying a bag horizontally; you exert upward force but move sideways.

❌

Negative Work

Force opposes displacement (90° < θ ≤ 180°)

cos 180° = −1 → W < 0

Example: Friction acts backward on a sliding box, removing kinetic energy.

Energy — The Capacity to Do Work

"Energy is the capacity of a system to do work."

Energy is not a substance you can see or touch. It is a measure of ability — the ability to cause change, move objects, or produce heat.

SI Unit: Joule (J) 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s²

Work as Energy Transfer

Kinetic Energy

Energy an object possesses because it is moving. Change the mass and velocity below — watch what happens to KE!

Mass (m)
5 kg
Velocity (v)
4 m/s
KE
40 J
KE = ½ × 5 × 4² = 40 J
KE ∝ m Double mass → KE doubles
KE ∝ v² Double v → KE × 4

KE = ½mv²

The squared relationship with velocity is why speed matters so much in road accidents — doubling speed quadruples the kinetic energy!

06

Potential Energy

Energy stored because of an object's position. Raise the object higher — it stores more energy, ready to release.

PE = 3 × 9.8 × 5 = 147 J

As height increases, PE increases linearly — because PE = mgh.

When the object falls, PE converts to KE. At the bottom, all PE has become KE (ignoring air resistance).

PE = mgh

Energy Transformation — The Roller Coaster

Watch how PE and KE exchange as the ball rolls. Total mechanical energy stays constant (no friction).

PE 0 J
KE 0 J
Total 0 J
KEi + PEi = KEf + PEf

Total mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces act.

Energy Cannot Disappear

The Law of Conservation of Energy is one of the most fundamental laws in physics. Energy may change form — mechanical to thermal, chemical to electrical — but the total amount in a closed system never changes.

09

Work-Energy Theorem

Wnet = ΔKE = KEf − KEi

The net work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy. Work and KE are two sides of the same coin.

Work Done: 0 J
ΔKE: 0 J
Final Velocity: 0 m/s

Worked Examples

Example 1: Find KE after work is done + Show Solution
Given: m = 5 kg, initial v = 2 m/s, Work done = 100 J
Required: Final velocity
Formula: Wnet = ½mvf² − ½mvi²
Substitution: 100 = ½(5)vf² − ½(5)(4)
Calculation: 100 = 2.5vf² − 10 → vf² = 44 → vf ≈ 6.63 m/s
Answer: 6.63 m/s
Example 2: Work done against friction + Show Solution
Given: m = 10 kg, vi = 8 m/s, vf = 0, friction stops the object
Required: Work done by friction
Formula: W = ΔKE = KEf − KEi
Substitution: W = 0 − ½(10)(8²)
Calculation: W = −320 J
Answer: −320 J (negative because friction opposes motion)
Example 3: Net work with multiple forces + Show Solution
Given: Push force = 50 N, friction = 10 N, d = 4 m, m = 8 kg, vi = 0
Required: Final KE and velocity
Formula: Wnet = Fnet × d = ΔKE
Substitution: Wnet = (50−10) × 4 = 160 J
Calculation: ½(8)vf² = 160 → vf² = 40 → vf ≈ 6.32 m/s
Answer: KE = 160 J, vf ≈ 6.32 m/s

11

Efficiency

Real machines always waste some energy. Efficiency tells us how much of the input energy is actually useful.

Useful Output 70 J
Wasted Energy 30 J
η = 70 / 100 × 100 = 70%
η = (Useful Output / Input Energy) × 100%

For real machines, η < 100% because energy is lost to friction, heat, and sound.

⚠ Elastic limit is approximately at x = 0.40 m for this spring. Beyond this, Hooke's Law no longer applies.

What Does the Spring Constant (k) Mean?

k measures the stiffness of a spring. A large k means the spring is stiff — it takes more force to stretch it by the same amount. A small k means the spring is flexible.

High k: Stiff Spring 🔩
e.g. k = 1000 N/m
Low k: Soft Spring 🌀
e.g. k = 50 N/m

MDCAT Concept Traps

These are the most common mistakes students make. Read each one carefully.

01
🚫

Force ≠ Work (alone)

Force alone does not guarantee work. If there is no displacement (d = 0), work is zero — even if you push with all your strength.

02
⊥

Force can act, Work can be zero

If force is perpendicular to displacement (θ = 90°), cos 90° = 0, so W = 0. Example: centripetal force in circular motion.

03
v²

KE depends on v², not v

If velocity doubles, KE becomes four times (not twice). This is why car crash severity increases dramatically with speed.

04
⏱

Greater Power ≠ More Work

Power is the rate of doing work. A machine doing the same work faster has greater power, not more total work.

05
≠

Energy ≠ Power

Energy is measured in Joules; Power is measured in Watts. They are fundamentally different quantities with different units.

06
↓

Negative Work Reduces KE

Negative work removes kinetic energy from an object. Friction does negative work and slows objects down.

07
≤100

Efficiency ≤ 100% Always

No real machine can have efficiency above 100%. Any claim otherwise violates the law of conservation of energy.

08
⚠

Mechanical Energy is Not Always Conserved

Mechanical energy is conserved only when non-conservative forces (like friction or air drag) are absent or neglected.

09
J

Work is in Joules

The SI unit of work is Joule (J), not Newton. 1 J = 1 N·m. Mixing up units in MDCAT MCQs costs marks.

10
W

Power is in Watts

The SI unit of power is Watt (W), not Joule. 1 W = 1 J/s. Do not confuse work (J) with power (W).

Solved MDCAT Numericals

Q1. Work done at an angle+ Show Solution
Given: F = 40 N, d = 8 m, θ = 60°
Required: Work done (W)
Formula: W = Fd cos θ
Substitution: W = 40 × 8 × cos 60°
Calculation: W = 320 × 0.5 = 160 J
Answer: W = 160 J
Q2. Kinetic energy of a moving car+ Show Solution
Given: m = 800 kg, v = 20 m/s
Required: KE
Formula: KE = ½mv²
Substitution: KE = ½ × 800 × 20²
Calculation: KE = 400 × 400 = 160,000 J
Answer: KE = 1.6 × 10⁵ J
Q3. Potential energy of an object at height+ Show Solution
Given: m = 5 kg, h = 12 m, g = 9.8 m/s²
Required: PE
Formula: PE = mgh
Substitution: PE = 5 × 9.8 × 12
Calculation: PE = 588 J
Answer: PE = 588 J
Q4. Conservation of energy — find velocity at bottom+ Show Solution
Given: m = 2 kg, h = 10 m, starts from rest, no friction
Required: Velocity at the bottom
Formula: mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2gh)
Substitution: v = √(2 × 9.8 × 10)
Calculation: v = √196 = 14 m/s
Answer: v = 14 m/s
Q5. Power of an engine+ Show Solution
Given: W = 12,000 J, t = 40 s
Required: Power (P)
Formula: P = W/t
Substitution: P = 12000 / 40
Calculation: P = 300 W
Answer: P = 300 W
Q6. Efficiency of a pump+ Show Solution
Given: Input power = 500 W, Useful output = 375 W
Required: Efficiency
Formula: η = (Useful output / Input) × 100
Substitution: η = (375 / 500) × 100
Calculation: η = 0.75 × 100 = 75%
Answer: η = 75%
Q7. Work-Energy Theorem — find final velocity+ Show Solution
Given: m = 3 kg, vi = 4 m/s, Wnet = 54 J
Required: Final velocity
Formula: Wnet = ½mvf² − ½mvi²
Substitution: 54 = ½(3)vf² − ½(3)(16)
Calculation: 54 = 1.5vf² − 24 → vf² = 52 → vf ≈ 7.2 m/s
Answer: vf ≈ 7.2 m/s
Q8. Elastic PE stored in a spring+ Show Solution
Given: k = 400 N/m, x = 0.15 m
Required: EPE
Formula: EPE = ½kx²
Substitution: EPE = ½ × 400 × (0.15)²
Calculation: EPE = 200 × 0.0225 = 4.5 J
Answer: EPE = 4.5 J
Q9. Hooke's Law — find spring constant+ Show Solution
Given: F = 60 N, x = 0.3 m
Required: Spring constant (k)
Formula: F = kx → k = F/x
Substitution: k = 60 / 0.3
Calculation: k = 200 N/m
Answer: k = 200 N/m
Q10. Power at constant velocity+ Show Solution
Given: F = 800 N (driving force), v = 25 m/s (constant)
Required: Power output
Formula: P = Fv
Substitution: P = 800 × 25
Calculation: P = 20,000 W
Answer: P = 20 kW

Work & Energy — 2 Minute Revision

⚡ Key Formulas

  • W = Fd cos θ — Work
  • KE = ½mv² — Kinetic Energy
  • PE = mgh — Gravitational PE
  • E = KE + PE — Mechanical Energy
  • Wnet = ΔKE — Work-Energy Theorem
  • P = W/t — Power
  • P = Fv — Power (constant v)
  • η = (Output/Input) × 100%
  • F = kx — Hooke's Law
  • EPE = ½kx² — Elastic PE

🔥 15 Things You Must Remember

  1. Work = 0 if d = 0 (no displacement)
  2. Work = 0 if θ = 90° (perpendicular force)
  3. SI unit of Work and Energy = Joule (J)
  4. SI unit of Power = Watt (W)
  5. KE ∝ v² (velocity has squared effect)
  6. PE ∝ h (height has linear effect)
  7. KE + PE = constant (no friction)
  8. Work is a scalar quantity
  9. Energy is a scalar quantity
  10. Negative work → KE decreases
  11. Friction does negative work
  12. Efficiency < 100% in real machines
  13. 1 W = 1 J/s
  14. k = spring constant (N/m)
  15. Hooke's Law only valid within elastic limit
