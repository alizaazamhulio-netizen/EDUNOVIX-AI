01 • FOUNDATIONAL CONCEPTS

Thermodynamic Systems & Boundaries

Thermodynamics is the study of heat, mechanical work, temperature, and energy transformations governed by macroscopic variables ($P, V, T, U, S$).

Essential Definitions

  • System: Specified portion of the universe chosen for thermodynamic investigation.
  • Surroundings: Everything in the universe outside the thermodynamic system.
  • Boundary: Real or imaginary envelope separating system from surroundings (can be fixed/movable, diathermic/adiabatic).
⭐ MDCAT Key Rule: The state of a thermodynamic system is uniquely determined by state variables like Pressure ($P$), Volume ($V$), Temperature ($T$), and Internal Energy ($U$), which do not depend on the path taken!
System Classification Visualizer Interactive Comparison
Open System: Exchanges both matter and energy across its boundaries (e.g., Boiling water in an open beaker).

03 • SPECIFIC HEAT LAB

Specific Heat Capacity: $Q = mc\Delta T$

The quantity of thermal energy required to raise the temperature of $1\text{ kg}$ of a substance by $1\text{ K}$ (or $1^\circ\text{C}$). SI Unit: $\text{J}\cdot\text{kg}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$.

Common Specific Heats ($c$)

04 • PHASE CHANGES

Latent Heat: $Q = mL$

Thermal energy absorbed or released during a change of phase at constant temperature. $\Delta T = 0$ during latent phase conversion.

MDCAT High-Yield Phase Constants

  • Latent Heat of Fusion ($L_f$): Energy needed to convert $1\text{ kg}$ of solid to liquid at melting point ($L_{f,\text{water}} = 3.36 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg}$).
  • Latent Heat of Vaporization ($L_v$): Energy needed to convert $1\text{ kg}$ of liquid to steam at boiling point ($L_{v,\text{water}} = 2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}$).
⭐ MDCAT Trap Alert: $L_v > L_f$ because vaporization involves completely overcoming intermolecular bonds and vastly expanding volume against atmospheric pressure!
📈 Heating Curve ($T$ vs Time) Plateau: $\Delta T = 0$
Current state: Solid Ice heating towards melting plateau ($0^\circ\text{C}$).
05 • THERMAL EQUILIBRIUM

Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics

"If system A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B is in thermal equilibrium with C, then A is in thermal equilibrium with C."

Theoretical Significance

The Zeroth Law gives mathematical validity to the concept of Temperature as a universal state property that determines whether heat will flow between bodies in contact.

🌡️ Thermometer Principle: Thermometers work based on the Zeroth Law! The mercury in the thermometer (System B) reaches thermal equilibrium with the body (System A), which directly reflects temperature scale calibration (System C).

06 • CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

First Law of Thermodynamics: $\Delta U = Q - W$

Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; heat added to a system ($Q$) equals the increase in internal energy ($\Delta U$) plus mechanical work done by the system ($W$).

⚡ 3-Way Linked First Law Lab $Q = \Delta U + W$

07 • MOLECULAR VELOCITY & P-V WORK

Internal Energy ($U$) & Gas Work ($W = P\Delta V$)

Internal energy of an ideal gas is solely a function of temperature ($U \propto T$). When gas expands against external pressure, it performs mechanical work ($W = \int P dV$).

🧠 Kinetic Molecular Motion $U \propto T \implies \Delta U = 0$ if $\Delta T = 0$
Molecular RMS speed: $v_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}}$. Notice how particle collision frequency and kinetic velocity scale directly with $T$.
📐 Piston Chamber: $W = P\Delta V$ Area Under Curve
Calculated Work $W = P(V_2 - V_1)$: +608 J (Expansion)
08 • GRAPHICAL ANALYSIS & PROCESSES

P–V Diagram Lab & The 4 Master Processes

The area under any curve in a $P\text{–}V$ diagram directly represents the mechanical work performed by the gas. Select any thermodynamic process below to analyze its path.

📊 Interactive Multi-Curve P–V Analyzer
Active Process: Isothermal Process ($T = \text{const}$)
Governing Equation: $PV = \text{constant} \implies P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$
Work Done & 1st Law: $\Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W = nRT \ln(V_2/V_1)$
$\Delta T = 0$

Isothermal Process

$T = \text{const} \implies \Delta U = 0$
  • Boyle's Law: $P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$
  • First Law Form: $Q = W$ (all absorbed heat becomes work)
  • Slope of Curve: $\frac{dP}{dV} = -\frac{P}{V}$
  • Condition: Slow process in conducting container (diathermic walls)
$Q = 0$

Adiabatic Process

$PV^\gamma = \text{const} \implies \Delta U = -W$
  • Poisson's Law: $P_1 V_1^\gamma = P_2 V_2^\gamma$ ($\gamma = C_p/C_v$)
  • Expansion: $W > 0 \implies \Delta U < 0 \implies$ Cooling occurs!
  • Slope of Curve: $\frac{dP}{dV} = -\gamma \frac{P}{V}$ (Steeper than Isothermal)
  • Condition: Very rapid process in insulated container
$P = \text{const}$

Isobaric Process

$W = P\Delta V = P(V_2 - V_1)$
  • Charles's Law: $\frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$
  • First Law Form: $Q = \Delta U + P\Delta V$
  • Heat absorbed: $Q = n C_p \Delta T$
  • Condition: Movable frictionless piston under constant pressure
$V = \text{const}$

Isochoric Process

$\Delta V = 0 \implies W = 0$
  • Gay-Lussac's Law: $\frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$
  • First Law Form: $\Delta U = Q$ (no mechanical work performed)
  • Heat absorbed: $Q = n C_v \Delta T$
  • Condition: Rigid sealed container with fixed boundaries
09 • CLOSED CYCLES & NET WORK

Cyclic Processes: $\Delta U = 0 \implies Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}}$

A cyclic thermodynamic process returns the working substance to its exact initial state. Because internal energy is a state function, net change in internal energy over a complete cycle is strictly zero ($\Delta U = 0$).

MDCAT Cyclic Principles

  • Net Work Done ($W_{\text{net}}$): Equal to the enclosed area inside the closed cycle loop on a $P\text{–}V$ diagram.
  • Clockwise Cycle ($\circlearrowright$): Net Work is Positive ($W_{\text{net}} > 0$). Operates as a Heat Engine.
  • Counter-Clockwise Cycle ($\circlearrowleft$): Net Work is Negative ($W_{\text{net}} < 0$). Operates as a Refrigerator / Heat Pump.

10 • HEAT ENGINES & CARNOT LIMIT

Heat Engines & Thermal Efficiency

A heat engine absorbs heat $Q_H$ from a high-temperature source ($T_H$), converts a fraction into mechanical work ($W$), and rejects residual heat $Q_C$ to a cold sink ($T_C$).

⚙️ Heat Engine Flow Simulator $Q_H = W + Q_C$
🔥 HOT RESERVOIR ($T_H$) 600 K
⚙️ ENGINE
W = 600 J
❄️ COLD RESERVOIR ($T_C$) 300 K
📈 Thermal Efficiency ($\eta$) $\eta = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}$
60.0%
EFFICIENCY $\eta$
Work Delivered $W = Q_H - Q_C$: 600 J
Real Engine Efficiency $\eta = \frac{W}{Q_H}$: 0.60 (60.0%)
Carnot Max Limit $\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H}$: 50.0% (at 600K/300K)
⭐ Kelvin-Planck Law: It is impossible to construct a 100% efficient heat engine operating in a cycle ($\eta < 100\%$). Rejection of heat ($Q_C > 0$) is unavoidable!
11 • REVERSE HEAT ENGINE

Refrigerator & Coefficient of Performance (COP)

A refrigerator transfers heat from a cold region ($Q_C$) to a hotter region ($Q_H$) using external mechanical work ($W$). $\text{COP} = \frac{Q_C}{W} = \frac{Q_C}{Q_H - Q_C}$.

Key Operating Principles

  • Energy Balance: $Q_H = Q_C + W_{\text{in}}$
  • Coefficient of Performance: $\text{COP} = \frac{\text{Desired Cooling Output}}{\text{Work Input}} = \frac{Q_C}{W}$
  • Carnot Refrigerator: $\text{COP}_{\text{Carnot}} = \frac{T_C}{T_H - T_C}$
❄️ Open Refrigerator Door Question: If you leave a refrigerator door open in a closed room, the room temperature will INCREASE because total heat rejected ($Q_H = Q_C + W$) is greater than heat extracted ($Q_C$) by the amount of electrical work $W$ converted to heat!

12 • SECOND LAW & ENTROPY

Second Law of Thermodynamics & Entropy ($\Delta S$)

The Second Law dictates the natural arrow of spontaneous heat flow (hot to cold) and establishes that entropy ($\Delta S = \frac{Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$) of an isolated system always tends toward a maximum.

⚠️ Direction of Spontaneous Heat Flow Clausius Statement
🔥 Hot Body (80°C) ➔ ❄️ Cold Body (20°C) [Spontaneous Flow]
⭐ Clausius Statement: Heat cannot flow spontaneously from a cooler body to a hotter body without an input of external work!
🌀 Entropy & Molecular Dispersion $\Delta S = \frac{Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$
Natural processes are irreversible and progress from low entropy (ordered microstates) to high entropy (maximum energy dispersal).

Heat vs Temperature

Heat is energy in transit ($\text{J}$). Temperature measures average kinetic energy ($\text{K}$).

⚡

First Law Principle

Direct statement of conservation of energy: $\Delta U = Q - W \implies Q = \Delta U + W$.

🌡️

Isothermal Process

$T = \text{const} \implies \Delta U = 0 \implies Q = W$. Boyle's law applies.

🚫

Adiabatic Process

$Q = 0 \implies \Delta U = -W$. Rapid process. Slope steeper by factor $\gamma$.

📈

Isobaric Process

$P = \text{const} \implies W = P\Delta V$. Charles's law applies.

📉

Isochoric Process

$V = \text{const} \implies \Delta V = 0 \implies W = 0 \implies \Delta U = Q$.

🔁

Cyclic Process

Returns to start state $\implies \Delta U = 0 \implies Q_{\text{net}} = W_{\text{net}}$.

⚙️

Heat Engine Efficiency

$\eta = 1 - \frac{Q_C}{Q_H}$. Maximum limit: Carnot $\eta_C = 1 - \frac{T_C}{T_H} < 100\%$.

❄️

Refrigerator COP

Transfers heat from cold to hot using work: $\text{COP} = \frac{Q_C}{W}$.

🌀

Entropy Rule

$\Delta S = \frac{Q_{\text{rev}}}{T}$. For isolated systems, entropy never decreases.
