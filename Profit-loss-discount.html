Module 01

Introduction & Core Principles

In quantitative reasoning and university entrance examinations, questions on Profit, Loss, and Discount test your ability to model commercial transactions algebraically and mentally. Every commercial scenario revolves around four foundational financial coordinates:

Definition Cost Price (CP)

The total capital expended to acquire or manufacture an item, including all transportation, repair, and overhead costs.

Definition Marked Price (MP)

The tag, catalogue, or listed price displayed publicly on an article before any concessions or reductions are offered.

Definition Selling Price (SP)

The actual net cash amount realized when the goods change ownership and are transferred to the end purchaser.

Definition Profit / Loss

The algebraic difference between SP and CP. If SP > CP, we gain a profit; if SP < CP, we suffer a loss.

⚠️

Fundamental Rule of Bases (Crucial Exam Concept)

Profit% and Loss% are calculated with Cost Price (CP) in the denominator.
Discount% is calculated with Marked Price (MP) in the denominator.

Module 02

💰 Cost Price (CP) & Overheads

Cost Price (CP) represents the total cash outflow required to bring a commodity to a saleable state. In university entrance exams (FAST, NUST, ECAT), overhead expenses (such as freight, cartage, packing, repair, and customs duty) are always added directly to the invoice purchase price to determine the Effective Cost Price.

Effective Cost Price Formula
Effective CP = Purchase Price + All Overhead Expenses
Overheads include transport, repairs, taxes paid during purchase, labor, and setup costs.

Solved Examples (CP & Overheads)

FAST-style Example 2.1: Machine Overheads
An engineer buys a second-hand lathe machine for Rs. 42,000 and spends Rs. 8,000 on its transit and calibration. What is the effective CP?
• Base purchase = Rs. 42,000
• Overhead expense = Rs. 8,000
Effective CP = 42,000 + 8,000 = Rs. 50,000
NET-style Example 2.2: Unit Batch Cost
A merchant buys 200 light bulbs at Rs. 15 each. Cartage costs Rs. 200. What is the CP per bulb?
• Total bulb cost = 200 × 15 = Rs. 3,000
• Total expenditure = 3,000 + 200 = Rs. 3,200
CP per bulb = 3,200 / 200 = Rs. 16.00
Module 03

🏷️ Selling Price (SP) & Realization

Selling Price (SP) is the exact financial realization received by the seller after concluding the transaction. It is the destination value that determines whether the venture yielded a surplus or incurred a deficit.

Selling Price Equations
SP = CP + Profit  |  SP = CP - Loss  |  SP = MP - Discount
SP depends on CP when analyzing profit/loss, and depends on MP when analyzing retail discounts.

Module 05 & 06

📈 Profit and Loss Conditions

⚖️ Mathematical Scale Comparison
Profit Case (SP > CP):
SP = Rs. 1,250 (CP 1000 + Profit 250)
Loss Case (SP < CP):
SP = Rs. 800 (CP 1000 - Loss 200)
NET-style Example 5.1: Direct Profit
A smartphone purchased for Rs. 45,000 is sold for Rs. 52,000. Find the profit.
• Profit = SP - CP = 52,000 - 45,000
Profit = Rs. 7,000
FAST-style Example 6.1: Direct Loss
An investor buys shares for Rs. 80,000 and sells during a market dip for Rs. 71,500. Find the loss.
• Loss = CP - SP = 80,000 - 71,500
Loss = Rs. 8,500
Module 07 & 08

📊 Profit & Loss Percentages

Core Percentage Formulas
Profit% = (Profit / CP) × 100  |  Loss% = (Loss / CP) × 100
Both percentages are strictly ratios of the financial difference divided by the original Cost Price.
🚫

The #1 Entrance Test Trap: Calculating Profit on Selling Price

If a question states "A merchant earns 25% profit on Selling Price", this is a non-standard metric. If SP = 100, profit = 25, meaning CP = 75. The true Profit% on CP is (25/75) × 100 = 33.33%!

Module 09 & 10

🔄 Forward & Reverse CP / SP Formulas

Forward Multipliers Calculating SP from CP
SP = CP × (1 + p/100)  [Profit]
SP = CP × (1 - l/100)  [Loss]

Example: If CP = 600 and gain is 20%, SP = 600 × 1.20 = Rs. 720.

Reverse Multipliers Calculating CP from SP
CP = SP / (1 + p/100)  [Profit]
CP = SP / (1 - l/100)  [Loss]

Example: If SP = 990 at 10% gain, CP = 990 / 1.10 = Rs. 900.

Module 12 & 13

🏷️ Discount & Discount Percentage

Discount is the monetary price concession granted off the catalogue or Marked Price.

Discount Equations
Discount = MP - SP  |  Discount% = (Discount / MP) × 100
SP = MP × (1 - Discount%/100)
Always remember: Discount is calculated on Marked Price (MP), NOT Cost Price!

Module 16 & 17

🔄 Successive Discounts & Chain Reductions

When multiple discounts are offered in sequence (e.g. 20% followed by 10%), each subsequent discount applies only to the diminished intermediate price, not the initial marked price. Therefore:

❌

Common Fallacy: Adding Discount Percentages

20% discount + 10% discount DOES NOT equal 30% discount!
On Rs. 100: Rs. 100 - 20% = Rs. 80. Then Rs. 80 - 10% (Rs. 8) = Rs. 72. Total discount is Rs. 28 (28%).

Successive Discount Formulas
Final Price = MP × (1 - d₁/100) × (1 - d₂/100) × (1 - d₃/100)
Equivalent Single Discount (2 Stages) = d₁ + d₂ - (d₁ × d₂)/100

Module 18 & 19

⚖️ Markup, Discount & Net Profit Relationship

One of the highest-frequency question styles in FAST, NUST NET, IBA, and ECAT is the interaction between initial markup percentage and subsequent retail discount.

The Master Retail Markup Formula
MP / CP = (100 + Profit%) / (100 - Discount%)
This direct algebraic bridge connects Cost Price, Marked Price, Target Profit, and Concession Discount in a single step without needing intermediate SP calculations.
FAST-style Example 18.1: Finding Markup from Desired Profit
By what percentage above CP should a shopkeeper mark goods so that after giving a 10% discount, he still secures a 26% profit?
• MP / CP = (100 + 26) / (100 - 10) = 126 / 90
• MP / CP = 1.40
Required Markup = 40% above Cost Price
NET-style Example 18.2: Net Profit from Markup and Discount
An item is marked 30% above CP and sold at a 15% discount. What is the net profit percentage?
• Net Multiplier = 1.30 × (1 - 0.15) = 1.30 × 0.85 = 1.105
Net Profit = (1.105 - 1) × 100 = 10.5%
Module 24

🧾 Tax & Discount (Order of Operations)

In quantitative aptitude tests, sales tax is mathematically levied after trade discount is deducted from the marked price:

Tax Computation Formula
Final Invoice = [MP × (1 - d/100)] × (1 + t/100)
Where d is discount percentage and t is sales tax percentage.

💼 Commission & Brokerage Mathematics

Commission Formula
Commission Amount = (Commission% / 100) × Total Gross Sales
Net Remittance to Owner = Total Sales - Commission
Module 28

🎯 University Entrance & Scholarship Test Strategies

FAST-style Pattern Speed & Arithmetic Density

Emphasis on rapid mental percentage conversions (e.g. 16.67% = 1/6, 12.5% = 1/8), reverse calculations without paper, and deceptive successive discount wording.

NET-style Pattern Conceptual Balance & Ratios

Emphasis on ratio representations (CP : SP = 4 : 5), equal volume comparisons (CP of 20 = SP of 25), and false weight dishonest dealer questions.

ECAT / NED Pattern Multi-Step Word Problems

Focuses on damaged/spoiled inventories (e.g., 10% rot, selling remainder to earn overall 20% gain) and blended commodities with weighted averages.

IBA Karachi & LUMS Quantitative Reasoning

Focuses on algebraic variables (selling at X vs Y, doubling SP triples profit) and multi-tier successive commercial chains (Manufacturer → Distributor → Retailer).

⚡ 30+ High-Yield Speed Shortcuts

Shortcut 01
Equal SP with Equal Profit & Loss%
Always Loss = (x / 10)² %
Sold 2 items at same price: one at 20% gain, one at 20% loss → Net 4% Loss.
Shortcut 02
CP of X articles = SP of Y articles
Profit/Loss% = [(X - Y) / Y] × 100
CP of 15 pens = SP of 12 pens → Gain = [(15 - 12)/12] × 100 = 25%.
Shortcut 03
Dishonest Dealer (False Weight)
Gain% = [Error / (True - Error)] × 100
Uses 900g for 1000g → Gain = [100 / 900] × 100 = 11.11%.
Shortcut 04
Two Successive Discounts
Equivalent = d₁ + d₂ - (d₁ × d₂)/100
20% and 10% → 20 + 10 - 2 = 28%.
Shortcut 05
Buy X Get Y Free
Discount% = [Y / (X + Y)] × 100
Buy 4 Get 1 Free → [1 / (4 + 1)] × 100 = 20%.
Shortcut 06
Fraction to Percentage Multipliers
1/6 = 16.67%, 1/7 = 14.28%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/12 = 8.33%
Quick mental conversions save 30+ seconds per MCQ.
Shortcut 07
Markup & Discount Bridge
MP / CP = (100 + P%) / (100 - D%)
Need 20% profit after 10% discount → MP/CP = 120/90 = 4/3 (33.3% markup).
Shortcut 08
Reverse 20% Profit Shortcut
CP = SP × (5 / 6)
If SP = Rs. 1,200 at 20% profit → CP = 1200 × (5/6) = Rs. 1,000.
Shortcut 09
Reverse 25% Profit Shortcut
CP = SP × (4 / 5)
If SP = Rs. 1,000 at 25% profit → CP = 1000 × (4/5) = Rs. 800.
Shortcut 10
Equal Profit and Loss at Two Different SPs
CP = (SP₁ + SP₂) / 2
Profit at 832 = Loss at 448 → CP = (832 + 448)/2 = Rs. 640.
Module 30

⚠️ Common Traps & Examination Errors

❌ Mistake: Using SP as base for Profit%
✅ Correct: Profit% is strictly (Profit / CP) × 100
💡 Tip: Always identify cost price first before calculating rate.
❌ Mistake: Using CP as base for Discount%
✅ Correct: Discount% is strictly (Discount / MP) × 100
💡 Tip: Concessions are reduced from the marked tag price.
❌ Mistake: Adding successive discounts directly (20% + 10% = 30%)
✅ Correct: Equivalent discount is 20 + 10 - 2 = 28%
💡 Tip: 2nd discount applies only to the already discounted subtotal.
❌ Mistake: Believing two equal +x% and -x% changes cancel out
✅ Correct: A +20% then -20% change yields an overall 4% DECREASE
💡 Tip: Multiplier is 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 (4% drop).
Module 31

📘 Complete Quick Formula Sheet

1. Basic Profit & Loss
Profit = SP - CP  |  Loss = CP - SP

Surplus exists when SP > CP; Deficit when SP < CP.

CP = 500, SP = 650 → Profit = 650 - 500 = Rs. 150.
2. Profit & Loss Percentages
P% = (Profit / CP) × 100

Base denominator is invariably Cost Price.

Profit = 100, CP = 400 → (100 / 400) × 100 = 25%.
3. Discount & Marked Price
Discount% = [(MP - SP) / MP] × 100

Concession divided by Marked Price.

MP = 1200, SP = 960 → Discount = 240 → (240 / 1200) × 100 = 20%.
4. Successive Discount Formula
Eq Discount = d₁ + d₂ - (d₁ × d₂)/100

Combines two sequential concessions into one single rate.

d₁ = 15%, d₂ = 10% → 15 + 10 - 1.5 = 23.5%.

Module 34

⚡ 10-Minute High-Yield Quick Revision

📌 Denominator Check
  • Profit% & Loss%: Denominator is always Cost Price (CP).
  • Discount%: Denominator is always Marked Price (MP).
  • Commission%: Denominator is always Gross Sales Value.
📌 Essential Multipliers
  • +20% Profit → Multiply CP by 1.20 or 6/5.
  • -10% Loss → Multiply CP by 0.90 or 9/10.
  • -25% Discount → Multiply MP by 0.75 or 3/4.
📌 Rapid Shortcuts
  • Equal SP with ±x% → Always Net Loss = (x / 10)² %.
  • Successive d₁ & d₂ → d₁ + d₂ - (d₁ × d₂)/100.
  • Dishonest weight → [Error / False Weight] × 100.
📌 Crucial Trap Warnings
  • Never add successive discounts directly (20% + 10% ≠ 30%).
  • Tax is calculated AFTER discount is subtracted.
  • Overheads (repairs, transport) must always be added to CP before profit calculation.
