Test Architecture

What is NUST Entry Test (NET)?

A comprehensive breakdown of Pakistan's most competitive undergraduate admission test for engineering, computer science, business, and natural sciences.

Official NUST Verification Notice: Admission series schedules (NET-1, NET-2, NET-3, NET-4), eligibility criteria, test weighting formulas, and computer-based test (CBT) guidelines are administered officially by the National University of Sciences and Technology. Always verify the latest official dates and policies at ugadmissions.nust.edu.pk.
🎯

Purpose & Competitiveness

The NUST Entry Test (NET) is the primary determinant for undergraduate admissions at NUST campuses (H-12 Islamabad, EME Rawalpindi, CEME, MCS, PNEC Karachi, CAVM). The test evaluates high-speed analytical aptitude, conceptual grasp of FSc/A-Level syllabus, and logical reasoning under intense time pressure.

  • Computer-Based Test (CBT) at Islamabad campus; Paper-based in Karachi/Quetta
  • Calculators are NOT allowed during the official test
  • No negative marking — attempt every single question
⚖️

Merit Weightage Distribution

NUST computes the aggregate merit score using a strict composite formula favoring the entry test:

75% NUST NET Score
15% HSSC / FSc Part-1
10% SSC / Matric / O-Level

A high NET score (155+ for Software/CS/Electrical at SEECS) is crucial to guarantee admission into your desired program.

🔬

Subject Streams & Combinations

Depending on your intermediate background and intended university major, test composition varies:

Engineering & Computing: Math (80) + Physics (60) + Chemistry/CS (30) + English (20) + Intelligence (10) = 200
Applied Biosciences / Biotechnology: Biology (80) + Chemistry (60) + Physics (30) + English (20) + Intelligence (10) = 200
Business Studies & Social Sciences (NBS/S3H): Basic Math (80) + English (80) + Intelligence (40) = 200

Guidance & Clarification

Frequently Asked Questions

Clear answers to the most crucial queries about NUST NET pattern, preparation strategies, merit, and rules.

The NUST Entry Test (NET) is the competitive undergraduate entrance exam conducted by NUST. It is organized in four series annually (NET-1 in Nov/Dec, NET-2 in Jan/Feb, NET-3 in Mar/Apr, and NET-4 in Jun/Jul). Candidates can appear in multiple series; NUST automatically considers your highest NET score for final merit computation.

For Pre-Engineering candidates: Mathematics (80 MCQs), Physics (60 MCQs), Chemistry (30 MCQs), English (20 MCQs), and Intelligence (10 MCQs) totaling 200 MCQs. For ICS candidates, Computer Science (30 MCQs) replaces Chemistry.

No. Handheld scientific calculators or digital calculators are strictly prohibited during the official NUST computer-based test (CBT). Therefore, practicing mental math shortcuts, formula estimations, and fast approximation methods is vital.

Mathematics carries 80 marks (40% of the entire exam). Focus heavily on FSc Part 2 Calculus (Integration, Differentiation), Conic Sections, Trigonometric Identities, and Vectors. Memorize standard shortcuts for finding tangents, normals, derivatives, and definite integral bounds without lengthy step-by-step working.

Physics (60 MCQs) tests fundamental definitions, proportionalities (e.g. if radius doubles, how does magnetic field change?), unit dimensions, and core numerical formulas in Mechanics, Electromagnetism, Wave Optics, and Modern Physics. Master the key summaries and exercise MCQs from the Punjab Textbook Board (PTB) / Federal Board books.

English contains 20 MCQs covering high-frequency vocabulary words (synonyms/antonyms), contextual sentence completion, preposition collocations, and reading comprehension passages. Practice with SAT/GRE wordlists and our built-in interactive verbal drills.

Intelligence comprises 10 MCQs. They follow predictable patterns: arithmetic/geometric number series, alphabet skip codes, word analogies, odd-one-out classifications, and direction sense problems. Spending 10 minutes reviewing series difference rules guarantees an easy 10/10.

No, there is zero negative marking in NUST NET. Each correct answer awards +1 mark. If you are running out of time, you should make calculated guesses and answer all 200 questions.

The standard aggregate formula is: 75% NUST NET score + 15% HSSC / FSc Part-1 + 10% SSC / Matric / O-Level equivalence. Because 75% comes directly from NET, scoring above 155 is the single most decisive factor.

Yes. You can apply for and sit in all four series (NET-1, NET-2, NET-3, and NET-4) during an academic year. There is no penalty for taking multiple attempts, and only your highest score is considered for merit.

Always verify official announcements directly at ugadmissions.nust.edu.pk. Registration windows open roughly 4 to 6 weeks before each series begins.
