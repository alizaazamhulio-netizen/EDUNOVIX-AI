What is Fluid Dynamics?

Definition
Fluid dynamics is the branch of physics that studies the motion of fluids and the forces associated with their motion.

A fluid is a substance that can flow. Both liquids and gases are fluids.

Where it is used

  • Flow of water through pipes
  • Movement of air
  • Blood flow through blood vessels
  • Flow through narrow tubes
  • Motion of fluids around objects
2

Ideal Fluid

For many physics problems we use the idea of an ideal fluid. An ideal fluid is considered to be:

  • Incompressible — its density stays almost constant when pressure changes
  • Non-viscous — no internal friction between layers
  • In steady flow — velocity at a point does not change with time
Incompressible fluid
An incompressible fluid is a fluid whose density remains approximately constant when pressure changes. For MDCAT problems, liquids are treated as incompressible.
3

Streamline Flow

Definition
Streamline flow is a type of fluid motion in which every particle of the fluid follows a smooth path and the paths do not cross each other.

The paths followed by the fluid particles are called streamlines. In steady flow, the velocity of the fluid at a particular point remains constant with time.

Equation of Continuity

The equation of continuity is based on the law of conservation of mass. For an incompressible fluid flowing through a pipe:

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂A = cross-sectional area (m²), v = fluid speed (m/s)

Important idea

  • Pipe becomes narrower → area decreases → speed increases
  • Pipe becomes wider → area increases → speed decreases

Worked example

If the area becomes half, A₂ = A₁/2, then A₁v₁ = (A₁/2)v₂, so v₂ = 2v₁ — the fluid moves twice as fast.

MDCAT Trap
Narrow pipe → faster flow (never slower).

Volume Flow Rate

Definition
Volume flow rate Q is the volume of fluid passing through a cross-section of a pipe per unit time.
Q = A vSI unit: m³/s

Therefore A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ simply means the volume flow rate stays constant for an incompressible fluid in steady flow.

6

Pressure in a Moving Fluid

Definition
Pressure is the normal force acting per unit area of a surface.
P = F / ASI unit: pascal (Pa) = N/m²

A moving fluid can have different pressures at different points. The relationship between pressure, speed and height is described by Bernoulli's equation.

7

Bernoulli's Principle

Definition
For steady flow of an ideal incompressible fluid, an increase in the speed of the fluid is associated with a decrease in its pressure.
  • Speed ↑ → Pressure ↓
  • Speed ↓ → Pressure ↑
8

9

Bernoulli's Equation for a Horizontal Pipe

If the pipe is at the same height, h₁ = h₂, the ρgh terms cancel:

P₁ + ½ρv₁² = P₂ + ½ρv₂²

If v₂ > v₁ then P₂ < P₁.

MDCAT Golden Rule
Higher fluid speed → lower pressure, for ideal steady-flow conditions.
10

Applications of Bernoulli's Principle

Atomizer / spray

When air moves rapidly across the opening of a tube, the pressure there decreases. The higher pressure elsewhere pushes the liquid upward, producing a spray.

Perfume sprayer

A perfume atomizer uses the pressure difference created by rapidly moving air to draw liquid upward and break it into small droplets.

Airplane wings

Air moving around an aerofoil creates a pressure distribution that contributes to lift. At MDCAT level: fluid motion → pressure difference → lift.

Venturi effect

Definition
The Venturi effect is the decrease in fluid pressure that occurs when a fluid flows through a narrower section of a pipe.

In the narrow region: Area ↓ → Speed ↑ → Pressure ↓. This is continuity + Bernoulli working together.

Torricelli's Law

For a liquid flowing out through a small opening in a tank:

v = √(2gh)v = speed of efflux (m/s), h = height of liquid above the opening (m)

Greater liquid height → greater exit speed.

12

Viscosity

Definition
Viscosity is the internal resistance of a fluid to the relative motion of its layers.

In simple words: viscosity tells us how strongly a fluid resists flowing. Honey has high viscosity; water has lower viscosity. A highly viscous fluid flows more slowly than a less viscous fluid under similar conditions.

High-yield traps

  • Fluid dynamics deals with fluids in motion; liquids and gases are both fluids.
  • Narrower pipe → greater speed; wider pipe → lower speed.
  • Continuity equation comes from conservation of mass.
  • Q = Av is volume flow rate; SI unit of pressure is the pascal.
  • Same height + greater speed → lower pressure.
  • Venturi effect occurs in the narrow section of a pipe.
  • Greater height above an opening → greater efflux speed.
  • Viscosity = resistance to relative motion of fluid layers.
