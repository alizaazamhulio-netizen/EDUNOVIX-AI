1. Introduction to Electromagnetism

The unified physical framework linking moving electric charges, magnetic fields, and induced electromotive force.

📖 Textbook Definition
Electromagnetism is the fundamental branch of physics that deals with electric charges, electric currents, magnetic fields, and the mutual interaction between electricity and magnetism.

1. Oersted's Discovery (1820)

A steady electric current flowing through a conductor produces a concentric magnetic field in the surrounding space.

Electric Current (I) ⟶ Magnetic Field (B)

2. Faraday's Discovery (1831)

A time-varying (changing) magnetic flux linked with a closed conducting loop generates an induced electric current and electromotive force.

Changing Magnetic Flux (ΔΦ / Δt) ⟶ Induced EMF (ε)
Module 02

2. Magnetic Field & Flux Density

Vector properties, field lines, and the definition of magnetic induction B.

📖 Definition: Magnetic Field
A Magnetic Field is the region of space surrounding a permanent magnet or current-carrying conductor within which its magnetic force can be experienced by another magnet, moving charge, or magnetic dipole.

The strength and direction of a magnetic field at any point is represented by the Magnetic Flux Density (or Magnetic Induction), denoted by the vector B.

B Magnetic Induction / Flux Density
SI Unit Tesla (T) = N / (A·m) = Wb / m²
CGS Unit Gauss (G); 1 Tesla = 10⁴ Gauss
Type Vector quantity (Tangent to field line)
⚡ High-Yield Properties of Magnetic Field Lines
  • Magnetic field lines are continuous closed loops (No magnetic monopoles exist).
  • Outside a bar magnet, lines emerge from the North Pole and enter the South Pole.
  • Inside a bar magnet or solenoid, lines travel from South to North.
  • Two magnetic field lines can never intersect, because at the point of intersection, the field would have two different directions, which is physically impossible.

Ampère's Law applications and geometry-dependent field configurations.

🔬 Interactive Field Lines Simulator
Geometry 01

Straight Current-Carrying Conductor

Field lines form concentric circles centered on the wire. The field strength drops inversely with radial distance r.

B = μ0 · I2π · r

Grasp the wire with your right hand with your outstretched thumb pointing in the direction of conventional current I. Your curled fingers indicate the direction of magnetic field lines B.
Geometry 02

Circular Coil (Center Field)

For a flat circular coil of N turns and radius R carrying current I, the field at the exact center is perpendicular to the plane:

Bcenter = μ0 · N · I2 · R
⚡ MDCAT Ratio Trick
Doubling the number of turns N doubles B. Halving the radius R doubles B.
Geometry 03

Long Solenoid

A helical winding of insulated wire. Inside a long tightly wound solenoid, the magnetic field is uniform, strong, and parallel to the axis:

B = μ0 · n · I = μ0 · (NL) · I
⚠️ Solenoid End Effect Trap
At the open ends of a solenoid, the magnetic field is exactly half of its value at the central interior: Bend = ½ μ0 n I.

4. Magnetic Force on Conductors & Charges

Lorentz magnetic force, vector cross products, and mechanical force on current-carrying segments.

Conductor Force

Force on a Current-Carrying Wire

When a straight conductor of length L carrying current I is placed in a uniform magnetic field B, it experiences a sideways deflecting force:
F = I · (L × B) &implies; F = I · L · B · sin(θ)
θ Angle between conductor length vector L and B
Max F When θ = 90° (Perpendicular), F = I L B
Min F When θ = 0° or 180° (Parallel), F = 0 N
Lorentz Force

Force on a Moving Charged Particle

A charge q moving with velocity v through a magnetic field B experiences the Lorentz magnetic force:
FB = q · (v × B) &implies; FB = q · v · B · sin(θ)
⚠️ Essential MDCAT Lorentz Rules
  • Stationary Charge (v = 0): Magnetic force is identically ZERO.
  • Uncharged Particle (q = 0, e.g. Neutron): Magnetic force is ZERO.
  • Zero Work Done: Because F ⊥ v, work done W = 0. Kinetic energy and speed are strictly CONSTANT!

Module 06

6. Torque on Coil & DC Electric Motor

Principle, split-ring commutator action, and electrical-to-mechanical power conversion.

⚙️ Interactive DC Motor Armature & Commutator
📖 Definition: Electric Motor
An Electric Motor is a device that converts electrical energy into continuous mechanical rotational energy utilizing the magnetic deflecting torque exerted on a current-carrying armature coil.
τ = N · I · A · B · cos(α) = N · I · A · B · sin(θ)
Split-Ring Commutator Function:

Reverses the current direction in each coil side every half-turn (180°), ensuring that the deflecting torque acts in a single unidirectional sense without reversing back and forth.

Carbon Brushes:

Provide sliding electrical contact to feed direct current from the external battery supply to the rotating split rings without tangling connecting wires.

Module 07

7. Magnetic Flux & Faraday's Law of Induction

Flux linkage, induced voltage generation, and motional electromotive force.

📖 Definition: Magnetic Flux
Magnetic Flux (Φ) is the measure of the total number of magnetic field lines passing perpendicularly through a specified surface area A.
Φ = B · A = B · A · cos(θ)
📖 Faraday's Law of Electromagnetic Induction
The magnitude of the electromotive force (ε) induced in a conducting circuit is directly proportional to the time rate of change of magnetic flux linked with the circuit.
ε = -N · ΔΦΔt
Module 08

8. Lenz's Law & Motional EMF

Directional opposition, energy conservation, and charge separation across moving conductors.

Direction Rule

Lenz's Law

"The direction of the induced current is always such as to oppose the change in magnetic flux that produces it."

The negative sign in Faraday’s formula (ε = -N ΔΦ/Δt) mathematically represents Lenz's Law. It is an expression of the Law of Conservation of Energy.

⚡ Magnet Approaching Loop Rule
Approaching North pole ⟶ Loop induces a North pole (repulsion).
Receding North pole ⟶ Loop induces a South pole (attraction).
Motional Effect

Motional EMF

The electromotive force induced across the ends of a conductor when it is mechanically moved across a magnetic field:
ε = -v · B · L · sin(θ)

Induced current in circuit of resistance R: I = v B L / R.
Mechanical power required to pull wire: P = (v2 B2 L2) / R.

10. Transformers & Core Energy Losses

Mutual induction, voltage transformation ratios, and eddy current mitigation.

📖 Definition: Transformer
A Transformer is a static electrical device that increases (steps up) or decreases (steps down) alternating voltage and current levels at constant frequency through mutual electromagnetic induction.
VsVp = NsNp = IpIs
Type of Transformer Loss Physical Cause Engineering Mitigation
1. Copper Loss (I²R) Joule heating dissipation in primary and secondary wire windings Use thick, high-conductivity copper wires of low electrical resistance.
2. Eddy Current Loss Induced circulating loop currents in the bulk iron core Use a laminated core composed of thin varnished iron sheets.
3. Hysteresis Loss Energy dissipated during repeated magnetic domain reversal cycles Use soft iron or high-silicon steel having a narrow hysteresis loop.
4. Magnetic Flux Leakage Incomplete magnetic coupling between primary and secondary coils Wind the secondary coil directly over the primary coil on a closed core.

11. Electromagnetic Waves

Maxwell's theory, transverse field orthogonality, and vacuum propagation speed.

Transverse Vector Synthesis
📖 Definition: Electromagnetic Wave
An Electromagnetic Wave is a self-propagating transverse wave consisting of mutually perpendicular, time-varying electric (E) and magnetic (B) fields oscillating in phase at right angles to the direction of propagation.
c = 1√μ0 · ε0 = f · λ = 3.0 × 108 m/s
⚡ Fundamental Properties of EM Waves
  • Do not require any material medium for propagation (travel freely through vacuum).
  • Strictly transverse: E ⊥ B ⊥ v. Can be polarized.
  • Ratio of amplitudes: E0 / B0 = c.
  • Carry both energy and momentum: Photon energy E = h f = h c / λ.

Problem 01: Moving Charge Lorentz Force
An electron (q = 1.6 × 10-19 C) moves with a velocity of 3.0 × 106 m/s into a uniform magnetic field of 0.4 T at an angle of 30° to the field lines. Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force exerted on the electron.
GIVEN:
q = 1.6 × 10-19 C, v = 3.0 × 106 m/s, B = 0.4 T, θ = 30°
REQUIRED:
Magnetic Force (F)
FORMULA:
F = q · v · B · sin(θ)
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
F = (1.6 × 10-19) × (3.0 × 106) × 0.4 × sin(30°)
F = (1.92 × 10-13) × 0.5 = 9.6 × 10-14 N
FINAL ANSWER:
The magnitude of the magnetic deflecting force is 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N.
Problem 02: Circular Orbit Centripetal Radius
A proton of mass 1.67 × 10-27 kg and charge 1.6 × 10-19 C enters perpendicularly into a 0.2 T magnetic field with a speed of 4.0 × 105 m/s. Determine the radius of its circular trajectory.
GIVEN:
m = 1.67 × 10-27 kg, q = 1.6 × 10-19 C, B = 0.2 T, v = 4.0 × 105 m/s
REQUIRED:
Orbital Radius (r)
FORMULA:
r = (m · v) / (q · B)
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
r = (1.67 × 10-27 × 4.0 × 105) / (1.6 × 10-19 × 0.2)
r = (6.68 × 10-22) / (3.2 × 10-20) = 0.020875 m = 2.09 cm
FINAL ANSWER:
The radius of the circular path is 2.09 cm (0.0209 m).
Problem 03: Straight Wire Field Ampère's Law
Find the magnetic flux density B at a radial distance of 4 cm (0.04 m) from a straight wire carrying an electric current of 10 A. (μ0 = 4π × 10-7 T·m/A).
GIVEN:
I = 10 A, r = 0.04 m, μ0 = 4π × 10-7 T·m/A
REQUIRED:
Magnetic Field (B)
FORMULA:
B = (μ0 · I) / (2π · r)
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
B = (4π × 10-7 × 10) / (2π × 0.04) = (2 × 10-6) / 0.04 = 5.0 × 10-5 T (0.5 Gauss)
FINAL ANSWER:
The magnetic field strength is 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ Tesla.
Problem 04: Solenoid Field Solenoid Formula
A solenoid of length 0.5 m is wound with 1000 turns of copper wire. If a current of 4 A passes through it, compute the uniform magnetic field in the interior.
GIVEN:
L = 0.5 m, N = 1000, I = 4 A, n = N/L = 2000 turns/m
REQUIRED:
Magnetic Field (B)
FORMULA:
B = μ0 · n · I
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
B = (4π × 10-7) × 2000 × 4 = 3.2π × 10-3 ≈ 1.005 × 10-2 T (10.05 mT)
FINAL ANSWER:
The magnetic field inside the solenoid is 1.01 × 10⁻² Tesla (10.1 mT).
Problem 05: Faraday's Induction Induced Voltage
A coil consisting of 200 turns has a cross-sectional area of 0.05 m². It is held perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field that decreases uniformly from 0.6 T to 0.1 T in 0.2 seconds. Calculate the magnitude of average induced EMF.
GIVEN:
N = 200, A = 0.05 m², B1 = 0.6 T, B2 = 0.1 T, Δt = 0.2 s
REQUIRED:
Induced EMF (|ε|)
FORMULA:
|ε| = N · |ΔΦ|Δt = N · A · |ΔB|Δt
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
|ΔB| = |0.1 - 0.6| = 0.5 T
|ε| = 200 × 0.05 × (0.5 / 0.2) = 10 × 2.5 = 25.0 Volts
FINAL ANSWER:
The magnitude of induced EMF is 25.0 V.
Problem 06: Motional EMF Moving Conductor
A metal rod of length 0.8 m moves at a speed of 15 m/s perpendicular to a magnetic field of 0.25 T. If the rod slides on rails connected to a 6 Ω resistor, calculate the induced current.
GIVEN:
L = 0.8 m, v = 15 m/s, B = 0.25 T, R = 6 Ω
REQUIRED:
Induced Motional Current (I)
FORMULA:
ε = v · B · L;   I = ε / R
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
ε = 15 × 0.25 × 0.8 = 3.0 V
I = 3.0 V / 6 Ω = 0.50 Amperes
FINAL ANSWER:
The induced motional current in the circuit is 0.50 A.
Problem 07: Step-Down Transformer Turns Ratio
A step-down transformer is used on a 240 V AC line to deliver 12 V to a device. If the primary winding has 1200 turns and the secondary delivers 4 A, calculate the secondary turns and the primary current (assume 100% efficiency).
GIVEN:
Vp = 240 V, Vs = 12 V, Np = 1200, Is = 4 A
REQUIRED:
Secondary Turns (Ns) and Primary Current (Ip)
FORMULA:
Ns = Np · (Vs / Vp);   Ip = Is · (Vs / Vp)
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
Ns = 1200 × (12 / 240) = 1200 × (1/20) = 60 turns
Ip = 4 × (12 / 240) = 4 / 20 = 0.20 A
FINAL ANSWER:
Secondary coil has 60 turns; primary current is 0.20 A.
Problem 08: AC Generator Peak EMF Peak Voltage
An AC generator has a rectangular coil of 50 turns and area 0.12 m² rotating at 1800 rpm in a uniform magnetic field of 0.3 T. Compute its maximum (peak) induced electromotive force.
GIVEN:
N = 50, A = 0.12 m², B = 0.3 T, f = 1800 / 60 = 30 Hz, ω = 2πf = 60π rad/s
REQUIRED:
Peak EMF (ε0)
FORMULA:
ε0 = N · A · B · ω
SUBSTITUTION & CALCULATION:
ε0 = 50 × 0.12 × 0.3 × (60 × 3.1416)
ε0 = 1.8 × 188.5 = 339.29 Volts
FINAL ANSWER:
The peak induced EMF is 339.3 Volts.
Problem 09: EM Wave Photon Energy Planck Relation
Calculate the frequency and photon energy in electron-volts (eV) for an X-ray having a wavelength of 0.15 nm (1.5 × 10-10 m). (h = 6.63 × 10-34 J·s, c = 3.0 × 108 m/s, 1 eV = 1.6 × 10-19 J).

λ = 1.5 × 10-10 m, c = 3.0 × 108 m/s, h = 6.63 × 10-34 J·s

E = (6.63 × 10-34 × 2.0 × 1018) / (1.6 × 10-19) = 8287.5 eV = 8.29 keV

Frequency is 2.0 × 10¹⁸ Hz; Photon energy is 8.29 keV.
Problem 10: Magnetic Dipole Torque Torque on Coil

19. High-Yield MDCAT Exam Traps & Pitfalls

Common misconceptions and deceptive questions frequently set by MDCAT examiners.

⚠️ TRAP 01: Angle in Magnetic Flux vs Conductor Force
Common Student Error: Using cos(θ) for wire force and sin(θ) for flux.
MDCAT Correct Fact: • Conductor Force F = I L B sin(θ) where θ is angle between wire length L and B.
• Magnetic Flux Φ = B A cos(θ) where θ is angle between B and the surface normal vector (perpendicular to surface plane).
⚠️ TRAP 02: Negative Charge Deflection Direction
Common Student Error: Forgetting that an electron's negative charge inverts the right-hand cross product vector.
MDCAT Correct Fact: Always use the Right-Hand Rule for positive charge (+q), then reverse the final force direction by 180° for an electron (-e).
⚠️ TRAP 03: Transformer Frequency
Common Student Error: Believing a step-up transformer increases AC frequency.
MDCAT Correct Fact: Transformers change voltage and current, but the AC frequency is strictly conserved (fs = fp).
