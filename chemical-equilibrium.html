Topic 01 Foundation

Introduction to Chemical Equilibrium

📖 Textbook Definition

"Chemical equilibrium is the state of a reversible reaction in which the rates of the forward and reverse reactions become equal, and the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant over time."

In many chemical reactions, reactants do not completely convert into products. As products accumulate, they begin reacting with each other to re-form the original reactants. When the speed at which products are formed exactly balances the speed at which products decompose back into reactants, a state of balance is reached. This state is known as chemical equilibrium.

📌 Important Point

Equilibrium is a macroscopic property. At the microscopic level, molecules continue to collide and react ceaselessly, but because the two opposing rates are equal, no net change in composition is observable.

💡 MDCAT Tip

Equilibrium can only be reached if the reaction is conducted in a closed system at a constant temperature so that matter cannot leave or enter.

⚠️ Common Mistake

Never think that chemical equilibrium means equal amounts or equal concentrations of reactants and products. Equilibrium only means equal reaction rates and constant concentrations.

Topics 02 & 03 Core Concept

Reversible vs. Irreversible Reactions

📖 Textbook Definition — Reversible Reaction

"A reversible reaction is a chemical reaction in which the products can react together under suitable conditions to re-form the original reactants. It is represented by a double arrow (⇌)."

N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)
📖 Textbook Definition — Irreversible Reaction

"An irreversible reaction is a chemical reaction that proceeds in only one direction until at least one of the reactants is completely consumed. It is represented by a single forward arrow (→)."

2KClO3(s) → 2KCl(s) + 3O2(g)

In an irreversible reaction, products escape (such as an open gas evolution) or form extremely stable, insoluble precipitates, preventing the reverse pathway. In contrast, in a closed vessel, many reactions allow both forward and reverse pathways to occur simultaneously.

Topics 04, 05 & 06 High-Yield Theory

Dynamic Nature & Characteristics of Chemical Equilibrium

📖 Textbook Definition — Dynamic Equilibrium

"Dynamic equilibrium is an equilibrium state in which the forward and reverse reactions continue to occur simultaneously and at exactly equal rates, so that no macroscopic change in the system is observed."

Chemical equilibrium is strictly dynamic, not static. Unlike a physical bridge in static equilibrium where all forces and motions are zero, chemical reactions at equilibrium are continuously proceeding at high microscopic velocity in both directions.

Core Characteristics of Chemical Equilibrium:

  • Dynamic State: Forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates (Rateforward = Ratereverse).
  • Constant Concentrations: The concentrations of reactants and products remain unchanged over time.
  • Closed System Requirement: Equilibrium can only be established in a closed container where no matter enters or escapes.
  • Approachable from Either Direction: Equilibrium can be reached starting purely with reactants or purely with products.
  • Unaffected by Catalysts: A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally, reaching equilibrium faster without altering the equilibrium position or the value of K.
  • Free Energy Minimum: At equilibrium, the Gibbs Free Energy change is zero (ΔG = 0).
💡 MDCAT Trap Warning

MDCAT question trap: "At equilibrium, does the reaction stop?" Answer: NO. The reaction never stops; only the net rate of reaction is zero because forward rate = reverse rate.

Topics 07 to 11 Animated Kinetics

Establishment of Equilibrium & Rate Curves

At the start of a reaction (Time t = 0), reactant concentrations are at their maximum, so the Rate of Forward Reaction (Rf) is highest. As reactants are consumed, Rf decreases over time.

Conversely, initially product concentrations are zero, so the Rate of Reverse Reaction (Rr) is zero. As products are formed, Rr increases. Eventually, both rates meet and become equal:

Core Equilibrium Condition
Rateforward = Ratereverse

The vertical dashed line marks the exact moment chemical equilibrium is established, where both curves merge into a single constant horizontal plateau.
Topics 12 to 17 Core Mathematics

Law of Mass Action, Kc and Kp

📖 Textbook Definition — Law of Mass Action

"The Law of Mass Action states that the rate of a chemical reaction is directly proportional to the product of the active masses (molar concentrations) of the reacting substances, each raised to a power equal to its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced chemical equation."

Proposed by Norwegian chemists Cato Guldberg and Peter Waage in 1864.

Derivation of Equilibrium Constant (Kc) Expression:

Consider a general reversible elementary reaction:

aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

Rate of forward reaction: Rf = kf [A]a [B]b
Rate of reverse reaction: Rr = kr [C]c [D]d

At chemical equilibrium, Rf = Rr:

Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Concentration (Kc)
Kc = kf kr = [C]c [D]d [A]a [B]b
[A], [B] Equilibrium molar concentrations of reactants (mol/dm³)
[C], [D] Equilibrium molar concentrations of products (mol/dm³)
a, b, c, d Stoichiometric coefficients from the balanced equation
kf, kr Specific rate constants for forward and reverse reactions

Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Partial Pressure (Kp):

For gaseous reactions, partial pressures of gases are used instead of molar concentrations:

Equilibrium Constant in Terms of Partial Pressures (Kp)
Kp = (PC)c (PD)d (PA)a (PB)b

Relationship Between Kp and Kc:

Universal Kp — Kc Master Relationship
Kp = Kc (R T)Δng
Δng Change in moles of gas = (moles of gaseous products) − (moles of gaseous reactants)
R Ideal gas constant = 0.0821 dm³·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹ (or 8.314 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹)
T Absolute temperature in Kelvin (K = °C + 273)
Condition of Δng Mathematical Outcome Representative Example
Δng = 0
(Equal gaseous moles)		 Kp = Kc
(RT)0 = 1		 H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)
Δn = 2 − 2 = 0
Δng > 0
(More product gas moles)		 Kp > Kc
(when RT > 1)		 PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g)
Δn = (1+1) − 1 = +1
Δng < 0
(Fewer product gas moles)		 Kp < Kc
(when RT > 1)		 N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)
Δn = 2 − 4 = −2
Topics 18, 19 & 20 Phases & States

Homogeneous vs. Heterogeneous Equilibrium & Pure Solids Rule

📖 Homogeneous Equilibrium

"An equilibrium state in which all reacting species and products exist in the single, same physical phase (all gases or all liquids in solution)."

2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)
📖 Heterogeneous Equilibrium

"An equilibrium state in which the reactants and products exist in two or more distinct physical phases."

CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g)
⭐ CRITICAL MDCAT EXAM RULE: Omission of Pure Solids and Liquids

Why are pure solids and pure liquids excluded from Kc and Kp expressions?
The concentration of a pure solid or liquid is proportional to its density and inversely proportional to its molar mass (Concentration = Density / Molar Mass). Because density and molar mass are intrinsic intensive properties, their concentrations remain strictly constant during a reaction at constant temperature.

Example: For CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g):
Kc = [CO2]    and    Kp = PCO2

Topics 21 to 23 Extent of Reaction

Magnitude of the Equilibrium Constant (K)

The numerical magnitude of K informs us about the extent of reaction (how much product is formed when equilibrium is reached), but tells us nothing about how fast the reaction proceeds.

Magnitude of K Physical Meaning Equilibrium Mixture Composition Representative Example
Very Large K
(K >> 103)		 Reaction proceeds almost to completion. Forward reaction is heavily favored. Consists almost entirely of products. Reactants are present only in trace amounts. 2O3(g) ⇌ 3O2(g)
Kc ≈ 1055 at 25 °C
Moderate K
(10−3 ≤ K ≤ 103)		 Appreciable amounts of both reactants and products coexist at equilibrium. Comparable concentrations of reactants and products present. N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g)
Kc = 0.211 at 100 °C
Very Small K
(K << 10−3)		 Reaction barely takes place. Reverse reaction is heavily favored. Consists almost entirely of unreacted reactants. Trace product only. 2H2O(g) ⇌ 2H2(g) + O2(g)

Topics 26 to 34 Fundamental Master Principle

Le Chatelier's Principle & Factors Affecting Equilibrium

📖 Textbook Definition — Le Chatelier's Principle

"If a chemical system at equilibrium is subjected to an external stress (a change in concentration, pressure, volume, or temperature), the system will adjust itself in such a direction so as to counteract and nullify the effect of that stress."

Formulated by French chemist Henri Louis Le Chatelier (1884).
🧪 Interactive Le Chatelier Stress & Shift Laboratory

Detailed Breakdown of Stress Factors:

1. Effect of Concentration

• Adding reactant: Equilibrium shifts FORWARD (→) to consume excess reactant.
• Removing product: Equilibrium shifts FORWARD (→) to replace missing product.
• Adding product: Equilibrium shifts REVERSE (←).
• Effect on K: Kc remains completely UNCHANGED.

2 & 3. Effect of Pressure and Volume

• Pressure affects only gaseous equilibria where Δng ≠ 0.
• Increasing Pressure (or Decreasing Volume): Shifts equilibrium to the side with FEWER gaseous moles.
• Decreasing Pressure (or Increasing Volume): Shifts equilibrium to the side with MORE gaseous moles.
• If Δng = 0 (e.g. H2 + I2 ⇌ 2HI): Pressure changes have NO EFFECT on equilibrium position.
• Effect on K: Kc and Kp remain completely UNCHANGED.

4. Effect of Temperature (The ONLY factor that alters K)

• Exothermic Reaction (ΔH < 0): Forward reaction releases heat.
  → Raising Temperature: Shifts REVERSE (←), Kc DECREASES.
  → Lowering Temperature: Shifts FORWARD (→), Kc INCREASES.
• Endothermic Reaction (ΔH > 0): Forward reaction absorbs heat.
  → Raising Temperature: Shifts FORWARD (→), Kc INCREASES.
  → Lowering Temperature: Shifts REVERSE (←), Kc DECREASES.

5. Effect of Catalyst

• Lowers the activation energy for BOTH forward and reverse reactions by the exact same amount.
• Does NOT change the equilibrium position.
• Does NOT change the value of Kc.
• Only reduces the time required to attain equilibrium.

Topics 35, 36 & 37 Industrial Chemistry

Industrial Applications: Haber & Contact Processes

🏭 The Haber-Bosch Process for Ammonia Synthesis
N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g)   (ΔH = −92.4 kJ/mol, Exothermic)
Condition 1
High Pressure: 200–250 atm

Reactants have 4 moles of gas; products have 2 moles. High pressure favors ammonia formation and increases yield.

Condition 2
Compromise Temp: 400–450 °C

Exothermic reaction favors low temp for high yield, but low temp makes the rate too slow. 450 °C is the optimum economic compromise.

Condition 3
Catalyst: Finely Divided Iron (Fe)

Iron with promoters (Al2O3, K2O, MgO) increases reaction rate so equilibrium is reached in seconds.

Condition 4
Continuous Removal of NH₃

Ammonia is liquefied and drained continuously, pulling the equilibrium forward according to Le Chatelier's principle.

The Contact Process for Sulfuric Acid (H2SO4):

2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)   (ΔH = −198 kJ/mol, Exothermic)

• Catalyst: Vanadium Pentoxide (V2O5) at 450 °C.
• Pressure: 1 to 2 atm (yield is already over 98% at near atmospheric pressure).

Topic 40 MDCAT Problem Bank

Solved MDCAT Numericals (Step-by-Step Breakdown)

Problem 01 • Kc Calculation

In a 1.0 dm³ flask, 0.2 mol of H₂ and 0.2 mol of I₂ are mixed at 440 °C. At equilibrium, 0.3 mol of HI is formed. Calculate the equilibrium constant Kc for the reaction: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).

Given:Volume V = 1.0 dm³; Initial [H₂] = 0.2 M, Initial [I₂] = 0.2 M; Equilibrium [HI] = 0.3 M
Required:Equilibrium constant Kc
Formula:Kc = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂])
Substitution:From stoichiometry, 2x = 0.3 → x = 0.15 mol reacted. Remaining [H₂] = 0.2 − 0.15 = 0.05 M; Remaining [I₂] = 0.2 − 0.15 = 0.05 M
Calculation:Kc = (0.3)² / (0.05 × 0.05) = 0.09 / 0.0025 = 36.0
Final Answer: Kc = 36.0 (Dimensionless, since Δn = 0)
Explanation:Since Δn = 0, units cancel out completely, making Kc dimensionless.
Problem 02 • Kp from Kc

For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), the value of Kc is 0.50 mol⁻²·dm⁶ at 400 K. Calculate the value of Kp at this temperature (R = 0.0821 dm³·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹).

Given:Kc = 0.50; T = 400 K; R = 0.0821 dm³·atm·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹
Required:Kp
Formula:Kp = Kc (RT)Δng ; Δng = 2 − (1 + 3) = −2
Calculation:RT = 0.0821 × 400 = 32.84
Kp = 0.50 × (32.84)⁻² = 0.50 / (1078.46) = 4.64 × 10⁻⁴ atm⁻²
Final Answer: Kp = 4.64 × 10⁻⁴ atm⁻²
Problem 03 • Partial Pressures & Kp

At equilibrium, a mixture contains partial pressures of PCl₅ = 0.5 atm, PCl₃ = 0.2 atm, and Cl₂ = 0.3 atm. Calculate Kp for: PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g).

Given:PPCl5 = 0.5 atm; PPCl3 = 0.2 atm; PCl2 = 0.3 atm
Formula:Kp = (PPCl3 × PCl2) / PPCl5
Calculation:Kp = (0.2 × 0.3) / 0.5 = 0.06 / 0.5 = 0.12 atm
Final Answer: Kp = 0.12 atm
Problem 04 • Units of Kc

Find the SI unit of Kc for the synthesis of ammonia: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).

Formula:Unit of Kc = (mol·dm⁻³)Δn
Calculation:Δn = 2 − (1 + 3) = −2
Unit = (mol·dm⁻³)⁻² = mol⁻²·dm⁶
Final Answer: mol⁻²·dm⁶ (or mol⁻²·L²)
Problem 05 • Reaction Quotient Q vs Kc

For the reaction 2A(g) ⇌ B(g), Kc = 4.0. At a given moment, [A] = 0.5 M and [B] = 2.0 M. Determine if the system is at equilibrium, and if not, predict the direction of shift.

Calculation:Qc = [B] / [A]² = 2.0 / (0.5)² = 2.0 / 0.25 = 8.0
Comparison:Qc (8.0) > Kc (4.0)
Final Answer: The system is NOT at equilibrium. It will shift in the REVERSE DIRECTION (←) to form more A.
Problem 06 • Heterogeneous Equilibrium Constant

Write the Kc and Kp expressions for the thermal decomposition of solid calcium carbonate: CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g). If the partial pressure of CO₂ at 800 °C is 0.4 atm, find Kp.

Rule:Pure solids CaCO₃(s) and CaO(s) have constant concentrations and are omitted from K expressions.
Expression:Kc = [CO₂]   and   Kp = PCO2
Final Answer: Kp = 0.4 atm
Problem 07 • Reversing & Multiplying Reaction

If Kc for 1/2 N₂(g) + 3/2 H₂(g) ⇌ NH₃(g) is 5.0, what is the value of Kc for: 2NH₃(g) ⇌ N₂(g) + 3H₂(g)?

Steps:1. The target reaction is reversed → Invert K: 1 / 5.0 = 0.2
2. The target reaction is multiplied by 2 → Square K: (0.2)² = 0.04
Final Answer: Kc' = 0.04 (or 1/25)
Problem 08 • Equilibrium Moles from Kc

For A + B ⇌ C + D, Kc = 16. If 1 mol each of A and B are mixed in a 1 dm³ vessel, calculate the moles of C at equilibrium.

ICE Table:[A] = 1−x, [B] = 1−x, [C] = x, [D] = x
Calculation:Kc = x² / (1−x)² = 16
Take square root on both sides: x / (1−x) = 4
x = 4 − 4x → 5x = 4 → x = 0.8 mol
Final Answer: Equilibrium moles of C = 0.8 mol
Problem 09 • Gaseous Moles Change (Δn)

Calculate Δng and determine whether Kp is greater than, less than, or equal to Kc for: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g).

Calculation:Δng = 2 − (2 + 1) = −1
Outcome:Since Δng < 0, Kp = Kc(RT)⁻¹ = Kc / (RT). Assuming RT > 1, Kp < Kc.
Final Answer: Δng = −1; Kp < Kc
Problem 10 • Total Pressure & Mole Fractions

An equilibrium mixture contains 0.4 mol N₂O₄ and 0.2 mol NO₂ at a total pressure of 3.0 atm. Calculate Kp for: N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g).

Mole Fractions:Total moles = 0.4 + 0.2 = 0.6 mol
XN2O4 = 0.4 / 0.6 = 2/3 ; XNO2 = 0.2 / 0.6 = 1/3
Partial Pressures:PN2O4 = (2/3) × 3.0 = 2.0 atm ; PNO2 = (1/3) × 3.0 = 1.0 atm
Kp Calculation:Kp = (PNO2)² / PN2O4 = (1.0)² / 2.0 = 0.50 atm
Final Answer: Kp = 0.50 atm
Problem 11 • Volume Expansion Effect

When 1 mol of PCl₅ is heated in a 1 dm³ vessel, 20% dissociates at equilibrium. What is the value of Kc for PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g)?

Dissociation:α = 0.20 → [PCl₃] = 0.20 M, [Cl₂] = 0.20 M, [PCl₅] = 1 − 0.20 = 0.80 M
Calculation:Kc = (0.20 × 0.20) / 0.80 = 0.04 / 0.80 = 0.05 mol·dm⁻³
Final Answer: Kc = 0.05 mol·dm⁻³
Problem 12 • Kp and Kc Equality

Under what specific condition is Kp numerically equal to Kc for ANY chemical reaction?

Formula:Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
Condition:When Δng = 0, (RT)⁰ = 1 regardless of temperature or value of R.
Final Answer: When Δng = 0 (Total gaseous product moles equal total gaseous reactant moles).

Topic 43 Warning Center

MDCAT Exam Traps & Critical Misconceptions

⚠️ Trap 1: Equilibrium Concentrations
Misconception: "At equilibrium, [Reactants] = [Products]."
Reality: Rates are equal (Rf = Rr). Concentrations are constant, but rarely equal.
⚠️ Trap 2: Catalyst on Kc
Misconception: "A catalyst increases the value of Kc and increases product yield."
Reality: Catalyst lowers activation energy equally for both paths. Kc and yield are completely unaffected.
⚠️ Trap 3: Solids in Kc
Misconception: "CaCO₃ is written in Kc expression because it is the reactant."
Reality: Pure solids have constant density and are strictly excluded from Kc and Kp.
⚠️ Trap 4: Pressure when Δn = 0
Misconception: "Increasing pressure always shifts a gaseous equilibrium."
Reality: If Δng = 0 (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI), pressure has zero shifting effect.
⚠️ Trap 5: Temperature & Kc
Misconception: "Kc is a universal constant that never changes."
Reality: Kc is a constant ONLY at fixed temperature. Changing temperature always changes Kc.
⚠️ Trap 6: Inert Gas at Const Volume
Misconception: "Adding He gas at constant volume shifts equilibrium because pressure rises."
Reality: Partial pressures and concentrations of reactants/products remain constant. No shift occurs!

Chemical Equilibrium — 5-Minute High-Yield Flash Revision

1. Core Rules of Equilibrium
  • Rateforward = Ratereverse
  • [Reactants] and [Products] remain constant.
  • Must be in a closed vessel.
  • ΔG = 0 at equilibrium.
2. Kp and Kc Quick Formulas
  • Kp = Kc(RT)Δng
  • Δn = 0 → Kp = Kc (No units)
  • Reversing reaction → K' = 1/K
  • Multiplying equation by n → K' = Kn
3. Reaction Quotient (Q) Rules
  • Q < K → Shifts Forward (→)
  • Q = K → Equilibrium State
  • Q > K → Shifts Reverse (←)
4. Le Chatelier Shift Rules
  • Pressure ↑ → Shifts to FEWER gas moles.
  • Volume ↑ → Shifts to MORE gas moles.
  • Catalyst → No shift; No change in K.
  • Temperature is the ONLY factor that changes K.
5. Haber Process Flash Facts
  • Pressure: 200–250 atm
  • Temperature: 400–450 °C (Compromise)
  • Catalyst: Finely divided Iron (Fe) with promoters
  • Continuous removal of liquefied NH₃ pulls yield.
6. Golden MDCAT Traps
  • Equilibrium does NOT mean [R] = [P].
  • Pure solids are omitted from Kc and Kp.
  • Inert gas at constant V causes NO shift.
  • Pressure has NO effect if Δn = 0.
