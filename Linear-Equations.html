Section 01

What is a Linear Equation?

An equation is a mathematical statement asserting that two expressions are equal, joined by an equality sign (=). A linear equation in one variable is an equation where the highest exponent of the variable is exactly 1 (first degree).

Standard Form: ax + b = 0   (where a, b are constants and a ≠ 0)

Key Components of an Equation

Variable

An unknown quantity represented by a letter (e.g., x, y, t).

Coefficient

The numerical multiplier of a variable. In 7x, 7 is the coefficient.

Constant

A fixed numerical value that does not change (e.g., 5, -12, 3/4).

LHS & RHS

Left-Hand Side and Right-Hand Side expressions separated by =.

Anatomy of an Example: 2x + 5 = 13

  • Variable: x
  • Coefficient of x: 2
  • Constant on LHS: 5
  • LHS Expression: 2x + 5
  • RHS Expression: 13
  • Solution: The numerical value of x that makes LHS equal to RHS (x = 4 because 2(4) + 5 = 13).

8 Foundational Examples

1. x + 9 = 20 → Linear (deg 1)
2. 3y - 7 = 14 → Linear (deg 1)
3. 0.5m = 8 → Linear (deg 1)
4. k / 4 + 2 = 10 → Linear (deg 1)
5. 5x + 3 = 2x - 9 → Linear (deg 1)
6. x² + 4 = 12 → NOT Linear (deg 2)
7. 1/x + 3 = 5 → NOT Linear (exponent -1)
8. √x + 2 = 6 → NOT Linear (fractional power)
Section 02

⚖️ The Golden Rule — Balance of an Equation

⚖️ The Golden Balance Principle

An equation represents a balanced scale. Whatever mathematical operation (Addition, Subtraction, Multiplication, Division) you perform on one side, you MUST perform equally on the other side.

If LHS = RHS, then LHS ± c = RHS ± c, and LHS × c = RHS × c (for c ≠ 0).

Demonstration: x + 5 = 12

1
Identify the operation to reverse: The constant +5 is attached to x. The inverse of addition is subtraction.
2
Subtract 5 from BOTH sides: x + 5 - 5 = 12 - 5
3
Simplify to find isolated variable: x = 7

Section 03

One-Step Equations

One-step equations require exactly one inverse arithmetic operation to isolate the variable.

Addition Type: x + 7 = 15
Rule: Subtract constant
x + 7 - 7 = 15 - 7 ⇒ x = 8

Practice: (1) y+12=30 → y=18, (2) a+9=4 → a=-5, (3) x+2.5=10 → x=7.5

Subtraction Type: x - 4 = 10
Rule: Add constant
x - 4 + 4 = 10 + 4 ⇒ x = 14

Practice: (1) m-9=21 → m=30, (2) k-15=-3 → k=12, (3) p-3.2=4.8 → p=8

Multiplication Type: 5x = 30
Rule: Divide by coefficient
5x / 5 = 30 / 5 ⇒ x = 6

Practice: (1) 7x=91 → x=13, (2) -4y=36 → y=-9, (3) 12k=144 → k=12

Division Type: x / 4 = 6
Rule: Multiply by denominator
(x / 4) × 4 = 6 × 4 ⇒ x = 24

Practice: (1) y/3=11 → y=33, (2) z/(-5)=8 → z=-40, (3) t/6=15 → t=90

Section 04

Two-Step Equations

Two-step equations combine two arithmetic operations. Standard Strategy: Undo addition or subtraction first (move constant), then undo multiplication or division (isolate variable).

Core Example: 2x + 5 = 17
1
Subtract 5 from both sides: 2x = 17 - 5 = 12
2
Divide both sides by 2: x = 12 / 2 = 6

10 Solved Examples (Varied Types)

1. Positive: 3x + 7 = 28 → 3x = 21 → x = 7
2. Subtraction: 4p - 9 = 27 → 4p = 36 → p = 9
3. Negative Coeff: -5y + 12 = -18 → -5y = -30 → y = 6
4. Division Term: x/3 - 4 = 6 → x/3 = 10 → x = 30
5. Fraction Constant: 2x + 1/2 = 9/2 → 2x = 4 → x = 2
6. Decimal: 0.4x + 1.6 = 4.8 → 0.4x = 3.2 → x = 8
7. Negative Result: 7k + 30 = 9 → 7k = -21 → k = -3
8. Reversed Form: 15 = 3 - 2m → 2m = -12 → m = -6
9. Fraction Coeff: (2/3)x - 5 = 7 → (2/3)x = 12 → x = 18
10. Zero RHS: 8x - 24 = 0 → 8x = 24 → x = 3
Section 05

Multi-Step Linear Equations

When equations contain multiple terms with variables and constants on the same side or both sides, follow this strict 5-step protocol:

1
Expand & Clear: Eliminate parentheses using distributive property and clear fractions by multiplying by LCM.
2
Combine Like Terms: Add/subtract variable terms together and constant terms together on each side.
3
Group Variables: Use addition/subtraction to gather all variable terms onto one side.
4
Group Constants: Move all numbers to the opposite side.
5
Isolate & Verify: Multiply or divide to get x alone, then substitute back to verify.
Detailed Multi-Step Walkthrough: 3x + 5 - 2x + 7 = 20
(3x - 2x) + (5 + 7) = 20
1x + 12 = 20
x = 20 - 12
x = 8 ✓
Section 06

Equations with Brackets & Distributive Law

Distributive Property: a(b + c) = ab + ac   |   -(b - c) = -b + c
⚡ FAST Shortcut — Avoid Unnecessary Distribution

If the multiplier outside the bracket evenly divides the RHS, divide both sides immediately instead of expanding. e.g., 3(x + 4) = 21 → x + 4 = 7 → x = 3 (Done in 2 seconds!).

Solved Examples with Negative Distribution

Example: 5(2x - 1) - 3(x + 2) = 12
Step 1: Distribute 5 & -3 → 10x - 5 - 3x - 6 = 12
Step 2: Combine like terms → 7x - 11 = 12
Step 3: Add 11 → 7x = 23 → x = 23/7
Section 07

Equations with Fractions & LCM Clearing

Fractions slow down mental calculation. The golden rule for entrance exams is: Clear all denominators in Step 1 by multiplying the entire equation by the LCM of all denominators.

Example: x/2 + x/3 = 10
LCM of 2 and 3 is 6.
Multiply every term by 6:
6 × (x/2) + 6 × (x/3) = 6 × 10
3x + 2x = 60
5x = 60 ⇒ x = 12 ✓
Algebraic Fraction Numerators: (x + 4)/2 - (x - 1)/3 = 4
Multiply by LCM (6):
3(x + 4) - 2(x - 1) = 24
3x + 12 - 2x + 2 = 24
x + 14 = 24 ⇒ x = 10 ✓
Section 08

Variables on Both Sides

Golden Technique: Always eliminate the smaller variable coefficient first. This keeps the coefficient positive and prevents negative sign errors.

Example: 5x - 3 = 2x + 12
Subtract 2x from both sides: 3x - 3 = 12
Add 3 to both sides: 3x = 15
Divide by 3: x = 5 ✓
Section 09

Equations with Decimals

Eliminate decimal points by multiplying both sides by 10, 100, or 1000 depending on the maximum decimal places.

0.25x + 1.5 = 4.25   → Multiply by 100 →   25x + 150 = 425
25x = 275   →   x = 11 ✓
Section 10

Special Cases: One, None, or Infinite Solutions

Entry tests frequently feature conceptual questions on degenerate equations:

1. Unique Solution (1 Solution)

Occurs when variable coefficients are unequal (a ≠ c in ax + b = cx + d). Example: 2x + 3 = 9 → x = 3.

2. No Solution (Contradiction)

Occurs when coefficients are equal but constants differ (a = c and b ≠ d). Example: 2x + 5 = 2x + 9 → 5 = 9 (False!).

3. Infinitely Many Solutions (Identity)

Both sides are mathematically identical (a = c and b = d). Example: 2(x + 3) = 2x + 6 → 6 = 6 (True for all x ∈ ℝ).

Section 11

Word Problems — 6-Step Translation Framework

Every word problem can be solved reliably using this 6-step entry-test framework: 1. Understand → 2. Define Variable → 3. Form Equation → 4. Solve → 5. Check → 6. Conclude.

Problem 1: If 7 is added to three times a certain number, the result is 40. Find the number.
1. Understand
Unknown number; 3 × number + 7 equals 40.
2. Define Variable
Let the unknown number be n.
3. Form Equation
3n + 7 = 40
4. Solve
3n = 33 ⇒ n = 11
5. Check
3(11) + 7 = 33 + 7 = 40 ✓
6. Final Answer
The number is 11.
Problem 2: One number is 6 more than another. Their sum is 50. Find both numbers.
1. Understand
Two numbers with difference 6 and sum 50.
2. Define Variable
Smaller = x, Larger = x + 6.
3. Form Equation
x + (x + 6) = 50
4. Solve
2x = 44 ⇒ x = 22; Larger = 28.
5. Check
22 + 28 = 50 and 28 - 22 = 6 ✓
6. Final Answer
The numbers are 22 and 28.
Problem 3: A mother is 28 years older than her daughter. In 6 years, the mother will be 3 times as old as the daughter. Find their current ages.
1. Understand
Current age difference 28; ratio after 6 years is 3:1.
2. Define Variable
Daughter = d, Mother = d + 28.
3. Form Equation
(d + 28 + 6) = 3(d + 6) → d + 34 = 3d + 18
4. Solve
2d = 16 ⇒ d = 8, Mother = 36.
5. Check
In 6 yrs: Daughter = 14, Mother = 42. 42 = 3(14) ✓
6. Final Answer
Daughter is 8 years old, Mother is 36.
Problem 4: A student bought 20 tickets for Rs. 1400. Adult tickets cost Rs. 100 each and student tickets cost Rs. 50 each. How many of each were bought?
1. Understand
Total tickets = 20, total value = Rs. 1400.
2. Define Variable
Adult tickets = a, Student tickets = 20 - a.
3. Form Equation
100a + 50(20 - a) = 1400
4. Solve
50a + 1000 = 1400 ⇒ 50a = 400 ⇒ a = 8.
5. Check
8 × 100 + 12 × 50 = 800 + 600 = 1400 ✓
6. Final Answer
8 adult tickets and 12 student tickets.
Problem 5: The sum of three consecutive odd integers is 87. Find all three integers.
1. Understand
Consecutive odd integers increase by 2.
2. Define Variable
Let integers be n, n+2, n+4.
3. Form Equation
n + (n + 2) + (n + 4) = 87 ⇒ 3n + 6 = 87
4. Solve
3n = 81 ⇒ n = 27. Integers: 27, 29, 31.
5. Check
27 + 29 + 31 = 87 ✓
6. Final Answer
The integers are 27, 29, and 31.
Problem 6: A car travels between two cities at 60 km/h and returns at 40 km/h. Total trip time is 5 hours. Find the distance between cities.
1. Understand
Time = Distance / Speed; Total time = 5 hours.
2. Define Variable
One-way distance = d km.
3. Form Equation
d/60 + d/40 = 5
4. Solve
Multiply by 120: 2d + 3d = 600 ⇒ 5d = 600 ⇒ d = 120 km.
5. Check
120/60 + 120/40 = 2 + 3 = 5 hours ✓
6. Final Answer
The distance between the cities is 120 km.
Problem 7: Pipe A fills a reservoir in 6 hours and Pipe B fills it in 4 hours. How long will both take together?
1. Understand
Work rate per hour = 1/6 and 1/4.
2. Define Variable
Combined time = t hours.
3. Form Equation
1/6 + 1/4 = 1/t ⇒ 5/12 = 1/t
4. Solve
t = 12/5 = 2.4 hours (2 hrs 24 mins).
5. Check
2.4 × (5/12) = 1 (complete job) ✓
6. Final Answer
Both pipes together take 2.4 hours.
Section 12

Linear Inequalities & Symbols

An inequality compares two algebraic expressions showing which is larger, smaller, or potentially equal.

> (Strictly Greater Than)

e.g., x > 5 → Excludes 5, values strictly to the right.

< (Strictly Less Than)

e.g., x < 10 → Excludes 10, values strictly to the left.

≥ (Greater Than or Equal To)

e.g., x ≥ 3 → Includes 3 (closed point).

≤ (Less Than or Equal To)

e.g., x ≤ 7 → Includes 7 (closed point).

Section 13

⚠️ The Golden Negative Inequality Rule

⚠️ CRITICAL ENTRY-TEST WARNING

Whenever you MULTIPLY or DIVIDE both sides of an inequality by a NEGATIVE number, you MUST REVERSE (flip) the inequality sign!

If -2x > 6  ⇒  Divide by -2  ⇒  x < -3   (Sign flips from > to <!)

Why Does the Sign Flip?

Consider simple true arithmetic: 2 < 5.
If we multiply both sides by -1, we get -2 and -5.
On the number line, -2 is to the right of -5, so -2 > -5. The relative order of numbers inverts across zero!

8 Solved Negative Inequality Examples

1. -3x ≥ 12 → x ≤ -4
2. -x < 9 → x > -9
3. 5 - 2x > 15 → -2x > 10 → x < -5
4. x / (-4) ≤ 3 → x ≥ -12
5. -7y - 4 < 17 → -7y < 21 → y > -3
6. 4 - 5x ≥ -16 → -5x ≥ -20 → x ≤ 4
7. (2 - x)/3 > 4 → 2 - x > 12 → -x > 10 → x < -10
8. -0.2x ≤ 6 → x ≥ -30
Section 14

Compound Inequalities

A compound inequality consists of two inequalities combined (e.g., a < x < b). Perform all operations simultaneously across all three parts (Left, Middle, Right).

Example: -4 < 2x + 2 ≤ 10
Subtract 2 from ALL parts:
-4 - 2 < 2x ≤ 10 - 2
-6 < 2x ≤ 8
Divide all parts by 2:
-3 < x ≤ 4 ✓

🚀 FAST & Scholarship Speed Techniques

Shortcut 1 • Direct Subtraction for Symmetric Systems

If coefficients interchange (e.g. 2024x + 2025y = A and 2025x + 2024y = B), subtract equations directly to find x - y in 3 seconds!

Shortcut 2 • Options Back-Substitution

When an equation has complex fractions, plug option B or C into the LHS first rather than solving by cross-multiplication.

Shortcut 3 • Parity & Unit Digit Filter

In equations like 7x + 15 = 92, 7x must end in 7 → x must end in 1 (e.g., 11). Eliminates 3 MCQ options instantly!

Shortcut 4 • Early Denominator Elimination

Always multiply by the lowest common multiple first — never work with fractional common denominators on individual terms.

Shortcut 5 • Sign & Direction Check

In inequalities with a negative x term, filter options immediately: if the original was >, the answer must have <.

Shortcut 6 • Constant Shift for Symmetric Equations

For equations like (x-a)/b + (x-b)/a = 2, the solution is algebraically symmetric: x = a + b.

Section 17

Common Mistakes (Wrong vs. Correct)

❌ 1. Negative Bracket Distribution
Wrong:
-(x - 4) = -x - 4
Correct:
-(x - 4) = -x + 4
Why: Negative sign applies to both terms inside.
❌ 2. Forgetting to Flip Inequality Sign
Wrong:
-3x > 9 → x > -3
Correct:
-3x > 9 → x < -3
Why: Dividing by negative reverses inequality direction.
❌ 3. Partial LCM Multiplication
Wrong:
x/3 + 2 = 5 → x + 2 = 15
Correct:
x/3 + 2 = 5 → x + 6 = 15
Why: You must multiply EVERY term on both sides by 3.
❌ 4. Combining Unlike Terms
Wrong:
3x + 5 = 8x
Correct:
3x + 5 cannot be combined!
Why: Variable terms and constant terms are not like terms.
Section 18

Formula & Rule Sheet

Linear Equation Form

  • ax + b = 0 (Standard Form, a ≠ 0)
  • Highest variable degree = 1
  • Unique Solution: x = -b / a

Golden Inequality Rules

  • a > b ⇒ a ± c > b ± c
  • a > b & c > 0 ⇒ ac > bc
  • a > b & c < 0 ⇒ ac < bc (FLIP!)

Rate & Motion Formulas

  • Distance = Speed × Time (d = s × t)
  • Opposite directions speed = s_1 + s_2
  • Work Rate = 1/t_1 + 1/t_2 = 1/T_{total}

Special Solution Conditions

  • ax + b = cx + d:
  • a ≠ c → Exactly 1 Solution
  • a = c, b ≠ d → No Solution
  • a = c, b = d → Infinitely Many
