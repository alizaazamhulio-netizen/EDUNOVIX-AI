Topic 01

Introduction to Atom & Subatomic Particles

What is an Atom?

An atom is the smallest fundamental unit of an element that retains all the characteristic chemical properties and identity of that element. Matter is composed of atoms, which chemically combine to form molecules and ionic compounds.

An individual atom is composed of two primary regions:

  • The Nucleus: A central, ultra-dense, positively charged core ($r \approx 10^{-15}\text{ m}$) containing protons and neutrons (collectively termed nucleons).
  • The Extra-Nuclear Region: A vast, mostly empty 3D space ($r \approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$) where negatively charged electrons reside in quantized energy levels and probability orbitals.

⭐ MDCAT HIGH-YIELD ESSENTIAL

Mass Concentration Rule: Almost all the mass ($> 99.98\%$) of an atom is concentrated in its tiny central nucleus. The mass of an electron is approximately $\frac{1}{1836}\text{th}$ (or $0.054\%$) the mass of a proton, and is usually neglected in atomic mass calculations.

Topics 02 & 03

Atomic Number ($Z$) and Mass Number ($A$)

🔢

Atomic Number ($Z$)

The Atomic Number ($Z$) equals the total number of protons present in the nucleus of an atom.

$$Z = \text{Number of Protons } (p)$$

For any neutral atom:

$$\text{Number of Protons } (p) = \text{Number of Electrons } (e^-)$$
⭐ The atomic number defines the fundamental chemical identity of an element.
⚖️

Mass Number ($A$)

The Mass Number ($A$) equals the total number of protons plus neutrons (nucleons) in the nucleus.

$$A = p + n = Z + n$$

To find the number of neutrons in an atom:

$$n = A - Z$$
⭐ Mass number is always an integer; atomic weight on periodic table is a weighted average.

Topic 04

Isotopes & Relative Atomic Mass

Definition of Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element having the same atomic number ($Z$) (same number of protons and electrons in neutral atom) but different mass numbers ($A$) due to different numbers of neutrons.

Carbon-12 ($^{12}_6\text{C}$)

Protons ($Z$): 6 Neutrons ($n$): 6 Electrons: 6 Abundance: 98.93% Standard for atomic mass scale ($1\text{ u} = \frac{1}{12}\text{ mass of } ^{12}\text{C}$)

Carbon-13 ($^{13}_6\text{C}$)

Protons ($Z$): 6 Neutrons ($n$): 7 Electrons: 6 Abundance: 1.07% Used in NMR spectroscopy

Carbon-14 ($^{14}_6\text{C}$)

Protons ($Z$): 6 Neutrons ($n$): 8 Electrons: 6 Abundance: Trace ($10^{-10}\%$) Radioactive ($\beta^-$ emitter, $t_{1/2} = 5730\text{ yrs}$, radiocarbon dating)
⭐ MDCAT HIGH-YIELD POINT: Chemical vs Physical Properties

Chemical Properties: Generally identical because isotopes have the exact same electronic configuration and valence electrons.
Physical & Nuclear Properties: Differ (mass, density, boiling point, nuclear stability, radioactive decay, rate of diffusion via Graham's Law).

Topic 05

Ions: Cations and Anions

➕

Cation (Positive Ion)

Formed when a neutral atom loses one or more valence electrons.

$$\text{Na} \xrightarrow{-1e^-} \text{Na}^+ \quad (11p, 10e^-)$$
  • $\text{Protons} > \text{Electrons}$
  • Net positive charge ($+1, +2, +3$)
  • Ionic Radius: Cation radius is smaller than its parent neutral atom due to increased effective nuclear charge ($Z_{\text{eff}}$) and loss of outermost valence shell.
➖

Anion (Negative Ion)

Formed when a neutral atom gains one or more electrons.

$$\text{Cl} \xrightarrow{+1e^-} \text{Cl}^- \quad (17p, 18e^-)$$
  • $\text{Electrons} > \text{Protons}$
  • Net negative charge ($-1, -2, -3$)
  • Ionic Radius: Anion radius is larger than its parent neutral atom due to electron-electron repulsion in the same shell.
⭐ CRUCIAL MDCAT RULE

The number of protons never changes during ordinary chemical ion formation! Only the electron count increases or decreases. Changing protons requires nuclear reactions, which transforms the atom into a totally different element.

Topic 06

Evolution of Atomic Models

The understanding of atomic structure developed through crucial experimental breakthroughs, transitioning from solid indivisible spheres to modern 3D quantum wave mechanics:

1808

John Dalton · Solid Sphere Model

Billiard Ball Model

Core Theory: Matter consists of indivisible, indestructible hard spheres. Atoms of the same element are identical in mass and properties. Chemical reactions represent combinations and rearrangements in simple whole-number ratios.

✅ Contribution: Explained Law of Definite & Multiple Proportions.
❌ Limitation: Failed because atoms ARE divisible (protons, neutrons, electrons) and isotopes possess different masses.
1897 / 1904

J. J. Thomson · Plum Pudding Model

Watermelon Model

Core Theory: Discovered the electron using cathode ray tube experiments ($e/m = 1.7588 \times 10^{11}\text{ C/kg}$). Proposed that electrons are embedded uniformly like raisins/seeds in a positively charged spherical matrix.

✅ Contribution: First model recognizing internal subatomic charges and electrical neutrality.
❌ Limitation: No central nucleus; could not explain large-angle alpha particle scattering.
1911

Ernest Rutherford · Nuclear Planetary Model

Gold Foil Discovery

Core Theory: Bombarded ultra-thin gold foil with $\alpha$-particles ($^4_2\text{He}^{2+}$). Proved that the atom has a tiny, ultra-dense, positively charged central nucleus containing almost all atomic mass, with electrons revolving around it.

✅ Contribution: Discovered the atomic nucleus and demonstrated that the atom is mostly empty space.
❌ Limitation: Classical Maxwell electrodynamics predicted accelerating electrons should continuously radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus within $10^{-8}\text{ s}$; could not explain discontinuous line spectra.
1913

Niels Bohr · Quantized Energy Level Model

Stationary Orbits

Core Theory: Applied Planck’s quantum theory. Electrons revolve only in non-radiating stationary orbits where angular momentum is quantized: $mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$. Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when electrons transition between discrete energy levels ($\Delta E = h\nu$).

✅ Contribution: Explained hydrogen atomic stability and calculated exact Balmer/Lyman emission line wavelengths.
❌ Limitation: Applicable only to 1-electron systems ($\text{H}, \text{He}^+, \text{Li}^{2+}$); failed to explain multi-electron atoms, Zeeman effect (magnetic splitting), Stark effect (electric splitting), or wave-particle duality.
1926+

Modern Quantum Mechanical Model

Schrödinger & Heisenberg

Core Theory: Integrates de Broglie matter waves ($\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$), Heisenberg’s Uncertainty Principle ($\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$), and Schrödinger’s 3D Wave Equation ($\hat{H}\psi = E\psi$). Electrons exist as 3D probability density clouds (orbitals), not planar circular tracks.

✅ Contribution: Accurately explains complex multi-electron atom structures, chemical bonding, spdf configurations, and spectroscopy.

📋 What did Rutherford Conclude from the Observations?

1
Atom is mostly empty space:

Because $\approx 99\%$ of $\alpha$-particles passed straight through without experiencing any deflection.

2
Nucleus has concentrated positive charge:

Alpha particles ($+2$ charge) that grazed closely were strongly repelled and deflected at sharp angles by Coulombic electrostatic repulsion.

3
Mass is concentrated in a tiny core:

A very small fraction ($1$ in $8,000$ to $20,000$) struck head-on and rebounded backward ($> 90^\circ$ to $180^\circ$).

4
Nuclear Dimensions:

Nuclear radius is $\approx 10^{-15}\text{ m}$ (1 Fermi), whereas atomic radius is $\approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ (1 Å). The atom's volume is $\approx 10^{15}$ times larger than the nucleus!

Topic 08

Bohr's Hydrogen Model & Quantum Transitions

Bohr's Core Postulates for Hydrogen ($Z=1$)

  1. Electrons revolve around the nucleus only in certain stable, non-radiating circular orbits called stationary orbits.
  2. Quantization of Angular Momentum: Only orbits where electron angular momentum ($L$) is an integral multiple of $\frac{h}{2\pi}$ are permitted: $$mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi} \quad (n = 1, 2, 3, \dots)$$
  3. Energy Transitions: An electron does not radiate while in a stationary orbit. Radiation is absorbed or emitted only when transitioning between levels: $$\Delta E = E_{\text{final}} - E_{\text{initial}} = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$
  4. Energy of $n^{\text{th}}$ orbit in Hydrogen: $$E_n = -\frac{13.6}{n^2}\text{ eV} = -\frac{2.18 \times 10^{-18}}{n^2}\text{ J}$$

Topic 09

Electromagnetic Radiation & Photon Energy

Electromagnetic radiation exhibits dual wave-particle character and travels through vacuum at the speed of light ($c = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$).

Wave Relationship

$$c = \lambda \nu$$

$\lambda$ = Wavelength ($\text{m}$ or $\text{nm}$)
$\nu$ = Frequency ($\text{s}^{-1}$ or $\text{Hz}$)
$c = 3.0 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$

Planck's Quantum Equation

$$E = h\nu = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$$

$E$ = Energy of one photon
$h = 6.626 \times 10^{-34}\text{ J}\cdot\text{s}$
$hc \approx 1240\text{ eV}\cdot\text{nm}$

Wave Number

$$\bar{\nu} = \frac{1}{\lambda} = \frac{\nu}{c}$$

$\bar{\nu}$ = Number of waves per unit length ($\text{m}^{-1}$ or $\text{cm}^{-1}$)
Directly proportional to energy ($E = hc\bar{\nu}$)

⭐ MDCAT HIGH-YIELD PROPORTIONALITIES

$$\text{Wavelength } (\lambda) \uparrow \iff \text{Frequency } (\nu) \downarrow \iff \text{Energy } (E) \downarrow \iff \text{Wave Number } (\bar{\nu}) \downarrow$$ Mnemonic for EM Spectrum (Increasing Energy / Decreasing Wavelength):
Radio < Microwave < Infrared < Visible < Ultraviolet < X-ray < Gamma ray ("Real Men In Violet Uniforms X-ray Galaxies")

Topic 11

Modern Atomic Model & Quantum Orbitals

Orbit vs Orbital — The Essential Distinction

Orbit (Bohr): A fixed 2D planar circular trajectory around the nucleus. Violates Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle.
Orbital (Schrödinger): A 3-dimensional region of space around the nucleus where the probability ($\psi^2$) of finding an electron is maximum ($\ge 90\%$).

Subshell Azimuthal Quantum No. ($l$) Orbital Shape Number of Orbitals ($2l+1$) Maximum Electron Capacity ($2(2l+1)$)
s $l = 0$ Spherical & symmetrical (non-directional) 1 ($s$) 2 electrons
p $l = 1$ Dumb-bell shaped ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) with 1 nodal plane 3 ($p_x, p_y, p_z$) 6 electrons
d $l = 2$ Double dumb-bell / Cloverleaf (except $d_{z^2}$ donut) 5 ($d_{xy}, d_{yz}, d_{xz}, d_{x^2-y^2}, d_{z^2}$) 10 electrons
f $l = 3$ Complex multi-lobed shapes 7 degenerate orbitals 14 electrons
⭐ MDCAT SHELL CAPACITY FORMULA

Maximum number of electrons in any principal energy level (shell $n$) = $2n^2$.
Maximum number of orbitals in any shell $n$ = $n^2$.
Example: For $n = 3$ (M-shell), number of orbitals = $3^2 = 9$ ($1\text{ s} + 3\text{ p} + 5\text{ d}$); maximum electrons = $2(3^2) = 18\text{ electrons}$.

Topic 12

Rules of Electron Configuration & spdf Builder

1

Aufbau Principle

Electrons progressively occupy the lowest available energy orbital before occupying higher energy levels.

$(n+l)$ Rule: Subshell with lower $(n+l)$ fills first (e.g. $4s \ [4+0=4]$ fills before $3d \ [3+2=5]$). If equal, lower $n$ fills first ($3d \ [3+2=5]$ fills before $4p \ [4+1=5]$).
$1s \to 2s \to 2p \to 3s \to 3p \to 4s \to 3d \to 4p \to 5s \to 4d \dots$
2

Pauli Exclusion Principle

No two electrons in the same atom can have the exact same set of all four quantum numbers ($n, l, m_l, m_s$).

Consequence: An individual orbital can hold at most two electrons, and they must have opposite spins ($\uparrow\downarrow$).

3

Hund's Rule of Multiplicity

When filling degenerate orbitals (orbitals of equal energy, such as $2p_x, 2p_y, 2p_z$), electrons occupy them singly with parallel spins before pairing begins.

Why? Minimizes electron-electron Coulombic repulsion and maximizes quantum exchange energy.

Chromium ($Z=24$): Expected $[Ar] 4s^2 3d^4 \implies$ Actual $[Ar] 4s^1 3d^5$ (Extra stability of exactly half-filled $d^5$ subshell).
